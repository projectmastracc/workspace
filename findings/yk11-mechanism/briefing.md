## 3. Executive Summary

Peer-reviewed literature characterizes YK11 as a **steroidal partial androgen-receptor (AR) agonist** that **does not induce N/C interaction**, can **block DHT-driven N/C interaction**, and shows **gene-selective** AR activity in cell models (**Probable**, in vitro; largely single-lab Kanno/Toho package). In **C2C12 myoblasts**, it drives myogenic markers partly via **AR-dependent follistatin (Fst) induction**—an **indirect** route that may secondarily antagonize myostatin/activin signaling, **not** direct MSTN ligand or receptor blockade (**Probable** in that model; **Speculative** in vivo; **Unknown** in humans).

**AR “selectivity”:** Gene selectivity is **Probable** in vitro; classic **tissue** selectivity (muscle/bone ≫ prostate) is **Speculative/Unknown**—no Hershberger-class package in the acquisition set.

**Humans:** **No** controlled efficacy, PK/PD, or safety trials. Community oral doses (e.g., 5–15 mg/day) are **Unknown** → **no dose recommendation**.

**DR bottom line:** Describe YK11 as a **gene-selective AR partial agonist with AR-dependent Fst induction in C2C12**. **Recommend against** “proven myostatin inhibitor,” “direct MSTN blocker,” and “safer because gene-selective” marketing. Prefer non-use; if exposure occurs, apply **class-level SARM/AAS harm reduction** only.

## 5. What Research Truly Says

1. **Partial AR agonism + nuclear translocation** — ARE-luciferase partial agonism (~10–20% of DHT max in discovery figures); nuclear AR translocation. (**Probable**, in vitro; Kanno 2011.)

2. **No N/C interaction; can prevent DHT-mediated N/C interaction** — Mammalian two-hybrid: YK11 null like hydroxyflutamide; co-treatment reduces DHT N/C signal. Mechanistic basis proposed for partial/gene-selective agonism. (**Probable** in that assay system; **Speculative** as universal tissue/cofactor truth; limited independent multi-lab replication.)

3. **Gene-selective AR agonist** — Differential endogenous gene induction vs DHT in MDA-MB-453 (e.g., FKBP51/FGF18 ≈ DHT; SARG DHT-only; HSD11B2 weaker). (**Probable** in vitro; **does not equal** tissue selectivity.)

4. **Myogenic differentiation in C2C12** — MyHC, MyoD, Myf5, myogenin; AR-dependent (FLU, AR siRNA). (**Probable** in vitro mouse myoblasts; **Speculative** for human hypertrophy magnitude.)

5. **Fst induction is AR-dependent and required for Myf5 arm** — *Fst* mRNA ↑ with YK11 not DHT under same conditions; anti-Fst Ab abolishes YK11-driven *Myf5*. (**Probable** in C2C12.) **Inference:** Fst may sequester myostatin/activins → functional pathway dampening — **not** a direct YK11–MSTN binding demonstration. (**Speculative** for systemic/human pathway dominance.)

6. **“Myostatin inhibitor” as primary identity** — Marketing and some secondary titles (Lee 2021) **overstate** mechanism. Correct class: **indirect Fst-mediated pathway modulation**. Direct MSTN antagonist / receptor binder: **unsupported** (**Unknown**/effectively refuted as stated).

7. **Tissue-selective clinical SARM profile** — Not demonstrated by Hershberger A/A ratios or human PSA/muscle packages in this set. (**Speculative/Unknown**.)

8. **Bone (preclinical)** — MC3T3-E1 osteogenic markers AR-dependent (Yatsu 2018); BMSC/rat defect extension (Wang 2025 abstract). (**Speculative–Probable** preclinical; **Unknown** clinical bone benefit.)

9. **Animal disease/safety extensions** — Mouse sepsis framed as myostatin-pathway modulation (Lee 2021; **Speculative** for sepsis benefit; not gym anabolism). Rodent organ/CNS signals (Dahleh line; **Speculative–Probable** that YK11 is not organ-inert at experimental exposures).

10. **Human performance/safety** — Zero RCTs. (**Unknown** efficacy and compound-specific event rates.) Regulatory: unauthorized SARM products; WADA ban. (**Established** regulatory status.)

## 6. Mechanism & Pharmacology

### 6.1 AR — partial agonism and gene selectivity

**Observed (in vitro, Kanno 2011):**

- Partial AR agonist (ARE-luciferase); nuclear translocation of AR.
- **Does not induce N/C interaction** (NTD–LBD physical interaction required for full AR transactivation in their two-hybrid system).
- **Prevents DHT-mediated N/C interaction**.
- **Gene-selective** relative activity vs full agonist by gene context (MDA-MB-453).

**Inference:** Missing N/C interaction is the proposed molecular basis for partial/gene-selective agonism and the authors’ SARM *proposal*. This is **cell-reporter and limited gene-expression evidence**, not in vivo prostate-vs-muscle selectivity.

### 6.2 Myostatin / follistatin — **indirect**, AR-dependent

**Observed (C2C12, Kanno 2013; full text reviewed):**

- YK11 (typically 100–500 nM) and DHT induce myogenic markers.
- **Fst mRNA induced by YK11 but not DHT** under those conditions.
- FLU and AR siRNA blunt *Fst* → **AR-dependent**.
- Neutralizing anti-Fst antibody abolishes YK11-driven **Myf5** mRNA ↑.
- Funding: MEXT Grant-in-Aid; Open Research Center (academic).

**What this is *not*:**

- Not direct binding/antagonism at myostatin (GDF8).
- Not ActRIIB decoy-receptor pharmacology.
- Not demonstrated clinical myostatin blockade in humans.

**Correct framing:** Steroidal **gene-selective AR partial agonist that can upregulate follistatin in vitro**, thereby **functionally antagonizing myostatin/TGF-β family signaling in that cell model**. Marketing “direct myostatin inhibitor” is **false as stated**.

### 6.3 Osteoblast / non-genomic signaling

Yatsu/Kanno 2018: MC3T3-E1 proliferation/mineralization, OPG/OCN; AR antagonist attenuates; p-Akt suggests non-genomic AR–Akt contribution. **In vitro only.**

## 11. Competing Interpretations

### A. “YK11 is a true myostatin inhibitor”

**Steelman pro:** Follistatin is a validated endogenous antagonist of myostatin and activins. If YK11 robustly raises Fst in muscle via AR, functional myostatin pathway tone should fall. Kanno 2013 shows Fst-dependence of *Myf5*. Lee 2021 and Park 2022 use pathway-level “myostatin inhibitor” language. For PED users, functional pathway effect may matter more than ligand class.

**Steelman con:** Clinical myostatin inhibitors (mAbs, ActRII traps) act *directly* on ligand/receptor with measured human pathway suppression. YK11’s strong primary evidence is *Fst* mRNA in a mouse cell line—not free myostatin, ActRII occupancy, or human biopsy p-Smad. DHT still induced MyHC without Fst in the same paper. Label misclassifies MoA and invites unsafe stacking with true MSTN agents.

**Adjudication:** Prefer: **AR partial agonist that can induce follistatin and thereby may secondarily antagonize myostatin/activin signaling in vitro**. “Myostatin inhibitor” as primary identity is **marketing overreach** (**Speculative** at best in humans). **Recommend against** the bunk framing.

### B. “Gene-selective SARM = safe tissue-selective anabolic”

**Steelman pro:** Missing N/C + differential cofactor recruitment is a legitimate molecular basis for gene selectivity; nonsteroidal SARM literature supports tissue cofactor hypotheses; myoblast/osteoblast data lean anabolic.

**Steelman con:** Gene selectivity in MDA-MB-453 ≠ levator ani ≫ prostate in vivo. Steroidal SARMs can still suppress HPG, affect lipids, and hit organs. N/C blockade may **interfere with full AR agonists** when stacked (Park 2022). Zero human safety/efficacy trials.

**Adjudication:** Gene selectivity **Probable** in vitro; tissue-selective clinical SARM profile **Unknown/Speculative**. Do not equate the two. **Recommend against** “safer because gene-selective.”

### C. “Stronger myogenic than DHT in vitro ⇒ superior human anabolic”

**Steelman pro:** Higher selected MRFs and unique Fst arm could mean greater differentiation drive.

**Steelman con:** C2C12 ≠ adult human hypertrophy; 100–500 nM and culture conditions do not establish oral free-drug exposure; “more potent” is endpoint- and time-dependent.

**Adjudication:** Selected in vitro MRF superiority **Probable** under Kanno 2013 conditions; human superiority **Unknown**.

## 17. Compound Context Summary

YK-11 (myostine) is a **steroidal, gene-selective AR partial agonist** that, in **mouse C2C12 cells**, drives myogenic markers partly via **AR-dependent follistatin induction**—an **indirect** myostatin-pathway effect, **not** direct MSTN blockade (Kanno 2011/2013). Osteoblast and mouse sepsis findings remain **preclinical**. **No human RCTs** establish dose, efficacy, or safety. Marketing claims of “direct/proven myostatin inhibitor,” “gene-selective so side-effect free,” or gym-protocol efficacy are **bro-science or oversell**. WADA bans it; Health Canada flags unauthorized SARM products (including myostine) for serious class risks. **Practical bottom line:** No evidence-based human dose can be recommended (**Unknown**). Prefer not to use; if research-chemical exposure still occurs, apply **SARM/AAS-class harm reduction** and **abandon the clinical-myostatin-blocker narrative**.
