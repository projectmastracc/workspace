# Example: Guidelines vs Literature (calibration)

## Executive Card

| Field | Content |
|-------|---------|
| **Subject** | Guideline “first-line” ranking vs meta-analytic effect sizes (hypothetical MDD case) |
| **Overall certainty** | **Probable** benefit for SSRIs in some patients; first-line ranking not **Established** over all alternatives |
| **Verdict** | Report partial alignment; do not defer to authority when literature is weaker than the guideline frame. |
| **Key practical note** | Psychotherapy/exercise remain evidence-aligned options; SSRI reasonable when indicated — not by authority alone. |
| **Top caveats** | Small effect sizes, placebo response, industry trial predominance. |

## Guidelines vs Literature

| Guideline | Recommends | Literature | Alignment | Severity |
|-----------|------------|------------|-----------|----------|
| Hypothetical APA Practice Guideline (2019) | SSRI class as first-line for moderate MDD | Meta-analyses show small effect vs placebo (Hedges g ≈ 0.3); high placebo response; industry trial predominance | **Partial** | **Material** |

**Verdict:** Guideline direction is **Probable** for some benefit in moderate MDD, but "first-line" framing **materially exceeds** certainty when effect sizes are small and publication bias is likely. Guideline panel predates several null replications (**Speculative** whether ranking vs psychotherapy is evidence-based).

**Evidence-graded recommendation:** If choosing an intervention for moderate MDD, literature supports psychotherapy and exercise as comparably or more robust first-line options (**Probable**). SSRI initiation is **Probable** reasonable when psychotherapy is unavailable or severe symptoms warrant pharmacotherapy — but not as blanket first-line per guideline authority alone. Recommend against treating APA 2019 ranking as evidence-**Established** superiority over non-pharmacological options.
