# Trenbolone Acetate (Tren A) vs Trenbolone Enanthate (Tren E)

**Secondary excerpt:** both vs Trenbolone Hexahydrobenzylcarbonate (Tren Hex / Parabolan)

**Class:** 19-nor AAS · nandrolone-group triene · AR agonist + PR agonist · non-aromatizing · not 5α-activated  
**Parent hormone:** 17β-hydroxyestra-4,9,11-trien-3-one (trenbolone, RU-2341)  
**Document type:** Ester comparison (same parent, different C17β esters)

**Evidence frame (source critique):** Human IM PK by ester is essentially absent — cite half-lives as **ranges / secondary citations**, not precise volunteer PK. Matched free-hormone AUC → similar systemic effect is **Probable**. Claims that acetate is *intrinsically* harsher (beyond peak–trough and pin logistics) are **Speculative**. UGL vial identity for “E” and “Hex” is **Unknown** per vial.

## 2. Chemistry, classification, PK

### 2.1 Shared parent

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **IUPAC / structure** | (8S,13S,14S,17S)-17-hydroxy-13-methyl-2,6,7,8,14,15,16,17-octahydro-1H-cyclopenta[a]phenanthren-3-one · estra-4,9,11-trien-17β-ol-3-one |
| **MW parent** | ~270.37 g·mol⁻¹ |
| **Scaffold** | 19-nortestosterone + Δ9 and Δ11 (triene). Increases AR affinity and metabolic stability vs plain nandrolone; blocks classical aromatization path and classical 5α-reductase *activation* to a more potent androgen. |
| **Metabolites of note** | 17α-trenbolone (epitrenbolone), trendione — both much weaker AR ligands (<5% of 17β-TbOH in Bauer rhAR assay). **Established** (binding). Urinary conjugates (glucuronides/sulfates). **Probable.** |
| **Aromatization** | Not a substrate for meaningful conversion to estradiol. **Established** structural + clinical consensus. Estrogenic *phenotypes* (gyno) when they occur track PR / estrogen-from-other-stack / PRL hypotheses, not tren→E2. |
| **5α-reductase** | Does not convert to a DHT-like super-androgen in prostate the way testosterone does; metabolites are *less* potent. Basis for rodent “tissue selectivity” (muscle/bone vs prostate). **Probable.** |
| **SHBG** | Low affinity vs DHT. **Established** (Bauer). High free fraction once released. |
| **Routes** | IM (or subQ oil depot) only for esters. Not 17α-alkylated → not designed as oral AAS (hepatic first-pass destroys parent). Livestock: subcutaneous acetate implants (Finaplix, Revalor combos with estradiol). |
| **Legal / history** | Never approved as free trenbolone for human medicine. Acetate = veterinary growth promoter. Hex = former French human pharmaceutical (Parabolan, Negma; ~1980–1997). Enanthate = black-market / UGL staple (“Trenabol”). US Schedule III (and analogs). |

### 2.2 Ester table (A vs E vs Hex)

Esterification at C17β slows release from oil depot; plasma esterases liberate free 17β-trenbolone. **Receptor pharmacology is identical once free.** Differences = absorption half-life, peak/trough, pin schedule, oil volume, active-hormone fraction by weight.

| Parameter | Acetate (A) | Enanthate (E) | Hex / HHBC (Parabolan) |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|-------------------------|
| **Ester chain** | Acetate (C2) | Enanthate (C7) | Hexahydrobenzylcarbonate / cyclohexylmethylcarbonate |
| **Approx MW** | ~312 | ~382 | ~411 |
| **Active fraction (base/ester)** | ~0.87 (87%) | ~0.70–0.71 (71%) | ~0.66 (66%); classic amp: **76 mg ester → 50 mg base** |
| **Cited IM t½ (community + structural lit)** | **~1–3 days** (Borodi 1–2 d; Ruiz/Strain ~3 d) | **~7–11 days** (Borodi ~11 d; some market sources ~5 d — treat as range) | **~5–10 days**, commonly **~8 d** (Borodi); clinical dosing was q10 d |
| **Time to “feel” / practical kick-in** | Days 1–3 | Days 5–14 to steady-state feel | Intermediate–long |
| **Clearance after last pin** | Fast (side taper in days–~1–2 wk) | Slow (sides/HPTA drag weeks) | Slow–intermediate |
| **Typical community pin freq** | **ED or EOD** | **2–3×/week** | 2×/wk or q5–10 d |
| **Peak–trough** | High if EOD/ED not stable; sharp peaks | Flatter if split well | Fairly flat if q5–7 d |
| **Oil volume / concentration** | Often 100 mg/mL; daily volume small but frequent | Often 100–200 mg/mL; fewer, larger boluses | Historical 76 mg/amp; UGL variable |
| **PIP / solvent risk** | Higher pin count → more site trauma; short esters + solvents classically linked to more **cough-on-pin** reports | Fewer pins; still oil-POME risk | Similar long-ester logistics |
| **Control / titration** | Best | Poor mid-cycle | Moderate |
| **“Stuck on sides” risk** | Lower | Higher | Higher |

**Dose conversion (active hormone — approximate):**

- 100 mg Tren A ≈ **87 mg** free trenbolone  
- 100 mg Tren E ≈ **70–71 mg** free trenbolone  
- 76 mg Tren Hex ≈ **50 mg** free trenbolone  
- Rough equipotent ester-weight rule of thumb used in community: **A mg × 0.87 ≈ E mg × 0.71** → **E dose ≈ A dose × 1.22** for similar weekly free hormone (ignore PK shape). **Probable** (stoichiometry); **Speculative** as bioequivalence because peak shape differs.

**Free-hormone half-life** after release is short (~hours class, Wikipedia cites 6–8 h for parent — poorly referenced). Depot release, not terminal free-drug half-life, drives ester duration. **Probable.**

**Human PK data quality:** Sparse. Yarrow rat TREN-enanthate weekly IM: plasma peak ~48 h, levels sustained ~7 days — supports multi-day enanthate depot. **Probable** in rodents; human t½ numbers are secondary citations / structural papers, not N=robust volunteer PK.

### 2.3 Formulation notes

- UGL concentration, solvent (BA/BB, guaiacol, EO), and oil type alter PIP and POME/cough risk more than ester identity alone.  
- “Homebrew from Finaplix” = acetate pellets dissolved into oil — purity/endotoxin/filter risk high. **Probable** harm vector.  
- Enanthate never had a major human pharmaceutical brand; all E is underground. Hex pharma is extinct; current “Parabolan” labels are UGL.

## 4. Clinical evidence (full evaluation)

### 4.1 What exists (and does not)

| Tier | What we have | What we do **not** have |
|------|----------------|-------------------------|
| **Human RCT (performance)** | None for any tren ester as PED | Dose–response for hypertrophy, strength, body comp in athletes |
| **Human pharmaceutical** | Historical Parabolan (Hex) use in France (underweight/catabolic indications era) — sparse modern English outcome data; discontinued 1997 | Controlled efficacy trials comparable to modern T trials |
| **Human PK** | Essentially absent as formal studies; half-lives are secondary citations (Borodi structural paper quoting durations; Ruiz/Strain textbook-style acetate ~3 d; Piatkowski qualitative paper citing Ace 1–2 d, Hex ~8 d, En ~11 d) | Crossover A vs E plasma AUC in humans |
| **Case reports / series** | Cholestasis with “Parabolan” (Anand 2006 — labeling confusion in abstract re acetate); CV events, psych, injection injury in tren/AAS reviews (Borecki 2024; structured case reviews ~2025); oil injection lung injury | Attribution pure to ester vs polypharmacy |
| **Qualitative / survey** | Piatkowski 2023 qualitative tren harms (“mind went to mush”); Parkinson & Evans 2006 AAS survey (class); Underwood/DrugPolicyFacts compound-specific harm framing | Validated psychometrics by ester |
| **Preclinical efficacy** | **Yarrow 2011** (TREN-enanthate IM weekly, orchiectomized rats): LABC hypertrophy, fat ↓, bone, Hb↑, prostate *not* enlarged like T — tissue-selective anabolic signal. McCoy 2012 BMD preservation. Yarrow 2010 review: SARM-like rationale | Direct human translation |
| **Receptor / binding** | **Bauer 2000**: 17β-TbOH rhAR ~DHT; bPR slightly > progesterone; metabolites weak; low SHBG. Relative affinity tables: AR ~190–197% of T; PR ~74–75% of P4; GR low | Full human tissue gene-expression map |
| **Neuro** | Ma 2015 17β-trenbolone neurodegeneration; Zelleroth 2019 AAS cortical culture toxicity; Bjørnebekk 2017 long-term AAS brain structure (class) | Dose-matched human imaging for tren esters |
| **Veterinary / residue** | WHO/JECFA TBA livestock; environmental “vampire steroid” reformation literature — exposure biology, not PED dosing | — |

### 4.2 Claim-level evaluation

**Claim: Trenbolone is a high-potency AR agonist with anabolic > classical androgenic tissue growth in models.**  
- Measured: binding, rodent muscle/bone/prostate, cattle lean mass.  
- Effect: large anabolic signal at doses that spare prostate relative to T in ORX rats (Yarrow).  
- Limits: rats less androgen-sensitive; “5× testosterone” internet A:A ratios are folklore vs ~3× affinity in better sources.  
- **Certainty: Established** (potent AR agonist); **Probable** (tissue selectivity narrative).

**Claim: Acetate vs enanthate differ in duration and injection practicality, not intrinsic anabolic quality.**  
- Measured: ester physics + sparse depot PK; community concordance.  
- **Certainty: Established** (prodrug principle); **Probable** (human t½ ranges as cited).

**Claim: Tren causes severe neuropsychiatric and sleep disruption in users.**  
- Measured: qualitative interviews, case reports, forum; preclinical CNS toxicity.  
- Effect direction: consistent harm signal; magnitude individual.  
- Limits: poly-drug, selection bias, no ester-randomized psych trial.  
- **Certainty: Probable** (user-level phenotype); **Speculative** (mechanism completeness, ester difference in *rate* not *type*).

**Claim: Tren is cardiovascularly adverse (lipids, BP, hard outcomes).**  
- AAS class effect + tren-enriched case reports (cardiomyopathy, MI, stroke in structured reviews).  
- **Certainty: Probable** risk elevation; ester difference secondary to duration of exposure and peaks.

**Claim: Hex/Parabolan is “milder” or “better” than A/E.**  
- No comparative human trial. Historical clinical dose was low (50 mg base / 10 d ≈ 35 mg base/week) — mildness may be **dose**, not ester magic.  
- **Certainty: Speculative** (milder myth); **Probable** that low historical dose explains gentler reputation.

### 4.3 What clinical data do **not** show

- Do **not** show safe long-term human PED protocols.  
- Do **not** show superiority of E over A for muscle gain at matched free-hormone AUC.  
- Do **not** show that any ester avoids trensomnia/CV/psych at performance doses.  
- Do **not** validate prolactin as the necessary mediator of all “progestin sides.”  
- Do **not** quantify human GR antagonism contribution to “anticatabolic” effect.

## 6. Desired effects — mechanism depth (shared) + ester practical differences

### 6.1 Lean mass / hypertrophy / nitrogen retention

- **Phenotype:** Rapid increases in muscularity and “full-hard” look at relatively low mg vs testosterone.  
- **Pathways:** P1 (AR myonuclear/protein synthesis), P6 (hypothesized GR anticatabolic), P12 (partitioning), P16 (IGF local — speculative).  
- **Chain:** Free trenbolone → high-affinity AR → anabolic gene program; low aromatization → less water vs wet compounds; livestock data show improved feed efficiency/mineral absorption.  
- **Clinical:** Rodent LABC and cattle lean mass **strong**; human RCT **absent**.  
- **Forum:** Near-universal “best recomp compound” reputation.  
- **Ester note:** Matched free-hormone exposure → similar tissue effect. A front-loads peaks; E builds cumulative levels. First 1–2 weeks A “feels” faster.  
- **Certainty:** Anabolic potency **Probable–Established** (preclinical + consensus); human effect size **Probable**.

### 6.2 Fat loss / nutrient partitioning

- **Phenotype:** Fat loss or recomp while holding muscle in deficit.  
- **Pathways:** P1, P12, P13, possible adrenergic/metabolic secondary effects.  
- **Clinical:** Rodent adiposity ↓ (Yarrow); cattle composition.  
- **Forum:** High for recomp; appetite drop can aid deficit (or destroy training fuel).  
- **Ester:** No difference in fat-oxidation biochemistry; adherence/appetite volatility may track peaks (A).  
- **Certainty:** **Probable** partitioning; magnitude context-dependent.

### 6.3 Strength and neural drive

- **Phenotype:** Rapid strength density, aggression in the gym.  
- **Pathways:** P1 CNS AR, P8, RBC/oxygen (P11).  
- **Forum:** Very high. Injury risk if load jumps.  
- **Ester:** A peaks can feel “spikier” for aggression/strength days.  
- **Certainty:** **Probable**.

### 6.4 Cosmetic “dryness” / hardness

- **Phenotype:** Less subcutaneous water than wet aromatizing stacks.  
- **Pathways:** P2 non-aromatizing; glycogen/electrolyte secondary.  
- **Caveat:** Night sweats and inflammation can still blur look.  
- **Certainty:** **Probable** vs wet orals/test-only high E2.

### 6.5 Practical ester differences for *desired* effects

| Goal | Prefer | Why | Certainty |
|------|--------|-----|-----------|

## 7. Side effects — mechanism depth + counters

*Ester callout pattern:* **Type of side is shared.** **Onset, peak sharpness, and washout** differ. A: sides appear early and can spike with peaks; drop dose → faster relief. E: sides accumulate into steady-state; lowering dose takes 1–3+ weeks to feel. Hex: closer to E.

### 7.1 Insomnia / “trensomnia” / fragmented sleep

1. **Phenotype:** Delayed sleep onset, mid-night waking, light sleep, vivid dreams; often dose-limiting.  
2. **Mechanism:**  
   - **Probable:** CNS AR activation → arousal, sleep architecture disruption.  
   - **Probable phenotype / Speculative mechanism:** night-time thermogenic/autonomic drive (sweats) fragments sleep.  
   - **Speculative:** orexin/wake-drive up-weighting; monoamine shifts; PR CNS effects.  
   - **Not established:** simple “high cortisol” as sole cause.

1. **Phenotype:** Short fuse, rage, anxiety, anhedonia, cognitive fog; qualitative research documents severe subjective cognitive/emotional impairment on tren.  
2. **Mechanism:** Limbic/prefrontal AR; sleep loss amplifier; possible direct neurotoxicity (preclinical); polypharmacy confounders. **Probable** user-level; **Speculative** molecular path completeness.

### 7.5 “Tren cough” / acute post-injection respiratory spasm

1. **Phenotype:** Violent dry cough, sometimes chest tightness, oily taste, dizziness — seconds to minutes after pin.  
2. **Mechanism:** **Established class:** pulmonary oil microembolism (POME) from oil entering venous circulation. Secondary hypotheses: prostaglandin release, solvent irritation. **Not** unique to tren; name is cultural.  
3. **Why A more reported:** More frequent pins + often higher solvent ratios in short-ester UGL + user expectation.  
4. **Counters**

| Pathway node | Counter | How | Clinical | Forum | Notes | Certainty |
|--------------|---------|-----|----------|-------|-------|-----------|
| IV injection | Aspirate; slow deep IM; rotate sites | Prevent venous oil | POME prevention teaching | High | | **Established** technique |
| Oil volume | Lower volume per pin; dilute carefully | Less embolic load | TU POME volume association | Moderate | | **Probable** |
| Solvent harshness | Cleaner carrier if quality known | Irritation ↓ | Thin | Common brew talk | UGL unknown | **Speculative** |
| Acute episode | Sit upright, calm breathing; ER if hypoxia/severe | Supportive | Case reports | High | Rare severe ARDS path | **Probable** |

### 7.6 HPTA suppression / hypogonadism off-cycle

1. **Phenotype:** Crash libido, fatigue, depression post-cycle without recovery plan; infertility risk.  
2. **Mechanism:** Potent AR + progestin feedback → LH/FSH collapse (**Established** class, strong for tren). Longer esters prolong exogenous presence → longer recovery lag (**Probable**).  
3. **Counters**

| Pathway node | Counter | How | Clinical | Forum | Notes | Certainty |
|--------------|---------|-----|----------|-------|-------|-----------|
| On-cycle T production | Always run exogenous testosterone base | Replace T | AAS practice | Near-universal modern | Non-optional for most | **Probable** |
| Recovery | Time clearance (longer for E/Hex); SERM/hCG protocols under medical care | Restart axis | TRT/PCT literature mixed quality | High | Bloodwork-guided | **Probable** need; protocol details **Speculative** without labs |
| Fertility | Bank sperm pre-use | Risk mitigation | Fertility medicine | Occasional | | **Probable** |

### 7.7 Sexual dysfunction / libido extremes / prolactin narrative

1. **Phenotype:** Hyperlibido early → ED, anorgasmia, “deca-dick”-like flatness.  
2. **Mechanism:** Suppression of gonadotropins; PR agonism; possible PRL elevation in some (**Speculative** necessity); estrogen from T base too high or too low.  
3. **Counters**

| Pathway node | Counter | How | Clinical | Forum | Notes | Certainty |

| Documented hyperprolactinemia | Dopamine agonists **only if labs + clinician** | D2→PRL↓ | Endocrinology | Common PED misuse | Blind caber harms | **Probable** when indicated; **Speculative** empiric |
| No T base | Add/maintain T | Androgen for erectile tissue | Physiology | High | | **Established** need for androgen |

### 7.8 Gynecomastia (despite non-aromatizing)

1. **Phenotype:** Glandular breast tissue growth.  
2. **Mechanism:** PR agonism synergizing with any circulating E2 (from T base or other); Borecki review cites PR + possible PRL/TRH loops. Tren does **not** need to convert to estrogen. **Probable.**  
3. **Counters**

| Pathway node | Counter | How | Clinical | Forum | Notes | Certainty |
|--------------|---------|-----|----------|-------|-------|-----------|
| E2 × PR synergy | Control E2 from base; lower tren | Removes co-signal | Breast endocrine | High | | **Probable** |
| Early tissue | SERM (e.g. tamoxifen) protocols | ERα breast | Gyno standard | High | Medical oversight ideal | **Probable** |
| Established gland | Surgery | Structural | Standard | — | Drugs won't fully reverse mature gyno | **Established** |

### 7.9 Lipids / metabolic / insulin feel

1. **Phenotype:** Tanked HDL; some report carb intolerance.  
2. **Mechanism:** Hepatic lipid-protein remodeling under AAS (**Probable**); insulin sensitivity data mixed (**Speculative**).  
3. **Counters:** Diet, cardio, limit cycle length, labs; medical lipid therapy if indicated. **Probable** partial mitigation only.

### 7.10 Androgenic skin/hair

1. **Phenotype:** Acne, accelerated MPB in genetically prone.  
2. **Mechanism:** AR in skin/hair follicle — tren is strong AR agonist even without 5α-activation. Finasteride does **not** block tren (wrong enzyme target for this molecule). **Established** logic.  
3. **Counters:** Topical/standard acne care; accept hair risk or don't run; topical anti-androgens **Speculative** vs systemic tren.

### 7.11 Injection-site / contamination injury

1. **Phenotype:** PIP, abscess, fibrosis, rare Nicolau syndrome / necrosis (Borecki).  
2. **Mechanism:** Volume, solvent, infection, intra-arterial injection. More pins (A) → more opportunities.  
3. **Counters:** Sterile technique, site rotation, abandon dirty gear. **Established.**

### 7.12 Hepatic signals

1. **Phenotype:** Generally lower hepatotoxicity than 17α-alkyl orals, but case cholestasis exists (Anand Parabolan report); polypharmacy common.  
2. **Certainty:** Oral-like toxicity **not** expected as class default; idiosyncratic injury **Possible**. Monitor if stacked with orals.

## 12. Excerpt — Tren Hex (Parabolan) vs A and E

### 12.1 Identity

- **Trenbolone hexahydrobenzylcarbonate** = trenbolone cyclohexylmethylcarbonate.  
- Brand history: **Parabolan**, **Hexabolan** (Negma, France). Marketed for human use ~**1980–1997**, then discontinued.  
- Clinical ampoule: **76 mg ester ≈ 50 mg trenbolone base**, dosed about **every 10 days** in labeled use.  
- Same free hormone after ester cleavage → **same pathway map (P1–P18)**.

### 12.2 PK position

| | Ace | Hex | Enanthate |
|--|-----|-----|-----------|
| Cited t½ | ~1–3 d | **~8 d** (range ~5–10; some forums claim 10–14) | ~7–11 d |
| Active fraction | ~87% | **~66%** | ~71% |
| Clinical / practical pin | ED–EOD | q5–10 d / 2× week | 2–3× week |
| Human pharma history | Vet mainly | **Yes (historical)** | No |

Hex is a **long ester**, operationally closer to enanthate than acetate. Borodi structural literature groups Hex ~8 d between Ace and En. Forum claims of Hex “longer than En” exist but conflict with the commonly cited 8 vs 11 d figures — treat exact ranking as **Uncertain within long-ester band**.

### 12.3 Myths vs mechanism

| Claim | Assessment |
|-------|------------|
| “Parabolan is milder / less side-effecty” | **Speculative.** Historical clinical dose was **low** (~35 mg base/week). PED UGL doses often match A/E performance ranges → sides return. |
| “Hex is the only real tren” | False. Free hormone identical. Pharma purity nostalgia ≠ receptor difference. |
| “Hex avoids trensomnia” | **Speculative / contradicted** by long-ester user reports when dose is real. |
| “Hex better for joints / quality” | Carrier oil and dose, not magic ester. **Speculative.** |
| Scarcity / price premium | Real market phenomenon; not pharmacology. |

### 12.4 Practical Hex notes

- **When Hex makes sense:** User wants long ester, fewer pins, and accepts slow titration; or reconstructing low-and-slow historical-style exposure.  
- **When it does not:** First tren experiment (use A); need fast exit; unreliable UGL labeling of “Parabolan.”  
- **Dose framing:** Compare **mg of base**, not mg on the vial. 228 mg Hex/week ester ≈ 150 mg base/week ≈ roughly **~172 mg Ace** or **~211 mg En** ester-weight for similar base (stoichiometry only). **Probable** math; **Speculative** felt equivalence.  
- **Sides:** Same catalog as A/E. Onset/washout like other long esters. Cough still possible (oil). Cholestasis case literature includes Parabolan-labeled product (polypharmacy era).  
- **Detection:** Long esters → long doping detection; Hex historically associated with multi-month windows in sport anti-doping folklore — treat as **Probable** long detection vs short Ace.

### 12.5 Bottom line (Hex)

Hex is **not a fourth hormone**. It is trenbolone on a long carbonate ester with a romantic pharmaceutical backstory. Prefer A for control, E for common long-ester convenience, Hex only if product integrity is trusted and long-ester kinetics are desired. **Probable.**

## 13. Open questions

Data that would most change this map:

1. **Human crossover PK** of A vs E vs Hex (serial plasma 17β-trenbolone, peak/trough, terminal release half-lives).  
2. **Sleep architecture PSG** under controlled tren dose vs placebo (orexin/cortisol/thermal mediation).  
3. **Prolactin and PR occupancy** studies in humans on tren ± testosterone with sexual phenotype endpoints.  
4. **Randomized ester comparison** at matched base-AUC for lipids, BP, mood scales, body composition.  
5. **Cardiac MRI / troponin** short-term signals at performance doses.  
6. **GR functional antagonism** quantified in human muscle under deficit.  
7. **Neuroimaging + cognitive batteries** pre/post tren (ester-stratified).  
8. **True UGL content assays** correlating labeled ester with analytical reality (confounds all forum dose lore).  
9. **Whether historical Parabolan low-dose regimens** produce acceptable risk/benefit for clinical cachexia (modern trial).  
10. **Genetic predictors** of trensomnia and psych intolerance (AR CAG repeats, etc.) — currently **Unknown**.

## 15. Short boundary

This document maps mechanisms, evidence tiers, and practical ester differences for research-oriented compound users. It is not a prescription, not medical advice, and not assurance of safety. Trenbolone esters are potent Schedule-controlled AAS in many jurisdictions, with substantial cardiometabolic, neuropsychiatric, and endocrine risk; human performance RCTs are absent. Decisions require personal legal context, baseline health, labs, and preferably clinician involvement — especially for blood pressure, cardiac symptoms, and psychiatric change.
