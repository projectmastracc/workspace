# NSI-189 (Amdiglurax / ALTO-100) — Compound Profile

**Research chemical / investigational neurogenic small molecule**  
**Audience:** compound users, performance/nootropic stackers, depression-cognition experimenters  
**Certainty labels:** Established · Probable · Speculative · Unknown  
**Evidence hierarchy (this run):** T1 primary RCT endpoints > T2 early/secondary clinical > T3 post-hoc/opinion > T4 company/patent > T5 preclinical > T6 forums  
**Bottom line:** Failed primary MADRS endpoints in two independent Phase 2 MDD programs (Papakostas 2020; Alto 2024). Cognitive secondary signals at **40 mg** are the residual clinical interest (unconfirmed in healthy users). Short-term (≤12 weeks) tolerability is good. Long-term safety unknown. Gray-market product is research-chemical territory with purity risk.

## 2. Chemistry, Classification, Pharmacokinetics

### 2.1 Chemistry

- **Scaffold:** Benzylpiperazine linked via amide to a 2-(3-methylbutylamino)pyridine — “isonicotinamide-like” neurogenic hit from Neuralstem’s ~10,269-compound phenotypic screen against human fetal hippocampal NSCs.
- **Salt forms:** Clinical programs used **NSI-189 phosphate**. Gray-market vendors sell freebase powder, phosphate powder, and phosphate capsules (e.g. 20 mg). Phosphate is more water-soluble/stable for oral solid formulations.
- **Mass equivalence (approximate, community):** Freebase MW 366.5; phosphate adds phosphate mass → rough rule of thumb **~32 mg freebase ≈ 40 mg phosphate salt**. Do not treat as bioequivalence without measured exposure. **Certainty: Speculative** (forum conversion).
- **Appearance / handling:** Off-white to pale solid (vendor descriptions). Oral small molecule — not an injectable peptide. No reconstitution.

### 2.2 Classification

|------|------------|-----------|
| Pharmacologic class | Neurogenic / neuroplasticity agent | Established (by design & phenotype) |
| Molecular target | TLX/NR2E1 agonist (EC₅₀ ~36.1 pM ALPHA assay, patent WO2022140643) | Probable (disclosed; not full peer-reviewed MoA paper as sole public primary) |
| Downstream | Indirect BDNF, SCF, (less) VEGF/GDNF; TrkB/Akt | Probable (preclinical + Ab-blockade experiments) |

**Practical PK implications**

- Once-daily dosing is PK-rational. BID splits (e.g. 40 mg BID = 80 mg/day in Ph2) were for higher daily dose, not short half-life.
- Full exposure takes ~4–5 days; any “day-1 stimulant feel” is not putative neurogenesis (weeks).
- Washout after multi-week use is multi-day. Residual subjective effects weeks after stop in Ph1b follow-up (to day 84) are **not** explained by residual parent drug alone — delayed plasticity, expectancy, or natural course of illness (**Speculative** which dominates).

### 2.4 Dose–exposure vs rodent neurogenesis

Mouse hippocampal **volume** increases: ~**36% at 10 mg/kg**, ~**66% at 30 mg/kg**, **bell-shaped** (100 mg/kg less effective than 30 mg/kg). Human clinical doses (40–120 mg/day in ~70 kg adult ≈ 0.6–1.7 mg/kg) sit **well below** those mouse volume-driving mg/kg levels. Human Ph1b MRI: **no significant** hippocampal or amygdala volume change (left HC numerical b=0.35, p=0.12, NS).

| Claim | Certainty |
|-------|-----------|
| Mouse volume ↑ at high mg/kg, bell-shaped | Established |
| Clinical MRI NS at studied doses/duration | Established |
| Human clinical exposure sufficient for mouse-comparable structural neurogenesis | Speculative (likely insufficient or wrong PD marker) |

## 4. Clinical Evidence — Full Evaluation

### 4.1 Methods frame (how to weight this section)

| Tier | Source | Confirmatory value |
|------|--------|--------------------|
| T1 | Papakostas 2020 primary MADRS (failed); Alto 2024 primary MADRS (failed) | Highest for efficacy conclusions — both **negative** |
| T2 | Fava 2016 safety/PK; Papakostas secondaries | Medium; cannot override T1 primary misses |
| T3 | Johe 2020 post-hoc; McIntyre 2017 review | Hypothesis-generating only |
| T4 | Alto press residual spin; patents; company screens | Context + MoA hypothesis |
| T5 | Preclinical multi-lab | MoA plausibility only |
| T6 | Longecity / Reddit | AE pattern-spotting only |

**Governing rule:** Pre-specified primary MADRS failures are high-weight negative evidence. SPCD (Papakostas) was designed to *improve* signal detection under high placebo response — a failed primary under SPCD is more credible null than a failed simple parallel trial of equal size. Positive secondaries after failed primary = hypothesis-generating under multiplicity. Large cognitive d (e.g. memory ~1.12) after failed primary is a red flag for overestimation until independent pre-specified replication. Sponsor-proximate authorship discounts promotional packaging but **does not** invalidate negative primaries (those cut against sponsor interest).

### 4.3 Phase 1b (Fava 2016) — detail

- **Population:** Recurrent MDD; remote diagnosis confirmation; inpatient dosing 28 days.
- **Arms:** 40 mg QD / BID / TID (n=6 active each) vs matched placebo (n=2 per cohort).
- **Primary objective:** safety, tolerability, PK — **not** powered efficacy.
- **Safety:** Relatively well tolerated all doses; **no SAEs**. Most common AEs: **headache, dizziness, somnolence**.
- **PK:** AUC dose-related nearly proportional; t½ **17.4–20.5 h**.
- **Exploratory efficacy:** SDQ and CPFQ significantly improved vs placebo; MADRS/CGI moved with medium–large effect sizes (underpowered). Improvements **persisted in follow-up to day 84** (off drug after day 28) — interesting for neuroplasticity narrative, confounded by tiny n, thin placebo arm, natural history, possible medication restarts.
- **MRI:** HI/amygdala volumes — **no statistically significant** drug effect; modest left HI numerical trend only. Underpowered null: does not disprove microstructural change, **does** fail to support mouse volume narrative at human Phase 1 doses/duration.
- **Methods limit:** Cells of 6:2 → imbalance, unblinding risk by AE pattern; multiplicity across scales/doses/timepoints; medium-large exploratory d’s expected under sampling noise.
- **Interpretation:** Safety + PK locked in; efficacy hypothesis-generating only. **Certainty of antidepressant effect from this study alone: Speculative.**

### 4.4 Phase 2 Neuralstem / Papakostas 2020 — detail (primary failure)

- **Design:** SPCD to reduce placebo-response inflation. Stage 1: 1:1:3 randomization to 40 mg, 80 mg, or placebo × 6 weeks. Stage 1 placebo non-responders re-randomized 1:1:1 to 40 / 80 / PBO for Stage 2. Total randomized **220** (40 mg n=44; 80 mg n=44; PBO n=132 Stage 1). Completers ~77%.
- **Inclusion:** Ages 18–60, DSM-5 MDD ≥8 weeks, MADRS ≥20. Excluded: ≥3 failed adequate AD trials this episode, bipolar/psychosis, unstable medical illness, recent SUD, concurrent antidepressants (some stable benzos/hypnotics/T3 allowed).
- **COI note (T1 primary still valid):** Heavy Neuralstem sponsorship; industry-tied authors; CogScreen collaborator dual role. Primary miss is against sponsor interest → high-credibility negative.

**Primary (MADRS) — FAILED**

| Dose | Pooled mean difference vs PBO | p | Cohen’s d notes |
|------|-------------------------------|---|-----------------|
| 40 mg | −1.8 | **0.224** | Stage1 d≈−0.17; Stage2 d≈−0.25 |
| 80 mg | −1.4 | **0.344** | Stage1 d≈−0.14; Stage2 d≈−0.19 |

Context: typical approved AD drug–placebo d ≈ 0.3; study powered for d ≥ 0.5. Observed MADRS separation is **sub-clinical and non-significant**. MCID for MADRS often ~5 points — observed difference ~1.4–1.8 points. HAMD-17 also NS (pooled p≈0.15–0.20).

**Secondary self-report (40 mg signals)**

| Scale | 40 mg pooled Δ vs PBO | p | Notes |
|-------|----------------------|---|-------|
| SDQ | −8.2 | **0.044** | Stage2 d=−0.64 drove pooling; Stage1 d=−0.11 |
| CPFQ | −1.9 | **0.035** | Cognition/physical function self-report |
| QIDS-SR | Stage2 −2.5 | Stage2 **0.040**; pooled NS | Only at stage endpoints (weaker analysis) |

SDQ item-level advantages (pooled): low affect, mood responsiveness, tearfulness, anxiety, decisions, work ability, functioning, optimism, outlook. Leaving CPFQ items out of SDQ still left SDQ significant → non-identical constructs.

**80 mg:** Generally **did not** separate on self-report depression scales. Flat/inverted dose–response is a clinical red flag for pure linear neurogenic story (or 40 mg near optimum / higher-dose noise).

**Objective cognition**

- **CogScreen:** Multiple subtests favored 40 mg; absolute Cohen’s d **0.12–1.12**; significant p 0.002–0.048 on selected endpoints. Delayed symbol-digit recall particularly large (d up to ~1.12). Working memory d≈0.81; executive function d≈0.66.
- **Cogstate battery:** **No** significant combined benefit — battery discordance undermines clean pro-cognitive claim.
- Multiplicity: cognition exploratory; positive CogScreen needs **independent replication with pre-specified cognitive primary**.

**Safety Phase 2**

- No drug-related SAEs.
- Stage 1 discontinuation **lower** on drug than placebo (PBO 18.9% vs 40 mg 9.1% vs 80 mg 2.3%, p=0.013) — unusual; possibly chance or less “activation” distress.
- Tolerability overall good; headache cluster consistent with Ph1b.

**Overall Phase 2 verdict:** Primary regulatory-style endpoint **failed**. Secondary self-report + some objective cognitive signals at **40 mg only** are real statistical findings in this dataset but **do not rescue** antidepressant approval path. **Certainty antidepressant efficacy: Not established (failed primary). Certainty of some patient-reported / cognitive signal at 40 mg in MDD: Probable within this trial, Speculative generalizability.**

### 4.5 Post-hoc Johe 2020 — moderate vs severe (T3 only)

- Dichotomized baseline MADRS at **30**.
- **80 mg** showed MADRS-6 benefit in **moderately** depressed (MADRS <30), p=0.046; **not** in severe (MADRS ≥30).
- CogScreen positives denser in moderate subgroup.
- Authors suggested adjunctive use / moderate-severity enrichment; conclusion language (“is effective”) **overreaches**.
- **Certainty: Speculative.** Post-hoc, garden-of-forking-paths risk, does not overturn failed primary. Alto’s later biomarker enrichment also failed at Ph2b.

### 4.6 Alto Neuroscience ALTO-100 Phase 2b (Oct 2024) — second independent failure

- **n=301** adults with MDD selected by **objective memory-based cognitive biomarker** prior to randomization; 34 US sites; 6-week double-blind; mono or adjunctive to inadequate-response AD.
- **Primary:** MADRS change end of DB period in biomarker+ population — **did not meet**.
- Key secondaries: also no benefit over placebo (company topline).
- Safety: favorable; common related AEs **headache, nausea, abnormal dreams** — rates similar to placebo. Replicates short-term tolerability narrative.
- Residual IR spin (T4 only): exploratory adjunctive biomarker+ slice Cohen’s d≈0.47, p≈0.09 — **not powered, not primary**.
- Strategic outcome: **not advancing in MDD**; full dataset review; **bipolar depression Ph2b** continues.
- **Transparency limit:** Press-release evidence incomplete until full peer-reviewed manuscript (CONSORT detail, CIs, assay lock, secondary hierarchy).
- **Interpretation:** Precision enrichment that looked promising in smaller Ph2a **did not replicate** under Phase 2b rigor. Second independent primary failure for the same molecule under a new sponsor. Classic enrichment optimism → Phase 2b deflation.

### 4.8 External validity: MDD trials → healthy nootropic users

| Dimension | MDD trial population | Healthy / nootropic user |
|-----------|----------------------|---------------------------|
| Baseline MADRS / anhedonia | Elevated; room to improve | Floor effects on depression scales |
| Cognitive impairment | Often state-related | May be near ceiling on many batteries |
| Endpoint relevance | MADRS, SDQ, CPFQ | Subjective “clarity,” productivity — unvalidated |
| Risk–benefit | Unmet need may justify experimental risk | Lower benefit ceiling; same unknown long-term risk |
| Regulatory / ethical | Structured consent, monitoring | Research-chem self-experimentation |

**Methods conclusion on transfer:** Even positive MDD secondaries would not automatically imply nootropic benefit in healthy people. With **failed primaries** in unenriched and biomarker-enriched MDD, transfer to healthy users is weaker still. Healthy-user efficacy is **especially underdetermined**.

## 6. Desired Effects — Mechanism Depth

### 6.1 Putative antidepressant / anti-anhedonia effect

**Clinical standing:** Not established on MADRS (dual primary fails); secondary SDQ items (mood responsiveness, optimism, functioning) positive at 40 mg in Ph2; Alto biomarker+ MADRS fail; exploratory adjunctive d≈0.47 p≈0.09 underpowered.  
**Verdict: Probable null as monotherapy for broad MDD; Speculative niche only.**

**Mechanistic depth:**

1. Chronic stress / glucocorticoid → HI atrophy associated with MDD severity, recurrence, early adversity (**Established** epidemiology/imaging).  
2. Adult hippocampal neurogenesis modulates stress resilience and some antidepressant behavioral effects in rodents (**Probable** animals; **Speculative** necessity in human MDD).  
3. NSI-189 was built to force the neurogenesis node without monoamines (**Established** design intent).  
4. If TLX agonism holds → **NSC-centric** rather than synapse-first (ketamine) or monoamine-first (SSRI) attack (**Probable** target class novelty).  
5. BDNF/TrkB is a final common path for many antidepressants and for exercise; NSI-189 may hitch this path **indirectly** (**Probable** preclinical; **Speculative** human).  
6. **P11 mesolimbic chain:** HI→VTA/NAc anhedonia theory supports “emotional range” narrative (**Speculative** NSI engagement); dual MADRS fails argue against strong reliable remittance.  
7. **Why MADRS may fail while SDQ moves:** MADRS optimized around TCA-era clusters; SDQ samples cognition, reactivity, functioning more broadly — authors argued this. Alternative: SDQ more placebo-/expectancy-sensitive. Both plausible (**Speculative** which dominates).  
8. **Why 40 mg > 80 mg clinically:** Bell-shaped biology (mouse volume), receptor desensitization, off-target emergence, side contamination of scales, or chance + small effective-n per arm. **Unknown.**

**Practical expectation if someone still experiments:** Any mood change is more likely **subtle, multi-week, multi-domain (energy/cognition/reactivity)** than classic SSRI “lift” or ketamine “reset.” Severe melancholic MDD is the **least** justified use-case given Johe post-hoc and dual MADRS fails.

### 6.2 Cognitive / memory effects

**Clinical standing:** Best positive thread — CogScreen memory d up to ~1.12, executive and working-memory signals at 40 mg in MDD; Cogstate null; healthy enhancement untested.  
**Verdict: Probable for depressed patients at ~40 mg within one failed-primary trial; Unknown for healthy non-depressed users.**

**Mechanistic depth:**

1. Dentate gyrus neurogenesis contributes to **pattern separation**, contextual memory, flexible learning in rodents (**Probable**).  
2. MDD cognitive impairment correlates with HI structure/function more tightly than some mood dimensions (**Probable** association).  
3. Largest human cognitive signal on delayed symbol-digit recall is **hippocampal-ish** in cognitive neuropsychology terms (**Speculative** mapping).  
4. Radiation and stroke models show cognitive rescue with neurogenesis/inflammation shifts (**Probable** in those models) — supports **injury/depletion** contexts more than “healthy optimizer.”  
5. Protein-synthesis-dependent LTP (P5) supports early synaptic facilitation independent of multi-week neurogenesis — dual-phase model.  
6. **Transfer to healthy high-performers:** empirical vacuum — ceiling effects, different bottlenecks (PFC monoamines, sleep), possible that neurogenesis boost only helps when baseline is suppressed.

**Practical expectation:** Do not count on IQ-test gains. Most rational residual hypothesis is **partial remediation of depression-related cognitive inefficiency**, not limitless enhancement. CogScreen ≠ Cogstate discordance is a hard limit on certainty.

## 11. Comparative Context

| Comparator | MoA sketch | Onset | Depression evidence | Cognition | Practical vs NSI-189 |
|------------|------------|-------|---------------------|-----------|----------------------|
| **SSRIs** (e.g. escitalopram) | SERT inhibition → monoamine + downstream BDNF | 2–6 wk | **Established** efficacy (modest d~0.3) | Often limited / side-effect dulling | NSI-189 failed to beat PBO on MADRS; SSRI is standard-of-care tool |
| **SNRIs / bupropion / mirtazapine** | Multi-monoamine | 2–6 wk | Established | Variable | Same regulatory gap for NSI-189 |

| **7,8-DHF / other TrkB agonist concepts** | Direct TrkB agonism hypotheses | — | Mostly preclinical | Preclinical | Related BDNF-axis idea; human data weaker than NSI-189’s trial volume |
| **Dihexa** | Ang IV analog; HGF/c-Met pro-cognitive claims | — | No serious depression Ph2 like NSI-189 | Preclinical heavy | **More** speculative clinically; worse evidence base than NSI-189 for human depression trials |

| **SSRIs + therapy / rTMS / ECT** | Network / monoamine / seizure remodeling | Variable | Stronger evidence ladder | Domain-specific | Medical pathways for real MDD |

**Positioning statement:** NSI-189 is **scientifically interesting** as a rare oral clinical-stage neurogenesis phenotype with secondary cognitive signals, and **clinically disappointing** as an antidepressant. It sits **below** approved ADs and procedural options for mood disorders, **beside** other experimental BDNF/neurogenesis RCs for curiosity, and **below** sleep/exercise for any risk-adjusted plasticity goal.

**Most defensible human signal:** pro-cognitive in depressed patients at ~40 mg — not antidepressant monotherapy. Positioning as general nootropic for healthy brains remains **Unknown**. TLX-neurogenesis story remains the best molecular narrative (P4) but is **not** validated by Phase 2b mood success.

## 12. Open Questions

1. **Is TLX agonism the true human MoA?** Patent-level EC₅₀ is striking; peer-reviewed target engagement PD markers in humans are missing. Need free brain EC₅₀ alignment and TLX-dependent knockout rescue fully public.  
2. **Why 40 mg > 80 mg** on several Ph2 self-report outcomes? True hormesis vs noise vs PK/PD nonlinearity vs side contamination of scales.  
3. **Are CogScreen gains real and durable?** Need replication with pre-registered cognitive primary endpoints, multiplicity control, and functional outcomes (work, driving). Why CogScreen ≠ Cogstate?  
4. **Healthy brain enhancement?** Completely open — ceiling effects likely. Zero adequate RCTs.  
5. **Minimum effective exposure for any CNS plasticity** relative to mouse mg/kg volume doses — are clinical doses underdosed for structure, yet still hit functional synapses?  
6. **Long-term safety of chronic TLX/NSC stimulation** (neoplasia, dysplasia, seizure, personality change).  
7. **Bipolar depression Ph2b** — will adjunctive design + different biology finally hit a primary?  
8. **Adjunctive niche:** Alto underpowered d=0.47 signal — needs powered replication before Speculative niche upgrades.  
9. **Sex, age, baseline HI volume, inflammation, cortisol** as modifiers — largely uncut.  
10. **Full human metabolism, CYP phenotyping, transporter profile** for rational interaction management.  
11. **Gray-market product quality epidemiology** — how often is “NSI-189” even the right molecule?  
12. **Does failed MADRS mean failed mechanism or failed indication/scale/population?** Cognition-first indications (radiation, chemo-brain, MCI) remain scientifically cleaner tests than broad MDD.  
13. **Oleic acid / dietary fat / BBB TLX biology** — any nutritional interaction with synthetic TLX agonists?  
14. **NET 1.1 μM:** needs primary source confirmation and free concentration modeling — currently Speculative.  
15. **If human neurogenesis is the clinical effector:** need better PD (imaging, biomarkers) than NS HC volume at 4 weeks.

Technical evidence synthesis for educated adults evaluating an investigational research chemical.
