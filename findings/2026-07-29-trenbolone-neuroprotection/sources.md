# Source Critique — Full Neuroprotection from Trenbolone

**RESEARCH_ID:** 94cfd27c  
**Role:** Source critic only (provenance, funding/COI, publication integrity, trust tiers, dual-compound interaction inventory).  
**Scope:** WHERE evidence comes from and HOW TRUSTWORTHY the source chain is — for trenbolone/AAS neural harms (A) and candidate mitigators (B: dose reduction/sleep defense, NAC, GABA-class supports, cerebrolysin/neurotrophic peptides, psychiatric care). No mechanism adjudication or efficacy synthesis.

**Method notes:** Full text retrieved for Scarth & Bjørnebekk 2021 (PMC8631164) and Klonteig et al. 2024 (PMC10804719). Prior workspace findings used for NAC and cerebrolysin mitigator provenance only. Combination literature searched via dual terms (NAC+trenbolone/AAS; cerebrolysin+trenbolone/AAS) — no human RCTs of full-stack neuroprotection found. PubMed MCP unavailable this pass; identifiers from PubMed/PMC/publisher pages and prior packages.

**Primary contrast to hold:** High-quality *harm association* literature (Oslo imaging/sleep; Zelleroth in-vitro tren signal; Chegeni aggression MA) is **not matched** by any high-trust literature that *full* multi-pathway neuroprotection against trenbolone is achievable. Mitigator evidence is **single-agent, different-indication** provenance (NAC for GSH/psych; cerebrolysin for stroke/TBI) plus **forum lore** — not interaction trials.

## 8. Practical rules for other personas

1. **Cite public funders (South-Eastern Norway Regional Health Authority)** when using Oslo imaging/sleep/review papers; state **no COI declared**.  
2. **Tren-specific cellular claims → Zelleroth PMID 33677017** with *in-vitro* tier; do not present as human MRI-level proof.  
3. **Sleep claims → Klonteig PMID 38254047 / PMC10804719** with self-report and class-AAS limits; do not equate to tren-only RCTs.  
4. **Aggression experimental → Chegeni DOI 10.1007/s00213-021-05818-7**; note small self-report *g* and null observer/high-dose strata.  
5. **NAC:** pull integrity from `findings/NAC/sources.md`; **do not** cite Tenório or Berk as if they tested AAS.  
6. **Cerebrolysin:** pull EVER/Cochrane split from `findings/cerebrolysin/sources.md`; **do not** use manufacturer PDFs as AAS evidence.  
7. **Forums:** descriptive only; **causal ceiling Unreliable**; never sole support for “full protection.”  
8. **Flag explicitly** whenever a high-confidence public narrative (full stack protection) is supported only by Low/Unreliable sources while Moderate–High sources document unmitigated associations of harm.  
9. **Unknown funding** on any paper where full COI block was not re-read line-by-line this pass should be stated (partial limitation for some secondary IDs such as Amaral full funding paragraph).

## 10. Bottom line (source critic only)

- **Strongest trustworthy chains** document **AAS-class** associations with brain structure/aging, sleep pathology, and small experimental increases in self-reported aggression, funded largely by **Norwegian public health**, plus **one key preclinical paper** highlighting trenbolone among AAS for cortical culture toxicity.  
- **Mitigator chains** for NAC and cerebrolysin are **indication-shifted** and, for cerebrolysin, **industry-structure–dominated** on the positive side.  
- **Dual-compound / full-stack neuroprotection against trenbolone has no High- or Moderate-trust primary source chain.**  
- **Forum consensus** on tren sleep/night sweats is multi-source and may align *directionally* with class AAS sleep literature, but remains **Low/Unreliable for causal mitigation claims**.  
- **Mismatch flag:** Popular certainty that “full neuroprotection is achievable with a stack” is **not** underwritten by the trust tiers of the available source inventory.
