# Naltrexone HCl — Full Research Briefing

**Slug:** `naltrexone` · **Type:** Compound profile · **Research ID:** 9178d80c · **Date:** 2026-07-31  
**Names:** Naltrexone hydrochloride · oral (ReVia, Depade, generics) · XR-NTX IM (Vivitrol) · low-dose naltrexone (LDN, off-label) · naltrexone/bupropion (Contrave)

---

## 1. Executive Card

| Field | Content |
|-------|---------|
| **Compound** | Competitive **opioid receptor antagonist** (primarily µ / MOR; weaker κ and δ). Pure antagonist — no agonist analgesia or euphoria at therapeutic doses. |
| **Primary use cases** | **FDA-approved:** alcohol use disorder (AUD); opioid use disorder (OUD) after detox. **Off-label / research:** low-dose naltrexone (LDN ~1–4.5 mg) for fibromyalgia, some autoimmune/inflammatory conditions; targeted “Sinclair Method” dosing for AUD; weight management as fixed combo with bupropion; cholestatic pruritus. |
| **Bottom line** | Naltrexone is a **first-line, evidence-based** medication for AUD (moderate effect; NNT ~12 for preventing return to heavy drinking in meta-analysis) and a **viable antagonist path** for OUD once opioid-free (oral adherence poor; XR-NTX stronger for retention when induction succeeds). Mechanism: blocks MOR → blunts opioid high and alcohol’s endorphin-mediated reward/dopamine facilitation; may dampen craving. **Critical safety:** never start if opioids on board → **precipitated withdrawal**; overdose risk if patients override blockade with high opioid doses after stopping. Hepatotoxicity is **dose-related** historically at high doses (~300 mg); at 50 mg monitoring still standard; LiverTox notes clinically apparent injury is not clearly established but LFT monitoring is practice. **LDN** has growing but still limited RCT support (best for fibromyalgia pain meta-signals); mechanism shifts toward **glial TLR4 / opioid growth factor** hypotheses rather than full daily MOR blockade. Not a “nootropic” or PED; performance relevance is harm-reduction for drinkers and pain/immune off-label use. |
| **Biggest upsides** | Reduces heavy drinking days / relapse risk in AUD (**Established–Probable**). Blocks exogenous opioids when adherent (**Established**). XR-NTX improves OUD abstinence metrics vs placebo when induction completed (**Probable**). LDN: possible pain relief in FM (**Probable** small-trial/meta). Non-scheduled, non-euphoric. |
| **Biggest downsides** | Precipitated withdrawal if not detoxed. Adherence (oral OUD). Nausea, dysphoria, sleep issues. Dose-related hepatotoxicity concern at high dose. Blocks needed opioids for surgery/acute pain. Overdose risk post-discontinuation. |
| **Stack role** | Medical addiction pharmacotherapy; optional LDN immune/pain experiment under clinician; **never** combine with opioid analgesics/heroin. |

**Certainty:** **Established** · **Probable** · **Speculative** · **Unknown**

## 2. Chemistry, classification, PK

### 2.1 Identity

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Class** | Competitive pure opioid antagonist (oxymorphone-related structure) |
| **Primary target** | µ-opioid receptor (MOR) >> κ (KOR), δ (DOR) |
| **Active metabolite** | **6β-naltrexol** (longer-lived antagonist contribution) |
| **Formulations** | Oral HCl tablets (typically 50 mg scored); XR IM 380 mg monthly (Vivitrol); compounded LDN capsules (1–4.5 mg common); fixed-dose naltrexone 8 mg + bupropion ER (Contrave titration) |

### 2.2 Pharmacokinetics (oral HCl)

| Parameter | Value | Certainty |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| **Absorption** | Nearly complete orally | **Established** |
| **Bioavailability** | **~5–40%** after extensive first-pass | **Established** |
| **Tmax** | ~1 h (parent and 6β-naltrexol) | **Established** |
| **t½ parent** | ~**4 h** | **Established** |
| **t½ 6β-naltrexol** | ~**13 h** | **Established** |
| **Protein binding** | ~21% | **Established** |
| **Vd** | Large (~1350 L IV estimate) | **Established** |
| **Metabolism** | Hepatic reduction to 6β-naltrexol; conjugation; extrahepatic contribution | **Established** |
| **Excretion** | Primarily renal (metabolites); little unchanged parent in urine | **Established** |
| **Dose proportionality** | AUC/Cmax proportional ~50–200 mg | **Established** |
| **XR-NTX** | Flip-flop kinetics; apparent t½ **5–10 days**; monthly 380 mg gluteal IM | **Established** |
| **Cirrhosis** | ↑ parent exposure / altered 6β-naltrexol ratio; caution | **Probable** |

**Duration of blockade (oral 50 mg):** clinically meaningful opioid blockade often cited ~24 h; receptor occupancy can outlast plasma parent levels because of potency + metabolite.

### 2.3 Standard dosing bands

| Indication | Typical regimen |
|------------|-----------------|
| **AUD oral** | **50 mg daily** (sometimes 100 mg QOD or 150 mg q3d historically); XR-NTX 380 mg IM q4wk |
| **OUD oral** | Start **25 mg** test → **50 mg daily** after confirmed opioid-free; XR-NTX 380 mg monthly preferred for adherence |
| **Sinclair / targeted** | **50 mg** ~**1 hour before** any drinking session; **not** on non-drinking days (protocol) |
| **LDN** | **1–4.5 mg** usually **nightly** (titrate 0.5–1.5 → 3–4.5) |
| **Contrave** | Escalating naltrexone **8–32 mg/day** with bupropion ER (fixed product schedule) |

## 4. Clinical evidence (full evaluation)

### 4.1 Alcohol use disorder (FDA indication)

| Tier | Finding | Certainty |
|------|---------|-----------|
| **Meta-analyses / guidelines** | Oral naltrexone reduces return to **heavy drinking** and drinking frequency; effect **moderate**. Jonas et al. JAMA 2014: NNT ≈ **12** for preventing return to heavy drinking. APA guidelines recommend for moderate–severe AUD. Cochrane (opioid antagonists for alcohol dependence): helps drink less / less often. | **Established** (direction); effect size **moderate** |
| **Mechanistic clinical** | Reduces alcohol’s rewarding effects via MOR block more than forcing continuous abstinence in all designs; often stronger on **heavy drinking days** than continuous abstinence rates alone. | **Probable** |
| **COMBINE / large trials** | Naltrexone among active arms improving outcomes with medical management; interaction with counseling type nuanced across literature. | **Probable** efficacy in structured care |
| **OPRM1** | Oslin 2003, Anton COMBINE genetics: Asp40 (G) carriers sometimes better response; later studies mixed/null — **not** ready as sole treatment-selection tool. | **Speculative–Probable** |
| **XR-NTX for AUD** | Monthly injection helps adherence; favorable in some comparative practice data. | **Probable** |
| **Sinclair Method** | Targeted pre-drinking naltrexone for extinction; Finnish/targeted-use literature and strong patient community; not identical to FDA label daily dosing; critics note selection/adherence and conflict with abstinence culture. Extinction claims (e.g. high % “success” in program data) need cautious interpretation. | Framework **Probable**; marketed success rates **Speculative** as universal |

**What AUD data do not show:** Miracle cure; works for everyone; replaces psychosocial care; safe to increase drinking under cover of the drug.

### 4.2 Opioid use disorder (FDA indication)

| Tier | Finding | Certainty |
|------|---------|-----------|
| **Pharmacology** | Full competitive block of opioid agonists when levels adequate. | **Established** |
| **Oral NTX** | Efficacy limited by **adherence**; meta retention/opioid-free signals mixed/nonsignificant in some pools; injectable superior for retention. | **Probable** adherence problem |
| **XR-NTX (Vivitrol)** | Krupitsky et al. pivotal-style data: much higher confirmed abstinence weeks vs placebo (e.g. median ~90% vs ~35% weeks), better retention, craving reduction after successful induction. Lee et al. criminal-justice XR-NTX reduced relapse vs usual treatment. | **Probable–Established** when induced |
| **XR-NTX vs buprenorphine** | Induction barrier: harder to start XR-NTX (need full detox); ITT often favors BUP-NX; **per-protocol** (successfully induced) similar effectiveness. | **Established** tradeoff |
| **Implants** | Some regions: better than oral for suppressing use; evidence quality variable; not US-standard. | **Probable** where available |

**Induction rule (non-negotiable):** Opioid-free typically **7–10 days** (longer after long-acting opioids like methadone ~**10–14 days**); confirm with history, UDS, often **naloxone challenge**.

### 4.3 Low-dose naltrexone (LDN, off-label)

| Tier | Finding | Certainty |
|------|---------|-----------|
| **Fibromyalgia** | Younger pilot + RCT signals; 2024 meta (4 RCTs, n=222): pain score MD ≈ **−0.86**, PPT improved; vivid dreams/nausea ↑; serious AEs not increased. Other reviews: low-grade evidence / mixed. | **Probable** modest pain benefit; QoL mixed |
| **Crohn’s, MS, other autoimmunity** | Small trials / observational signals; prescription-reduction cohorts (e.g. Norway) interesting but confounded. | **Speculative–Probable** condition-specific |
| **ME/CFS** | Case series / anecdote; trials lacking. | **Speculative** |
| **Mechanism** | TLR4/microglia and OGF rebound — mechanistically attractive, not fully proven in humans at 4.5 mg. | **Speculative** |
| **Dose** | Not interchangeable with 50 mg AUD dosing. | — |

### 4.4 Weight (Contrave)

Fixed naltrexone + bupropion ER: FDA-approved chronic weight management; effect is **combo**, not pure naltrexone 50 mg. **Probable** for product; do not extrapolate high-dose NTX alone as fat-loss drug.

### 4.5 Other

- **Cholestatic pruritus:** systematic support for naltrexone as option — **Probable** in refractory itch.  
- **Pediatric:** safety not established.  
- **Pregnancy:** category C historically; methadone/buprenorphine usually preferred for OUD in pregnancy.

### 4.6 Hepatotoxicity evidence

- High-dose obesity trials (~**300 mg/day**): clear ALT elevations vs placebo → dose-related hepatotoxin signal (**Established** at high dose).  
- Margin often described as only ~**5×** the 50 mg AUD/OUD dose.  
- At 50 mg: LFT elevations often similar to placebo in some studies; rare clinical hepatitis hard to attribute cleanly (LiverTox).  
- Contraindicated in **acute hepatitis / liver failure**; caution with advanced disease; baseline + periodic LFTs standard.

## 6. Desired effects — mechanism depth

### 6.1 Reduce heavy drinking (AUD)

- **Phenotype:** Fewer binge days, less “loss of control” after first drinks, reduced craving for some.
- **Pathways:** P1–P2, P4; extinction P5 if targeted.
- **Clinical:** Meta-analyses positive; NNT ~12 heavy drinking. **Certainty: Established** benefit class; individual response variable.
- **Practical:** 50 mg daily **or** TSM 50 mg 60 min pre-drink; pair with counseling/medical management.

### 6.2 Opioid blockade / relapse prevention (OUD)

- **Phenotype:** Heroin/oxycodone “don’t work”; lower use if adherent.
- **Pathways:** P1, P7 risk if stop.
- **Clinical:** XR-NTX strongest when induced. **Certainty: Established** pharmacology; **Probable** outcomes with XR-NTX.
- **Practical:** Full detox → challenge → 50 mg oral or 380 mg IM monthly + psychosocial care.

### 6.3 Pharmacological extinction (Sinclair)

- **Phenotype:** Over months, desire for alcohol fades if **never** drink without NTX; reinforce non-drinking pleasures off-drug.
- **Pathways:** P5 + P1–P2.
- **Certainty: Probable** mechanism; adherence-critical; not magic if pills skipped.

### 6.4 LDN pain / inflammatory symptom relief

- **Phenotype:** Lower FM pain, sometimes energy/sleep (dreams vivid).
- **Pathways:** P8–P9 (hypothesized).
- **Certainty: Probable** modest average pain effect in FM; **Speculative** broader autoimmunity.

### 6.5 Weight loss (only as Contrave)

- Combo POMC/reward dual mechanism. **Certainty: Probable** for approved product.

## 7. Side effects — mechanism depth + counters

### 7.1 Nausea, GI cramps, diarrhea, anorexia

1. **Phenotype:** Most common initiation AEs (often >10% in OUD/AUD trials for several GI/CNS items).
2. **Mechanism:** P13 opioid-tone GI disruption; central nausea.
3. **Counters**

| Node | Counter | Certainty |
|------|---------|-----------|
| GI irritation | Take with food; start 25 mg | **Probable** |
| Nausea | Temporary; antiemetic short-term if clinician OK | **Speculative** |
| Persistent | Switch to XR-NTX or reassess | **Probable** option |

### 7.2 Precipitated opioid withdrawal

1. **Phenotype:** Acute severe withdrawal if residual opioids present.
2. **Mechanism:** P6 sudden MOR occupancy by antagonist.
3. **Counters:** Strict opioid-free window; UDS; naloxone challenge; manage with non-opioids (clonidine, etc.) if induced. **Established** prevention protocol.

### 7.3 Headache, insomnia, anxiety, low energy / irritability

1. **Phenotype:** Common early CNS AEs.
2. **Mechanism:** MOR/KOR tone change; sleep disruption.
3. **Counters:** Dose timing; sleep hygiene; screen depression. **Probable**.

### 7.4 Dysphoria / anhedonia (full-dose daily)

1. **Phenotype:** “Life feels flat”; reduced pleasure from food/sex/exercise.
2. **Mechanism:** P15 partial endogenous opioid reward block.
3. **Counters:** Consider targeted TSM instead of daily if AUD and clinician agrees; don’t abandon all rewards. **Speculative–Probable**.

### 7.5 Depression / suicidality (rare but labeled)

1. **Phenotype:** Mood worsening, SI — monitor especially early.
2. **Mechanism:** Complex (substance use base rate + opioid system).
3. **Counters:** Screen; stop and refer psychiatry if SI. **Probable** monitoring need.

### 7.6 Hepatotoxicity / LFT elevation

1. **Phenotype:** Asymptomatic ALT ↑; rare clinical hepatitis discussion.
2. **Mechanism:** P12 dose-related hepatocellular stress at high dose.
3. **Counters**

| Node | Counter | Certainty |
|------|---------|-----------|
| Dose-related injury | Stay at approved doses; avoid 300 mg experimental | **Established** |
| Monitoring | Baseline + periodic LFTs | **Probable** standard |
| Acute hepatitis symptoms | Stop; urgent care | **Established** |
| LDN | Much lower mass dose; hepatic risk expected lower | **Speculative** absolute |

### 7.7 Injection-site reactions (XR-NTX)

1. **Phenotype:** Pain, induration, rare serious site injury if mis-injected.
2. **Counters:** Proper gluteal IM technique only. **Established**.

### 7.8 Vivid dreams (especially LDN)

1. **Phenotype:** Intense dreams; usually tolerable.
2. **Counters:** Morning dosing trial; dose reduce. **Probable**.

### 7.9 Overdose after stopping / trying to override

1. **Phenotype:** Fatal OD with high-potency opioids.
2. **Mechanism:** P7 lost tolerance + desperate dose escalation.
3. **Counters:** Patient education; never “breakthrough dose”; carry OD education/naloxone culture. **Established** risk counseling.

### 7.10 Acute pain / surgery conflict

1. **Phenotype:** Standard opioids ineffective under blockade.
2. **Counters:** Stop oral NTX ≥72 h pre-op (and XR-NTX ~30 days) per peri-op planning; regional/non-opioid strategies; team communication. **Established** planning need.

## 10. Monitoring, contraindications, stop criteria

### Monitoring

| Item | Why |
|------|-----|
| LFTs baseline + periodic | Hepatotoxicity caution |
| Depression / SI | Labeled risk |
| Adherence (oral) | Main failure mode |
| UDS / withdrawal scales | Induction safety |
| Injection site (XR) | Local injury |
| Drinking/opioid use logs | Efficacy |

### Contraindications (core)

- Current opioid dependence / acute withdrawal / failed naloxone challenge / positive opioid UDS when starting  
- Acute hepatitis or liver failure  
- Hypersensitivity  
- Need for ongoing opioid analgesia without plan  

### Stop / urgent care if

- Signs of acute hepatitis (jaundice, dark urine, RUQ pain)  
- Severe depression/SI  
- Accidental opioid use with severe withdrawal  
- Plans for emergency opioid analgesia — notify all clinicians

## 12. Open questions

1. Optimal TSM vs daily NTX head-to-head RCTs with modern endpoints.  
2. Reliable biomarkers (beyond inconsistent OPRM1) for NTX AUD responders.  
3. Large multi-center LDN trials in FM, Crohn’s, long COVID/ME with inflammatory markers.  
4. Safer rapid XR-NTX induction protocols (active research).  
5. Long-term anhedonia quantification at 50 mg daily.  
6. True incidence of severe DILI at standard doses in contemporary cohorts.

## 14. Short boundary

This briefing maps mechanisms and evidence for naltrexone HCl across approved and off-label uses. It is not a prescription or a substitute for addiction medicine, emergency care, or surgical planning. Starting naltrexone while opioids remain in the system can cause severe withdrawal. Overriding blockade with high-dose opioids can be fatal.
