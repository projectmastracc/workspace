## 2. Evidence Mapping (per claim)

### C1 — Partial AR agonist
- **Supports:** Kanno et al. 2011 (*Biol Pharm Bull*): ARE-luciferase partial agonism; nuclear translocation of AR. Peer-reviewed primary molecular pharmacology.  
- **Contradicts:** None direct; later papers accept this characterization.  
- **Study tier:** In vitro reporter / cell biology — **Very Low–Low** for clinical claims; moderate for molecular description within that system.  
- **Certainty:** **Probable** (in vitro partial agonism at AR). **Unknown** for human AR pharmacodynamics / efficacy.

### C2 — No N/C interaction; blocks DHT N/C interaction
- **Supports:** Kanno 2011 abstract and subsequent self-citations (Kanno 2013, Kanno 2022): YK11 activates AR without N/C interaction; prevents DHT-mediated N/C interaction. Mechanistic rationale for partial/gene-selective agonism.  
- **Contradicts:** No independent multi-lab replication of N/C assays identified in this pass.  
- **Certainty:** **Probable** for the described cell/mammalian two-hybrid-style systems from the discovering lab. **Speculative** as general truth across all tissues/cofactor contexts.

### C3 — Gene-selective AR agonist
- **Supports:** Kanno 2011: gene-selective agonist in MDA-MB-453; relative effect vs full agonist varies by gene. Kanno 2022: differential DNA-binding, cofactor recruitment, docking/mutational work (Gln902), distinct enhancer occupancy vs DHT.  
- **Contradicts:** Gene selectivity ≠ tissue selectivity. No comprehensive genome-wide human muscle ChIP-seq for YK11.  
- **Certainty:** **Probable** for *in vitro gene selectivity* in AR-positive breast cancer cell models. **Speculative** that this maps to a favorable human muscle vs prostate gene program.

### C4 — Tissue-selective SARM (muscle/bone ≫ androgenic tissues)
- **Supports:** Author/review labeling as SARM based on partial/gene-selective AR activity + myogenic/osteoblastic in vitro effects (Kanno 2011/2013; Yatsu 2018).  
- **Contradicts / gaps:** No robust published Hershberger-style levator ani vs seminal vesicle / prostate A/A ratios for YK11 comparable to clinical nonsteroidal SARM programs. Park 2022 notes mechanisms incompletely understood and no human trials. Steroidal scaffold; designer-drug use context.  
- **Certainty:** **Speculative** as a “true tissue-selective SARM” in the clinical sense. **Unknown** for human prostate vs muscle selectivity.

### C5 — Myogenic differentiation (C2C12)
- **Supports:** Kanno 2013 full text: MyHC protein ↑ with YK11 and DHT (500 nM, 7 d); Myf5/MyoD/myogenin mRNA; FLU attenuates MRF induction. n=3 technical biological replicates per figure set; Student’s t-test. Funding: MEXT / Open Research Center (academic).  
- **Limits:** Mouse myoblast line only; high nM concentrations; differentiation medium with horse serum; not in vivo hypertrophy.  
- **Certainty:** **Probable** that YK11 promotes C2C12 myogenic differentiation markers in vitro. **Speculative** for human muscle hypertrophy magnitude.

### C6–C7 — Follistatin induction (AR-dependent); required for Myf5 branch
- **Supports (Kanno 2013):**  
  - *Fst* mRNA ↑ with YK11 (not DHT) at Day 2/4.  
  - FLU and AR siRNA blunt *Fst* induction.  
  - Neutralizing anti-Fst antibody abolishes YK11-induced *Myf5* mRNA ↑.  
- **Observed:** Fst mRNA and functional dependence for *Myf5*.  
- **Inferred:** Fst mediates part of YK11’s myogenic program via extracellular antagonism of TGF-β family ligands (including myostatin/activins) — **mechanistic inference**, not direct measurement of MSTN–ActRIIB binding competition by YK11.  
- **Authors claim:** Fst induction is important for the anabolic effect of YK11.  
- **Certainty:** **Probable** that YK11 induces *Fst* in C2C12 via AR and that Fst is required for Myf5 upregulation in that assay. **Speculative** that this dominates whole-organism anabolism or human muscle.

### C8 — “Myostatin inhibitor”
- **Supports (narrow reading):** If “myostatin inhibitor” means *indirect functional antagonism of myostatin signaling via Fst upregulation*, then Kanno 2013 supports that pathway in C2C12. Lee 2021 frames YK11 as a myostatin inhibitor in a mouse sepsis model (abstract-level: myostatin protein and related pathway markers modulated; reduced cytokines/organ injury/mortality). Park 2022 review: “blocks myostatin by upregulating its antagonist, follistatin… mediated through the AR.”  
- **Contradicts (strict / marketing reading):** No evidence YK11 is a direct MSTN ligand, ActRIIB antagonist, or monoclonal-style myostatin blocker. Lee 2021 title language overshoots the established molecular mechanism.  
- **Certainty:**  
  - Indirect Fst-mediated pathway modulation in cells: **Probable**.  
  - Label “myostatin inhibitor” as primary MoA in humans: **Speculative**.  
  - Equivalence to clinical myostatin/ActRII pathway biologics: **Unknown** (no human data; different mechanism class).

### C9 — Direct MSTN receptor antagonist / binds myostatin
- **Supports:** None in peer-reviewed primary MoA papers.  
- **Contradicts:** Kanno 2013 pathway is AR → *Fst* → (inferred) ligand sequestration of myostatin/activins.  
- **Certainty:** **Unknown** / effectively **unsupported** — recommend against this claim (**Established** that literature does not show direct MSTN binding).

### C10 — Human anabolic efficacy / gym doses
- **Supports:** None (no RCTs, no PK/PD, no dose-finding).  
- **Contradicts:** Regulatory status as unauthorized SARM product; absence of clinical literature.  
- **Certainty:** **Unknown**. Community dose anecdotes are not evidence.

### C11 — Osteoblast / bone anabolism
- **Supports:** Yatsu et al. 2018: MC3T3-E1 proliferation, mineralization, OPG/OCN; AR antagonist attenuates; p-Akt non-genomic signaling suggested. Wang 2025: BMSC osteogenesis AR-dependent; rat cranial defect model (abstract-level).  
- **Certainty:** **Speculative–Probable** for in vitro osteogenic markers; **Speculative** for clinical bone benefit.

### C12 — Sepsis muscle wasting / systemic benefit (mouse)
- **Supports:** Lee et al. 2021 (*BBRC*) abstract: gram-negative sepsis mouse model; framed as myostatin inhibitor; pathway markers (Fst, NF-κB, MRFs, p-FOXO3a, p-Smad2); reduced inflammatory/organ markers and mortality. Authors declare no COI.  
- **Limits:** Full methods/doses not retrieved this pass; acute disease model ≠ bodybuilding anabolism; single paper.  
- **Certainty:** **Speculative** for sepsis benefit; **does not establish** human myostatin blockade for hypertrophy.

### C13 — Organ / safety signals (rodent)
- **Supports:** Dahleh et al. 2024 and related work: prostate, kidney, liver focus with YK11 ± resistance exercise; other lines report hippocampal oxidative stress / mitochondrial dysfunction signals. Abstracts frame YK11 as AR agonist + myostatin-inhibition narrative.  
- **Certainty:** **Speculative–Probable** that YK11 is not organ-inert in rodents at experimental exposures. **Unknown** human organ safety profile (no trials).

### C14 — Regulatory risk framing
- **Supports:** Health Canada: SARMs unauthorized; bodybuilding products with SARMs (including YK-11 / myostine) linked to class risk language (heart attack, stroke, liver damage); long-term effects unknown. Repeated product seizures/advisories.  
- **Certainty:** **Established** that YK11 is not an approved therapeutic and is treated as unauthorized SARM-class product by regulators. Specific event rates in humans for pure YK11: **Unknown**.

## 3. Steelmanned Opposition (contested claims)

### Contested claim A: “YK11 is a true myostatin inhibitor”
**Steelman pro:** Follistatin is a validated endogenous antagonist of myostatin and activins. If YK11 robustly raises Fst in muscle via AR, functional myostatin pathway tone should fall. Kanno 2013 shows Fst-dependence of a key MRF. Lee 2021 and Park 2022 use “myostatin inhibitor” as a convenient pathway-level label. For PED users, pathway-level functional inhibition may matter more than ligand class.  

**Steelman con:** Clinical “myostatin inhibitors” (mAbs, ActRII traps) act *directly* on the ligand or receptor with measured MSTN pathway suppression in humans. YK11’s only strong primary evidence is *Fst mRNA* in a mouse cell line, not reduced free myostatin, not ActRII occupancy, not human biopsy p-Smad. DHT failed to induce Fst in C2C12 while still inducing MyHC — so Fst is not the only AR myogenic route, and YK11’s uniqueness may be model-specific. Calling YK11 a myostatin inhibitor **misclassifies mechanism** and invites unsafe stacking with true MSTN pathway agents.  

**Adjudication:** Prefer precise language: **AR partial agonist that can induce follistatin and thereby may secondarily antagonize myostatin/activin signaling in vitro**. “Myostatin inhibitor” as primary identity is **marketing overreach** (**Speculative** at best for humans).

### Contested claim B: “Gene-selective SARM = safe tissue-selective anabolic”
**Steelman pro:** Missing N/C interaction and differential cofactor recruitment (Kanno 2011/2022) is a legitimate molecular basis for gene selectivity; nonsteroidal SARM literature supports coactivator/corepressor tissue hypotheses. Osteoblast and myoblast data suggest anabolic-leaning activity.  

**Steelman con:** Gene selectivity in MDA-MB-453 does not prove levator ani ≫ prostate in vivo. Steroidal SARMs can still suppress HPG, affect lipids, and hit liver/kidney. Park 2022 notes YK11 may **interfere with full AR activation by other ligands** (N/C blockade) — a stacking liability, not a pure safety feature. Zero human safety/efficacy trials.  

**Adjudication:** Gene selectivity is **Probable** in vitro; tissue-selective clinical SARM profile is **Unknown/Speculative**. Do not equate the two.

### Contested claim C: “Stronger myogenic than DHT in vitro ⇒ superior human anabolic”
**Steelman pro:** Higher Myf5/myogenin at Day 4 vs DHT and unique Fst arm could mean greater differentiation drive.  

**Steelman con:** C2C12 differentiation is not adult human muscle hypertrophy. Concentrations (100–500 nM) and culture conditions do not establish oral human free-drug exposure. DHT’s myogenic path can be Fst-independent in this model — so “more potent” is endpoint- and time-dependent.  

**Adjudication:** In vitro superiority on selected MRFs is **Probable** within Kanno 2013’s conditions; human superiority is **Unknown**.

## 5. What Research Truly Says (plain-language synthesis)

1. **Molecular AR pharmacology (best supported):** YK11 is a **steroidal partial AR agonist** that promotes nuclear AR translocation, activates ARE-driven transcription only partially relative to DHT, **fails to induce N/C interaction**, and can **interfere with DHT-driven N/C interaction**. In AR-positive human breast cancer cells it is **gene-selective** versus a full agonist. (**Probable**, in vitro.)

2. **Muscle cell biology:** In mouse C2C12 myoblasts, YK11 promotes differentiation markers (MyHC, MRFs) in an AR-dependent manner. Unlike DHT in the same model, it **upregulates follistatin mRNA**; neutralizing Fst blocks the Myf5 response. Authors conclude Fst induction is important for YK11’s anabolic-like effect in this system. (**Probable** for C2C12; **Speculative** for human muscle.)

3. **Myostatin:** The rigorous reading is **indirect pathway modulation via Fst**, not direct myostatin enzyme/receptor pharmacology. Fst can sequester myostatin and related TGF-β ligands; that is a plausible downstream consequence, not a demonstrated primary drug–target interaction. Marketing that presents YK11 as a dedicated myostatin blocker **exceeds the data**.

4. **Selectivity:** Evidence supports **gene selectivity** more than **tissue selectivity**. Classic nonsteroidal SARM tissue-selectivity packages (Hershberger + clinical PSA/lipids) are largely **missing** for YK11.

5. **Bone:** Additional in vitro (and limited in vivo rodent) work suggests AR-dependent osteogenic effects (**Speculative–Probable** preclinical).

6. **Humans:** There are **no controlled human efficacy or safety trials**. Designer-drug / anti-doping detections exist; that is exposure evidence, not efficacy. Regulatory agencies treat YK11-containing products as unauthorized SARMs with serious class risk warnings.

**Bottom line:** Literature supports YK11 as a **gene-selective AR partial agonist that can induce follistatin and thereby may secondarily dampen myostatin/activin signaling in muscle cells**. It does **not** support equating YK11 with clinical myostatin inhibitors or with proven human anabolic drugs.

## One-Paragraph Research Answer

Peer-reviewed literature characterizes YK11 as a **steroidal partial androgen-receptor agonist** that **avoids N/C interaction**, shows **gene-selective** AR activity in cell models, and in **C2C12 myoblasts** drives myogenic differentiation partly through **AR-dependent follistatin induction**—an **indirect** route that can antagonize myostatin/activin signaling without YK11 being a direct myostatin ligand or receptor blocker (**Probable** in vitro; **Speculative** in vivo; **Unknown** in humans). Claims of clinical **tissue selectivity**, **human myostatin blockade**, and **gym-dose anabolism** are **not supported** by controlled human data; regulators treat YK11 products as unauthorized SARMs. **DR:** do not recommend human dosing; recommend against marketing that overstates human myostatin inhibition; harm reduction is class-level only.
