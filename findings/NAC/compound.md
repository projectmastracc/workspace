## 1. Compound Identity

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Common names** | N-acetylcysteine, NAC, N-acetyl-L-cysteine, acetylcysteine |
| **Chemical identity** | N-acetyl derivative of L-cysteine; CAS 616-91-1; C₅H₉NO₃S |
| **Drug class** | Amino acid derivative; glutathione (GSH) precursor; mucolytic (disulfide-reducing agent); acetaminophen antidote |
| **Regulatory (US)** | **FDA-approved drug** for (1) acetaminophen (APAP) overdose and (2) mucolytic use (inhalation/oral medical product). Also widely sold as a **dietary supplement**. FDA has stated NAC is excluded from the statutory “dietary supplement” definition because it was approved as a drug before substantial DS marketing (FD&C Act); agency has exercised **enforcement discretion** for certain NAC-containing products while considering rulemaking to permit DS use. Status: widely available OTC as “supplement”; prescription/OTC drug products also exist (Acetadote® IV, Mucomyst®, etc.). |
| **Taxonomy** | Multi-class: **nutrition** (amino_acid) + **neuropharmacology** (neuromodulator / glutamatergic adjunct) + medical antidote/mucolytic. Performance lens secondary only. |

**Primary sources:** StatPearls NBK537183 (PMID 30725868); FDA enforcement discretion guidance on NAC products; Tenório et al. 2021 DOI [10.3390/antiox10060967](https://doi.org/10.3390/antiox10060967) (PMID 34208683).

## 3. Mechanism & Pharmacology

### Core mechanisms

1. **GSH precursor (**Established**)**  
   NAC supplies L-cysteine, the rate-limiting substrate for γ-glutamylcysteine ligase → glutathione synthesis. In APAP overdose, this repletes hepatic GSH that detoxifies NAPQI (N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone imine). NAC also directly conjugates toxic metabolites and scavenges free radicals.  
   Source: StatPearls NBK537183; Heard 2008 PMC2637612.

2. **Mucolytic / disulfide breaking (**Established**)**  
   Free thiol reduces disulfide bonds in mucus glycoproteins → lower viscosity of airway secretions. Relevant to inhalation and high-dose oral mucolytic use.  
   Source: Aldini et al. 2018 DOI [10.1080/10715762.2018.1468564](https://doi.org/10.1080/10715762.2018.1468564); StatPearls.

3. **Glutamate modulation via system xc⁻ (**Probable** for CNS effects)**  
   Extracellular cystine (from NAC → cysteine oxidation) exchanges for intracellular glutamate via the cystine–glutamate antiporter. Increases extrasynaptic glutamate → mGluR2/3 activation → reduced synaptic glutamate release. Basis for psychiatric/addiction research (OCD, schizophrenia negative symptoms, craving).  
   Source: Dean et al. 2011 *J Psychiatry Neurosci*; Bradlow et al. 2022 DOI [10.1007/s40263-022-00907-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40263-022-00907-3); Eghdami 2024 DOI [10.3389/fpsyt.2024.1421150](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2024.1421150).

4. **Redox / H₂S–sulfane sulfur (**Speculative** for “fast antioxidant” claims)**  
   Some work proposes rapid antioxidant action via sulfane sulfur / H₂S-related pathways beyond classic GSH elevation (Ezeriņa et al. 2018 PMID 29429900). Clinical relevance of this pathway remains incompletely mapped.

5. **Anti-inflammatory / Nrf2 (**Probable** mechanistically; outcome-dependent)**  
   Redox-sensitive pathway modulation (including Nrf2) summarized in Tenório 2021; clinical translation is indication-specific.

### Pharmacokinetics (oral / IV)

| Parameter | Approximate values | Certainty |
|-----------|-------------------|-----------|
| Oral bioavailability | ~4–10% (extensive first-pass; deacetylation) | **Established** |
| Tmax (oral) | ~1–2 h | **Established** |
| t½ | ~5.6 h (IV single dose); oral ~6 h classically; longer terminal phases reported (~15–19 h multiphasic) | **Established** (formulation-dependent) |
| Protein binding | ~66–87% | **Established** |
| Metabolism | Deacetylation → cysteine; disulfide conjugates; incorporation into GSH; urinary sulfur metabolites | **Established** |
| Elimination | Renal clearance of parent small fraction; extensive metabolic clearance | **Established** |

Sources: Olsson et al. 1988 (bioavailability ~9%); StatPearls; Papi et al. 2020 PMC7854405; Texas HHS NAC monograph.

**Analyst inference:** Low oral bioavailability means oral effects depend heavily on first-pass cysteine/GSH delivery and tissue uptake—not high systemic free NAC levels. Do not equate oral mg doses with IV APAP-protocol mg/kg exposure.

## 4. Documented Effects

Certainty labels per project evidence-grading rules.

### Medical — established / probable

| Effect | Population | Certainty | Notes / sources |
|--------|------------|-----------|-----------------|
| **APAP overdose antidote** — prevents/treats hepatotoxicity when given early | Acute APAP poisoning | **Established** | Near-complete protection if started ≤8 h post-ingestion; Rumack–Matthew nomogram guides treatment. FDA-approved. Oral 72-h or IV 21-h (or simplified 2-bag) regimens. PMID 30725868; Heard 2008; Dart et al. 2023 JAMA Netw Open consensus. |
| **Mucolytic (inhaled)** — thins secretions | CF, bronchitis, thick mucus | **Established** (label use) | FDA-approved indication for viscid secretions. |
| **Oral mucolytic / COPD exacerbation reduction** | Chronic bronchitis / COPD | **Probable** | Cazzola et al. 2015 meta-analysis (13 trials, n≈4155): RR of exacerbations ~0.75; signal stronger with **≥1200 mg/day** in spirometrically confirmed COPD; 600 mg/day may suffice in chronic bronchitis without obstruction. GOLD/ACCP: selective/conditional use. Huang 2023 MA more null on some endpoints—evidence not uniform. DOI/ERS: Cazzola *Eur Respir Rev* 2015; Chest ACCP 2015 COPD exacerbation prevention guideline. |
| **Contrast-induced nephropathy (CIN) prevention** | At-risk imaging patients | **Unknown** → no recommendation for routine use | Mixed/null large trials; many modern practices de-emphasize routine NAC; hydration remains core. |

| Effect | Certainty | Notes / sources |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Schizophrenia adjunct** — negative / total PANSS improvement with longer treatment (~24 wk) | **Probable** (modest) | 2020 MA: no clear effect at 8 wk; significant total/negative PANSS advantage at 24 wk (1000–3600 mg/day). | Effect | Certainty | Notes |
|--------|-----------|-------|

## 5. Documented Risks & Contraindications

### Adverse effects

| Route / context | AEs | Certainty |
|-----------------|-----|-----------|
| **Oral (supplement / mucolytic doses)** | Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, flatulence, GERD; sulfur odor; poor palatability of liquid | **Established** common GI AEs |
| **IV (medical)** | Rate-related **anaphylactoid** reactions up to ~18% (mostly mild–moderate; severe bronchospasm/hypotension ~1%); flushing, erythema; spurious INR elevation; false-positive urine ketones | **Established** |
| **Inhaled** | Bronchospasm risk—especially asthma/reactive airway disease; use bronchodilator pre-treatment per respiratory practice | **Established** caution |
| **Massive iatrogenic IV overdose** | Hemolysis, thrombocytopenia, acidosis, renal failure, cerebral edema/seizures (case reports) | **Established** toxicity at massive dosing errors |

Sources: StatPearls NBK537183; Yarema et al. 2018 PMID 29423816 (anaphylactoid); Kerr et al. 2005.

### Contraindications / cautions

- **Hypersensitivity** to acetylcysteine — **do not use** (**Established**).
- **Asthma / bronchospasm history** — caution especially IV/inhaled; monitor (**Established**).
- **Fluid overload risk** (CHF, etc.) — adjust IV diluent volume (**Established** clinical practice).
- **Active peptic ulcer / esophageal varices** — oral liquid may increase GI bleeding concern (**Probable** caution).
- **Pregnancy** — used when indicated for APAP OD (benefit >> theoretical risk); Category B historical framing (**Established** medical use when needed).

### Interactions

| Interaction | Clinical note | Certainty |
|-------------|----------------|-----------|
| **Nitroglycerin** | Potentiates vasodilatory effects → **hypotension** and **severe headache** | **Established** (moderate interaction) PMID 1505148; StatPearls |
| **Activated charcoal (APAP OD context)** | Charcoal binds oral NAC *and* APAP; APAP adsorption clinically more important. **Early NAC takes priority** over charcoal if tradeoff; oral NAC may be staggered if multi-dose charcoal needed for co-ingestants | **Established** toxicology practice |
| **Carbamazepine** | May lower carbamazepine levels | **Probable** |
| **Metals / metal-binding** | Thiols can chelate/complex metals; separate from multi-mineral supplements by hours is common practical advice (**Speculative** for clinical harm at standard oral doses; mechanistic plausibility) |
| **Heavy metal chelation protocols** | Marketing claims of “chelation with oral NAC” for general detox — **Unknown** / not evidence-based medical chelation |

### Long-term safety gaps

- Decades of mucolytic and supplement use support **generally good tolerability** at 600–2400 mg/day oral (**Probable**).
- High-dose psychiatric ranges (2000–3000+ mg/day) for many months: limited large long-term safety datasets beyond trial AEs (**Probable** short-term safety; long-term **Unknown** for rare events).
- Chronic high-dose antioxidant interference with exercise adaptation: **Speculative**.

### 8.2 Dosing

#### A. Medical APAP overdose — HOSPITAL PROTOCOLS ONLY

**Do NOT invent or self-administer DIY overdose doses.** Weight-based regimens require serum APAP levels, Rumack–Matthew nomogram interpretation, LFTs/INR monitoring, and capacity to manage anaphylactoid reactions.

**Reference regimens (for education only — clinician-administered):**

| Route | Protocol (label/classic) | Notes |
|-------|--------------------------|-------|
| **Oral (72-h)** | Loading **140 mg/kg** once, then **70 mg/kg** every 4 h × 17 doses (total ~1330 mg/kg) | Dilute; re-dose if vomited; unpleasant taste |
| **IV (21-h three-bag, Acetadote-type)** | **150 mg/kg** over 1 h → **50 mg/kg** over 4 h → **100 mg/kg** over 16 h (total **300 mg/kg**) | Anaphylactoid risk; fluid load |
| **IV two-bag alternatives** | Institutional simplified regimens also used | Per local toxicology protocol |

Sources: FDA Acetadote label; StatPearls NBK537183; Heard 2008 NEJM/PMC2637612; Dart 2023 consensus.

**If APAP overdose is suspected:** call emergency services / poison control immediately. Early NAC (ideally ≤8 h) is critical (**Established**).

#### B. Mucolytic oral regimens (respiratory literature)

| Indication pattern | Typical oral range | Certainty |
|--------------------|--------------------|-----------|
| Chronic bronchitis (without clear obstruction) | Often **600 mg/day** | **Probable** (Cazzola 2015 interpretation) |
| COPD with obstruction / exacerbation prevention | Often **≥1200 mg/day** (e.g., 600 mg BID or 600 mg TID) | **Probable** at adequate dose; some MAs mixed |
| Upper range in studies | **1200–1800+ mg/day** | Trial-dependent |

- Duration in trials: weeks to months of continuous use for exacerbation endpoints.
- Inhaled NAC: medical product dosing per label (nebulized 10–20% solutions)—not the same as swallowing supplement capsules.

Sources: Cazzola et al. 2015 *Eur Respir Rev*; ACCP/CTS 2015 Chest guideline; Huang 2023 *Ther Adv Respir Dis* (more null—acknowledge uncertainty).

**DR:** For frequent-exacerbation COPD under respiratory care, literature supports trying adequate-dose oral NAC (**Probable**); do not expect FEV₁ reverse-remodeling.

#### C. Psychiatric trial ranges (adjunct)

| Condition | Typical trial dosing | Division | Certainty for use |
|-----------|----------------------|----------|-------------------|
| OCD (SSRI adjunct) | Trials used **2000–3000+ mg/day** (some up to 4000 mg) | Often **600–1000 mg BID–TID** if trialed | **Speculative** — not a reliable adult benefit; optional low-stakes only after failed standard care; expect small/null effect |
| Schizophrenia (antipsychotic adjunct) | **1000–3600 mg/day** (often ~**2000 mg/day**) | Divided BID–TID; benefits often after **≥8–24 weeks** | **Probable** modest (negative/total symptoms with duration) |
| Addiction / OCRD exploratory | Often **1200–2400+ mg/day** | Divided | **Unknown** universal; **Speculative** by substance only |

Sources: Eghdami 2024 DOI 10.3389/fpsyt.2024.1421150; Sarris 2022 PMID 35304155; Bradlow 2022 DOI 10.1007/s40263-022-00907-3; Oliver 2015; Deepmala 2015 PMID 25957927.

**DR:**  
- **Recommend against** NAC monotherapy for moderate–severe OCD or schizophrenia.  
- **SCZ:** Reasonable to discuss as adjunct at **~2000 mg/day divided** with psychiatrist if residual negative symptoms (**Probable** modest).  
- **Adult OCD:** Do **not** promise benefit; optional low-stakes adjunct only after SSRI ± ERP incomplete (**Speculative**); if tried, trial-range divided dosing and reassess by 8–12 weeks — stop if no meaningful Y-BOCS change.

#### D. General supplement / antioxidant self-use

| Goal | Dose band often used | Certainty |
|------|----------------------|-----------|
| General wellness GSH support | **600–1200 mg/day** | **Unknown** clinical outcome benefit in healthy people |
| Higher “research mimic” without psych indication | **1200–1800 mg/day** divided | Tolerability **Probable**; benefit **Unknown** |

**No recommendation** for high-dose chronic use solely for unproven longevity claims (**Unknown**).

### 8.3 Timing & administration

- **With food:** Preferred if GI upset occurs (nausea/diarrhea) — **Probable** practical harm reduction; food may slightly alter absorption but improves adherence.
- **Divided doses:** BID–TID for ≥1200 mg/day — matches psych/mucolytic trials and reduces single-dose GI load (**Probable**).
- **Sulfur smell/taste:** Common; effervescent or enteric-coated products sometimes better tolerated (**Speculative** product-dependent).
- **Evening vs morning:** No strong circadian requirement (**Unknown**); split for convenience.
- **Do not crush assumptions about “empty stomach for max effect”** as mandatory — GI tolerance usually wins.

### 8.7 Who should not use

- Known **hypersensitivity** to N-acetylcysteine (**Established** contraindication).
- Unsupervised use during **suspected APAP overdose** (go to hospital) (**Established**).
- Uncontrolled severe **asthma** without clinical supervision for inhaled/IV routes (**Established** caution).
- Concurrent **nitroglycerin** without clinician awareness (**Established** interaction).
- Pregnant individuals using high-dose “wellness” stacks without indication — APAP-OD treatment is different and may be indicated (**context-specific**).
- Anyone expecting NAC to replace evidence-based treatment for OCD, psychosis, COPD, or addiction (**recommend against**).

## 9. Compound Context Summary

N-acetylcysteine is a cysteine prodrug used as the **established** antidote for acetaminophen overdose and as a **mucolytic**, with **probable** benefit for COPD/chronic bronchitis exacerbations at adequate oral doses (often 600–1200+ mg/day). As a neuropharmacologic adjunct it modestly helps some schizophrenia negative symptoms (~**2000 mg/day**, longer duration)—never first-line monotherapy. Adult OCD signal is fragile after Sarris 2022/AIS; optional low-stakes adjunct only (**Speculative**). Oral use is usually well tolerated aside from GI upset; IV use risks anaphylactoid reactions and requires medical settings. Avoid nitroglycerin combinations; do not DIY overdose protocols. GlyNAC anti-aging: **no recommendation** (**Speculative** biomarkers; **Unknown** healthspan/lifespan). Supplement quality varies; hypersensitivity is a hard stop. **Unknown** endpoints (universal detox, longevity, CIN routine use, addiction cure) get **no recommendation**.
