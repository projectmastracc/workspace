## 2. Chemistry, Classification, Pharmacokinetics

### 2.1 Identity and structure

| Parameter | Value | Certainty |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Common name | Androsterone (ADT) | **Established** |
| Systematic | 5α-androstan-3α-ol-17-one / 3α-hydroxy-5α-androstan-17-one | **Established** |
| CAS | 53-41-8 | **Established** |
| Formula / MW | C₁₉H₃₀O₂ / 290.44 g·mol⁻¹ | **Established** |
| Class | Endogenous C19 steroid; weak androgen; androstane neurosteroid; backdoor intermediate; major conjugated urinary metabolite | **Established** |
| Key stereochemistry | **5α** A-ring reduction + **3α**-OH + 17-ketone | **Established** — both 5α and 3α required for full GABA SAR |
| Epimers | Epiandrosterone (3β); etiocholanolone (5β); epietiocholanolone (3β,5β) | **Established** |
| Natural occurrence | Human serum/urine/CSF/brain; axilla/skin; pine pollen, celery, truffles (trace plant/food literature) | **Established** human; plant **Probable** |

**SAR rule (GABA_A path — Established, Kaminski 2005):**  
3α-OH essential → epiandrosterone inactive. 5α preferred over 5β → etiocholanolone less potent. Glucuronide conjugate inactive at 300 mg/kg IP mice. Unnatural **ent**-androsterone more potent than natural (Zolkowska 2014) — reinforces stereospecific receptor pharmacology; does not imply natural ADT is uniquely optimized for supplementation.

**AR path:** Weak agonist relative to T/DHT. Classic encyclopedia/secondary potency ≈ **1/7 of testosterone** (**Probable** historical; some sources ~1/10). Low AR affinity relative to GABA_A relevance is a recurring framing in sexual-neurosteroid discussion (Bloch context).

### 2.2 Biosynthesis (forward / classic)

```
Cholesterol → pregnenolone → ... → DHEA / androstenedione / testosterone
        │
        ├─ 5α-reductase → DHT → 3α-HSD → 3α-androstanediol → 17β-HSD → ANDROSTERONE
        │
        └─ androstenedione → 5α-reductase → 5α-androstanedione → 3α-HSD → ANDROSTERONE
```

Parallel 5β route yields **etiocholanolone**.

**Enzyme detail (Established):** T → DHT via SRD5A1/2; DHT → 3α-diol via AKR1C2 (and related); 3α-diol → ADT via 17β-HSD oxidation. Parallel: DHT → 5α-androstanedione → ADT.

**Certainty:** Pathway map **Established** steroid biochemistry.

### 2.3 Backdoor pathway (critical for Peat/androgen framing)

Classic path: T → DHT.  
**Backdoor path:** C21 steroids (progesterone / 17-OHP) → 5α-reduced intermediates → androsterone / androstanediol nodes → DHT **without** requiring T as intermediate.

**O'Shaughnessy et al. PLoS Biol 2019 (Established fetal human data):**
- Androsterone is the **principal backdoor androgen** in male fetal circulation.
- Circulating DHT largely **undetectable** (<1 ng/mL) in their fetal plasma dataset while ADT is present and sex-dependent (male > female).
- Backdoor intermediates concentrated in **placenta, fetal liver, fetal adrenal** — little de novo testicular backdoor synthesis.
- Placental **progesterone** is the key feedstock substrate.
- Implication: masculinization is multi-tissue / multi-pathway, not testis-T alone.

**Adult exogenous implication:** Tissue-selective oxidative 3α-HSD (e.g. HSD17B6 / RoDH-like and related) + 17β-HSD can reconvert ADT-family steroids toward DHT/androstanedione in prostate and other tissues (**Probable** enzyme existence from prostate literature). **Magnitude of systemic DHT rise from Idealabs-style 1–5 mg topical ADT in adults = Unknown.** Do not equate fetal centrality with adult re-androgenization potency. Fetal developmental enzyme topology ≠ adult male skin/liver/prostate/brain after microdose topical ADT.

**Allometric / concentration gap (methods-critical):**
- Kaminski ED50s are **tens to hundreds of mg/kg IP in mice** — for a 70 kg human, linear mg/kg scaling → grams-class systemic exposure.
- Community dose is **1–5 mg total topical** (~0.014–0.07 mg/kg *applied*, unknown fraction absorbed).
- Even generous 100% absorption is **orders of magnitude** below anticonvulsant ED50s.
- Slice effects at **10–100 μM** vs cited human unconjugated ADT ~**3–5 nM** → ~2,000–30,000× gap.
- Do **not** dose-convert mouse anticonvulsant mg/kg to human drop counts. Mouse data prove **mechanism class** and SAR, not Idealabs dosing.

### 2.7 History

Isolated 1931 by Butenandt & Tscherning from large volumes of male urine (~17,000 L → ~50 mg crystalline) — first identified androgen before testosterone’s full clinical era. Chemically synthesized shortly after. Modern interest splits three ways: (1) conjugated androgen diagnostics (Labrie ADT-G), (2) neurosteroid epilepsy research (Rogawski/Kaminski line), (3) Peat/Idealabs micro-androgen experiments.

### 4.2 Kaminski RM et al., *Epilepsia* 2005 — anticonvulsant profile (**Established** preclinical)

**Design:** Male NIH Swiss mice; IP androsterone / etiocholanolone / epiandrosterone / ADT-glucuronide; electrical and chemoconvulsant models; rat hippocampal slice 4-AP discharges. Funding: NIH/NINDS Epilepsy Research Section (Rogawski intramural) — low commercial COI.

**ED50s for androsterone (5α,3α-A), mg/kg IP:**

| Model | ED50 (mg/kg) | Protective index (TD50/ED50) |
|-------|--------------|------------------------------|
| 6 Hz electrical | **29.1** (16.2–52.1) | **5.23** |
| PTZ | **43.5** (31.6–60.0) | **3.50** |
| Pilocarpine | **105** (47.7–232.7) | **1.44** |
| 4-AP | **215** (173.8–266.6) | **0.70** |
| MES | **224** (182.5–274.1) | **0.68** |

- **TD50** motor (inverted screen): **~152 mg/kg**.
- Etiocholanolone less potent (6 Hz ED50 76.9; PTZ 139).
- Epiandrosterone inactive (>300 mg/kg).
- Androsterone glucuronide inactive 300 mg/kg.
- In vitro: 10–100 μM inhibits CA3 4-AP discharges concentration-dependently.
- Time course: peak protection ~**15 min** post-IP at 100 mg/kg in 6 Hz; minimal by 2 h.
- Serum unconjugated ADT cited ~3–5 nM; urine conjugates ~3–4 mg/24 h young men.
- Paper notes T ~10× more androgenic and inactive in seizure models in their hands.

**What the design can support:** Natural androsterone is an active anticonvulsant neurosteroid in rodents when CNS-accessible concentrations are achieved; activity tracks 3α-reduced A-ring SAR shared with allopregnanolone-class PAMs. Micromolar free concentrations modulate hippocampal network excitability.

**What the design cannot support:**
1. **Route mismatch** — IP bypasses skin barrier, first-pass UGT, cutaneous metabolism. Idealabs use is topical mg.
2. **Dose scale** — ED50s orders of magnitude above 1–5 mg topical human.
3. **Concentration gap** — 10–100 μM slice vs ~3–5 nM free serum physiology.
4. **Endpoint mismatch** — seizure protection ≠ libido, warmth, dry androgen feel, training recovery.
5. **Vehicle confounds** — cyclodextrin IP ≠ oil/alcohol topical free-fraction curve.

**Interpretation for users:** Proves ADT is a real neurosteroid anticonvulsant with classical 3α SAR. Does **not** validate 1–5 mg topical for anxiety/sleep. MES/4-AP need near-toxic doses (PI <1) — not a clean “high therapeutic index wonder steroid.”

**Related:** Zolkowska 2014 — **ent**-androsterone more potent than natural. Tutka et al. — androsterone can elevate MES threshold and enhance some AEDs (carbamazepine, gabapentin, phenobarbital) in mice at research doses (**Probable** preclinical interaction class; **not** a DIY epilepsy stack).

### 4.3 Bloch M et al., *Arch Gen Psychiatry* 2006 — CSF and sexual function (**Probable** human correlational)

**Design:** Healthy men; pharmacologically induced hypogonadism ± testosterone replacement; CSF steroids and monoamines vs sexual interest symptoms. Funding: NIH/NIMH clinical endocrinology–psychiatry (Schmidt/Rubinow lineage) — low product COI.

**Key findings:**
- CSF testosterone, DHT, and **androsterone** lower in hypogonadism vs T-replaced state.
- Decreased sexual interest in hypogonadal state.
- **CSF androsterone alone** correlated with decreased libido during hypogonadism **and** during T replacement (reported *r* ≈ −0.76 and −0.81).
- Change in sexual interest severity correlated with change in CSF androsterone (*r* ≈ −0.68).
- CSF T/DHT did **not** show the same libido correlation pattern; monoamines (5-HIAA, HVA) not correlated.
- Authors propose ADT as GABA_A-linked mediator of androgen effects on libido (low AR affinity context).

**What the design can support:** Under androgen deprivation, CSF ADT covaries with sexual-function change more tightly than CSF T/DHT in that sample — hypothesis-generating for **endogenous neurosteroid mediation** (**Probable** biomarker/mediator candidate).

**What the design cannot support:**
1. **Association ≠ exogenous causation** — study does not administer androsterone.
2. **Confounded axis collapse** — induced hypogonadism collapses T, DHT, E2, 3α-diol, allopregnanolone precursors together; CSF ADT may be a **sensitive reporter** of upstream androgen flux rather than the sole causal effector.
3. **State specificity** — pathologic/induced low-androgen state; external validity to eugonadal gym users or TRT users is weak (ceiling effects).
4. **No dose–response, no PK, no free-fraction CNS after exogenous ADT** — exogenous ADT may be conjugated, fail to raise free CSF ADT, or raise ADT-G without PAM-active free steroid.

**Certainty:** Association **Probable**. Causal “take ADT drops for libido” **Speculative**.

### 4.4 O'Shaughnessy PJ et al., *PLoS Biol* 2019 — fetal backdoor (**Established**)

- Androsterone principal backdoor androgen in human male fetal circulation; DHT largely undetectable in plasma in their measurements.
- Sex-dependent titres (male > female); n≈42 male, 16 female second-trimester fetuses.
- Synthesis multi-tissue (placenta, liver, adrenal); placental progesterone substrate.
- Funding: public UK/EU/SE; declared no competing interests.
- Reframes ADT from “inactive waste” to developmental androgen node.

**User relevance:** Explains Peat/Idealabs interest in ADT as “meaningful androgen intermediate.” Does **not** prove adult topical microdosing recreates fetal backdoor flux or increases adult DHT in plasma/tissue to clinically meaningful levels (**Unknown**). Fetal masculinization biology must not invent fetal-level risk from microgram–milligram adult topical use without adult data — wrong population and risk frame.

### 4.5 Labrie / ADT-G diagnostic literature (**Probable** marker; zero intervention efficacy)

- ADT-G major circulating C19 metabolite; used as marker of total androgenic activity / intracrine androgen metabolism, especially when serum T is an incomplete proxy (women, aging men contexts in Labrie framing).
- Male ADT-G reference example ~56–377 nmol/L (Adaway 2010 assay-specific).
- Decline of conjugated C19 steroids with age documented (Labrie 1997 JCEM cluster).

**User relevance:** Blood ADT-G can rise from many androgen sources; a high ADT-G does not equal “androsterone supplementation worked as neurosteroid.” Conjugates are not the GABA-active species. **Category error risk:** “ADT-G reflects androgenicity → therefore take ADT” confuses marker with parent-drug intervention. Labrie contributes **zero** efficacy certainty for exogenous ADT.

### 4.6 Pheromone / chemosignal literature (**Speculative** behavioral utility)

- ADT present in axilla, skin, urine; musky odor related to androstenol class.
- Maiworm & Langthaler 1992-type psychophysics: odor exposure can shift attractiveness ratings.
- Not established as reliable interpersonal intervention; not a substitute for systemic pharmacology.

### 4.7 WebMD / consumer monographs

- Claims marketed for muscle, sexual function, etc.; editorial position essentially **insufficient evidence**.
- Side-effect framing often AAS-class generic (not dose-mapped to 1–5 mg topical).
- Chemistry framing crude: “converted to testosterone” overstated (see disambiguation).
- Use only as signal that **regulatory-grade evidence is absent** — not as mechanism authority.

## 6. Desired Effects — Mechanism Depth

### 6.1 Calm / anxiolysis / “nervous system quieter”

1. **Phenotype sought:** Less edge, smoother social tone, reduced hyperexcitability.  
2. **Pathways:** **P1** GABA_A PAM (primary); possible mild P17.  
3. **Chain:** Free ADT in CNS → neurosteroid site on GABA_A → increased inhibitory tone → subjective calm.  
4. **Clinical:** No anxiolysis RCTs. Mouse PI best in 6 Hz/PTZ (GABA-sensitive models).  
5. **Forum:** Moderate positive subset; many nulls.  
6. **Practice:** 1–2 drops trial; avoid stacking multiple GABAergics until response known.  
7. **Certainty of effect at community dose:** **Speculative**. Mechanism class **Established**.

### 6.2 Sleep onset / sleep quality

1. **Phenotype:** Easier sleep latency.  
2. **Pathways:** P1.  
3. **Clinical:** None.  
4. **Forum:** Occasional; inconsistent.  
5. **Risk:** Excess → next-day flatness / residual sedation (**Speculative**).  
6. **Certainty:** **Speculative**.

### 6.3 Libido / sexual interest

1. **Phenotype:** Increased sexual interest, not necessarily erection mechanics alone.  
2. **Pathways:** **P6** (Bloch CSF ADT correlation); **P2** weak AR; possible **P3** local DHT.  
3. **Chain options:** (a) CNS neurosteroid modulation of sexual interest circuits; (b) mild androgen receptor tone; (c) conversion to more potent androgens in tissue.  
4. **Clinical:** Bloch supports ADT as *marker/mediator candidate* under androgen manipulation — **not** an exogenous treatment trial.  
5. **Forum:** Polarized; best reports often with concurrent TRT/androgen baseline.  
6. **Practice:** If libido is sole goal and T is low, fix T/E2 first; ADT is adjunct hypothesis. Same GABA PAM can help performance anxiety or kill drive via blunting — inverted-U (**Probable** class).  
7. **Certainty:** Exogenous benefit **Speculative**; CSF association **Probable**.

### 6.4 Body warmth / temperature / metabolic “alive” feel (Peat)

1. **Phenotype:** Warmer extremities, higher subjective temp/pulse.  
2. **Pathways:** **P7** hypothesized; possible thyroid stack confounds; mild androgen metabolic effects **Speculative**. No direct ADT→UCP thermogenesis map.  
3. **Clinical:** No controlled thermometry.  
4. **Forum:** Recurring theme among responders.  
5. **Certainty:** **Speculative**.

### 6.5 Mild dry androgenic aesthetic / “presence”

1. **Phenotype:** Slightly harder/drier feel, confidence, mild skin oil change.  
2. **Pathways:** P2, P3, P14.  
3. **Clinical:** Not studied; weak AR + microdose predict small effect size.  
4. **Forum:** Some “dry androgen” language; not Masteron-class hardness.  
5. **Certainty:** **Speculative**; not a contest-prep agent.

### 6.6 Muscle / strength / recomp

1. **Phenotype users hope for:** Lean mass.  
2. **Reality:** No RCTs; potency ~1/7 T; doses milligrams not hundreds of mg. Prior for null anabolic effect at 1–5 mg is high.  
3. **Certainty:** **Not supported** for hypertrophy. Any recomp anecdotes confounded. Cross-contamination from 1-andro PH culture is **F-tier**.

### 6.7 Seizure threshold / neurological protection

1. **Phenotype:** Theoretical reduced seizure propensity.  
2. **Pathways:** P1, P16.  
3. **Clinical:** Not an approved therapy; no human ADT epilepsy trials.  
4. **Certainty of community-dose relevance:** **Unknown**/unlikely at 1–5 mg vs mouse ED50s.  
5. **Do not** self-treat epilepsy with ADT drops.

### 6.8 Pheromone / social signaling

1. **Pathways:** P9.  
2. **Certainty:** **Speculative**; not reliable primary use.

## 7. Side Effects — Mechanism Depth + Counters

### 7.1 Null response / “expensive nothing” (S1)

1. **Phenotype:** No subjective change at 1–5 mg. Common forum signal.  
2. **Mechanism:** Insufficient free CNS/systemic exposure (P5 conjugation, poor dermal absorption, true small effect size, expectation mismatch, high baseline neurosteroid/androgen tone, UGT phenotype variance).  
3. **Counters**

| Pathway node | Counter | How it hits node | Practice | Certainty |

| First-pass if oral | Prefer topical vs oral | Avoid hepatic UGT first-pass | Stick to topical | **Probable** |
| Poor absorption site | Alternate sites (trunk, thin skin; scrotum community lore) | ↑ dermal flux | Avoid face/scalp; wash hands | **Speculative** site ranking |
