## 3. Mechanism & Pharmacology

### 3.1 Androgen receptor — partial agonism and gene selectivity

**Observed (in vitro):**

1. **Partial AR agonist** in ARE-luciferase reporter assays (Kanno et al. 2011; PMID 21372378; DOI 10.1248/bpb.34.318).
2. **Accelerates nuclear translocation** of AR.
3. **Does not induce N/C interaction** (physical NTD/AF1–LBD/AF2 interaction required for full AR transactivation).
4. **Prevents DHT-mediated N/C interaction** — can antagonize full-agonist conformational activation when co-present.
5. **Gene-selective** in MDA-MB-453 cells: relative activity vs full androgen varies by gene context.

**Inference (Speculative for tissue outcomes):** Lack of N/C interaction is the proposed molecular basis for partial/gene-selective agonism and the authors’ SARM designation. This is **cell-reporter and limited gene-expression evidence**, not Hershberger-style prostate-vs-muscle in vivo selectivity for YK-11.

### 3.2 Myostatin / follistatin pathway — **indirect**, AR-dependent

**Observed (C2C12 myoblasts, Kanno et al. 2013; PMID 23995658; DOI 10.1248/bpb.b13-00231):**

- YK11 (typically 100–500 nM in key assays) and DHT both induce myogenic differentiation markers (MyHC protein; MyoD, Myf5, myogenin mRNA).
- **Fst mRNA induced by YK11 but not by DHT** under the same conditions in C2C12.
- Fst induction blocked by hydroxyflutamide (AR antagonist) and by AR siRNA → **AR-dependent**.
- Neutralizing anti-Fst antibody abolished YK11-driven **Myf5** induction → Fst is mechanistically important for that arm of differentiation.
- Funding: MEXT Grant-in-Aid; Open Research Center Project (academic; no industry COI disclosed in paper).

**What this is *not*:**

- Not direct binding/antagonism at the myostatin (GDF8) ligand.
- Not ActRIIB decoy-receptor pharmacology.
- Not demonstrated clinical myostatin blockade in humans.

**Correct framing:** YK-11 is best described as a **steroidal gene-selective AR partial agonist that can upregulate follistatin in vitro**, thereby **functionally antagonizing myostatin/TGF-β family signaling in that cell model**. Marketing shorthand “direct myostatin inhibitor” is **false**.

### 3.3 Osteoblast / non-genomic signaling

Yatsu, Kanno et al. 2018 (PMID 29491216; DOI 10.1248/bpb.b17-00748): In MC3T3-E1 osteoblasts, YK11 and DHT accelerated proliferation/mineralization and raised OPG/OCN markers; effects attenuated by AR antagonist; **p-Akt** increased → non-genomic AR–Akt contribution hypothesized. **In vitro only.**

### 3.4 In vivo animal extensions

- **Lee et al. 2021** (PMID 33588136; DOI 10.1016/j.bbrc.2021.01.030): Mouse gram-negative sepsis model; authors frame YK11 as “myostatin inhibitor”; reported changes in myostatin protein, Fst, NF-κB, MRFs, p-FOXO3a, p-Smad2; reduced cytokines/organ injury markers and mortality. Abstract-level acquisition; full dose/methods paywalled. Authors declare no COI; non-U.S. government support. **Surrogate sepsis outcomes, not gym hypertrophy.**
- **Dahleh et al. 2023–2024:** Rat models at stated anabolic doses (e.g., 0.35 g/kg in hippocampal work — note unit as reported in abstract) show hippocampal oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, pro-inflammatory cytokine shifts, BDNF/TrkB/CREB downregulation, apoptotic signaling, impaired aversive memory consolidation; PBPK modeling suggests brain permeability. **Safety signals, not efficacy.**
- Human clinical PK/PD, bioavailability, half-life, metabolite activity: **not established in peer-reviewed clinical literature** (doping-control metabolite papers exist for detection, not therapeutic PK).

## 9. Compound Context Summary

YK-11 (myostine) is a **steroidal, gene-selective AR partial agonist** that, in **mouse C2C12 cells**, drives myogenic markers partly via **AR-dependent follistatin induction**—an **indirect** myostatin-pathway effect, **not** direct MSTN blockade (Kanno 2011/2013). Osteoblast and mouse sepsis findings remain **preclinical**. **No human RCTs** establish dose, efficacy, or safety. Marketing claims that it is a “direct myostatin inhibitor,” “gene-selective so side-effect free,” or “safer than steroids” are **bro-science or oversell**. WADA bans it; Health Canada flags unauthorized SARM products (including myostine) for serious risks. **Practical bottom line:** **No evidence-based human dose can be recommended (**Unknown**)**. Prefer not to use; if research-chemical exposure still occurs, apply **SARM/AAS-class harm reduction** (lipids, LFTs, HPG labs, PCT uncertainty, contamination risk) and **abandon the clinical-myostatin-blocker narrative**.
