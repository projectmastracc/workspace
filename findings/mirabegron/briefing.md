# Mirabegron (Myrbetriq) — Full Compound Profile

**Research ID:** 28f1a406  
**Date:** 2026-07-31  
**Audience:** Nootropic / gym-performance / metabolic researchers; dual-lens OAB + off-label metabolic use

## 1. Executive Card

| Field | Content |
|-------|---------|
| **Compound** | Mirabegron (YM-178); brands **Myrbetriq** (US), **Betmiga** (EU/others). Selective **β3-adrenergic receptor (ADRB3) agonist** — not a classical CNS stimulant, SARM, mitochondrial uncoupler, or antimuscarinic. |
| **Primary use cases** | **Domain A — Approved:** Overactive bladder (OAB) — urgency, urge incontinence, frequency (**Established**). **Domain B — Off-label / research:** BAT activation, WAT beiging, REE/EE increase, glucose/insulin remodeling, metabolic “quality” without proven scale weight loss (**Established** for BAT/REE/glucose endpoints at studied doses; **Established null** short-term fat/weight loss in humans). |
| **Bottom line** | First-in-class oral selective β3 agonist, FDA-approved 2012 for OAB at **25–50 mg ER once daily**. In humans it can activate BAT (dose-dependent; clearest at **100–200 mg**), raise acute REE (~**+200 kcal/d at 200 mg** single dose), induce SC WAT beiging markers at **50 mg**, and improve insulin sensitivity / HDL / adiponectin — **without consistent body-weight or fat-mass reduction** at studied durations (O'Mara 100 mg × 4 wk; Finlin 50 mg × 12 wk). Animal HFD models show fat loss more readily; that has **not** translated. Residual β1 / NET-linked CV load rises with dose and limits high-dose metabolic protocols. Practical role: OAB alternative to anticholinergics; metabolic tool for **glucose/beiging/BAT quality**, **not** a proven fat-loss primary. |
| **Biggest upsides** | OAB efficacy with dry mouth ≈ placebo (**Established**); BAT/REE at research doses (**Established** acute high-dose; **Probable** at 100 mg chronic short-term); WAT beiging + IS at approved 50 mg in obese IR (**Established** short-term open-label); HDL/adiponectin ↑ (**Probable**); no anticholinergic cognitive burden vs antimuscarinics (**Established**). |
| **Biggest downsides** | Dose-related ↑ BP/HR — mild at 25–50 mg, substantial at 150–200 mg (**Established**); moderate time-dependent CYP2D6 inhibition → polypharmacy risk (**Established**); rare urinary retention (BOO ± antimuscarinic) (**Established**); rare angioedema (**Established**); **no consistent human fat/weight loss** despite REE/BAT (**Established null** short-term) — expectation mismatch for gym users. |
| **Stack role** | OAB: mono or + solifenacin (retention caution). Metabolic: adjunct for adipose remodeling / IS when **calorie deficit + training already present**; not a substitute for GLP-1s, deficit, or cold/exercise. Avoid heavy CYP2D6 substrates, digoxin without monitoring, and uncontrolled pressor stacks. |
| **Dose anchors** | **25–50 mg** = approved OAB (**Established**); **100 mg** = best-documented research metabolic chronic dose (BAT/REE without fat loss); **150–200 mg** = acute/mechanistic, high CV cost — not a casual lifestyle dose. |

## 2. Chemistry, classification, PK

### Identity

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **IUPAC** | 2-(2-Aminothiazol-4-yl)-N-[4-(2-{[(2R)-2-hydroxy-2-phenylethyl]amino}ethyl)phenyl]acetamide |
| **Formula / MW** | C₂₁H₂₄N₄O₂S; 396.51 g/mol |
| **CAS** | 223673-61-8 |
| **Class** | Selective human β3-AR agonist; secondary amine + aminothiazole acetamide scaffold |
| **Form** | Extended-release (ER) oral tablets 25 mg and 50 mg; **swallow whole** — do not crush/chew/divide (**Established**, label) |
| **Solubility** | Practically insoluble in water (~0.082 mg/mL); soluble in methanol, DMSO |

### Classification notes

- **Not** an uncoupler: UCP1 is the thermogenic effector *after* β3 → cAMP → lipolysis; the drug does not directly uncouple mitochondria.
- **Not** a classical “stim” (amphetamine/ephedrine class): CNS penetration and subjective stimulation are low at OAB doses; high-dose CV effects are largely peripheral β1/NET-related (**Probable**).
- **Analogs:** Vibegron (Gemtesa) — selective β3 agonist without CYP2D6 inhibition label; research tools CL-316,243 / BRL-37344 — mostly animal.

### Pharmacokinetics — **Established** (US label / clinical pharmacology)

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|--------|
| **Tmax** | ~3.5 h |
| **Absolute BA** | ~29% (25 mg) → ~35% (50 mg); **more-than-dose-proportional** above 50 mg |
| **Dose proportionality** | 50→100 mg: Cmax ×~2.9, AUC ×~2.6; 50→200 mg: Cmax ×~8.4, AUC ×~6.5 |
| **Steady state** | ~7 days QD; accumulation ~2× single-dose exposure |
| **t½ terminal** | ~**50 h** (adults) |
| **Vss** | ~1670 L (extensive distribution) |
| **Protein binding** | ~71% (albumin + α1-AGP); also enters erythrocytes (~2× plasma) |
| **Metabolism** | Multi-path: dealkylation, oxidation, direct glucuronidation, amide hydrolysis. Major circulating species = parent. Two major plasma glucuronides (inactive at β3). CYP2D6/3A4 limited role in *elimination of parent*; BChE, UGTs, possibly ADH also contribute |
| **Excretion** | ~55% urine / ~34% feces; ~25% urine as unchanged parent (dose-dependent fraction) |
| **Food** | High-fat: Cmax −45%, AUC −17%; low-fat: Cmax −75%, AUC −51%. Phase 3 efficacy with or without food → label allows either |
| **Sex** | Females higher exposure; body-weight-corrected ~20–30% higher vs males |
| **Renal** | Severe (eGFR 15–29): ↑ exposure → max 25 mg; ESRD not studied / not recommended |
| **Hepatic** | Moderate (Child-Pugh B): max 25 mg; severe not studied / not recommended |
| **Age** | No clinically significant PK shift in elderly; no dose adjustment for age alone |
| **CNS** | Not a high-CNS-penetrant stimulant profile; post-marketing neuropsych AEs confounded (**Speculative** for direct β3 CNS effect) |

### Selectivity — **Established** / **Probable**

- Human β3 EC50 nanomolar (~10–22 nM class reporting); **>400-fold** selectivity vs β1/β2 in classic functional assays (Takasu / Nagiri cryo-EM lineage).
- Residual activity at high concentration: at 10 μM, residual β1 ~3%, β2 ~15% relative to isoproterenol (Brucker-type assays).
- Label: very low intrinsic activity at cloned human β1/β2, but **β1 stimulation observed at 200 mg** in humans.
- High-dose CV: attenuated by propranolol or bisoprolol → mechanism includes **β1** and/or **enhanced noradrenaline release / NET interaction** (van Gelderen 2017) (**Probable–Established**).
- Weak α1 antagonism (urethral/vascular research) — **Speculative** clinical weight.

## 4. Clinical evidence (full evaluation)

### Dual-domain rule

| Domain | Question | Evidence base | Certainty ceiling |
|--------|----------|---------------|-------------------|
| **A — OAB** | Urgency, frequency, urge incontinence; BP/HR at 25–50 mg | Phase 3 RCTs (SCORPIO, ARIES, CAPRICORN), labels, AUA/SUFU | **Established** at approved doses |
| **B — Metabolic** | BAT, REE, beiging, glucose, body composition | Small acute RCTs/crossovers; small open-label chronic metabolic studies; animals | **Established** only for measured endpoints in those designs; **null** short-term fat loss |

Domain A sample size does **not** upgrade Domain B fat-loss claims. Domain B mechanism positivity does **not** upgrade OAB beyond label.

### 4.1 OAB efficacy — Phase 3 monotherapy (**Established**)

Astellas-sponsored pivotal 12-week double-blind placebo-controlled RCTs (label Studies 1–3; SCORPIO / ARIES / CAPRICORN lineage; pooled safety ~2,700):

| Endpoint (Week 12 vs placebo) | Effect (approx, 50 mg) | Certainty |
|------------------------------|------------------------|-----------|
| Incontinence episodes / 24 h | −0.34 to −0.42 episodes (placebo-subtracted) | **Established** |
| Micturitions / 24 h | −0.42 to −0.61 | **Established** |
| Volume voided / micturition | +11–12 mL (50 mg; 25 mg weaker on volume in Study 3) | **Established** |

- Onset: 25 mg effective by ~8 weeks; 50 mg by ~4 weeks; maintained through 12 weeks.
- 12-month active-controlled (TAURUS-class): sustained efficacy; dry mouth 2.8% (50 mg) vs 8.6% tolterodine ER 4 mg (**Established** tolerability advantage).
- Combo with solifenacin: SYMPHONY / BESIDE / SYNERGY — additive OAB benefit; higher dry mouth / constipation / retention caution (**Established**).
- AUA/SUFU 2024 idiopathic OAB: oral pharma class (antimuscarinics **or** β3 agonists) **Strong Recommendation, Grade A**.

**COI note:** Entire pivotal OAB program is Astellas-sponsored. Multi-trial geographic replication supports direction of effect; discount marketing framing, not the diary endpoint signal.

**What OAB data do *not* show:** Superiority to all antimuscarinics on every endpoint; zero CV risk; any body-composition or fat-loss claim (not measured).

### 4.2 OAB safety (label pooled) — **Established**

| Signal | Detail | Certainty |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Common AEs (>2% and >placebo, mono 25–50 mg) | Hypertension, nasopharyngitis, UTI, headache | **Established** |
| Mean BP @ 50 mg OAB trials | ~**0.5–1 mmHg** > placebo | **Established** |
| Pulse | ~+1 bpm mean at 50 mg efficacy; thorough QT: +6.7 / +11 / +17 bpm at 50/100/200 mg | **Established** dose-related |
| Severe uncontrolled HTN | Not recommended if ≥180 / ≥110 | **Established** label |
| Urinary retention | Rare; caution BOO + antimuscarinic | **Established** |
| Angioedema | Rare, first dose or delayed — stop, airway | **Established** post-marketing |
| QTc | 50 mg upper bound <10 ms; larger at 200 mg (esp. females) | **Established** |
| MACE vs antimuscarinics (labeled use) | Hoffman 2021 PASS: no higher risk | **Probable** (large, Astellas-funded observational) |

### 4.3 Metabolic / BAT human studies (Domain B)

| Study | Design / n | Dose | Key results | Limits | Certainty |
|-------|------------|------|-------------|--------|-----------|
| **Cypess 2015** *Cell Metab* PMID 25565203 | Acute, n=12 lean men, placebo-controlled | **200 mg** single | BAT FDG-PET ↑ **all** subjects; RMR **+203±40 kcal/d (+13%)**; BAT activity predicted ΔRMR; WAT lipolysis ↑ | Supratherapeutic; CV sides; male-only; acute | **Established**: high-dose acute BAT/REE |
| **Loh 2019** *Diabetes Obes Metab* PMID 30203462 | Acute ascending dose n=17 (11M/6F) | 50 / 100 / 150 / **200** mg | EE ↑ at **100** & **200**; Tsc ↑ 50–150; SBP/HR worst 150–200; **100 mg** best EE:CV trade | Acute; small | **Probable** dose-response map |

| Baskin et al. | Acute 50 vs 200 | — | BAT dose-dependent; REE clearer at 200 | Small | **Probable** |

**Funding:** Cypess / O'Mara / Finlin = NIH-linked academic (not Astellas OAB registration). Small n and open-label chronic designs are the main limits — not manufacturer obesity marketing. Null body-composition findings were published despite academic incentive for positive mechanism.

### 4.4 Hard lock — human fat / weight loss (**Established null short-term**)

1. **O'Mara 2020:** 100 mg × 4 weeks — BAT ↑, REE ↑, metabolic blood markers improved — **no change in weight or body composition**.
2. **Finlin 2020:** 50 mg × 12 weeks obese IR — beiging + IS improved — **no BAT↑, no REE↑, no weight/fat loss**.
3. These nulls are **decisive against short-term positive fat-loss claims** at 50–100 mg: composition was measured and unchanged while other metabolic endpoints moved (assay sensitivity/adherence sufficient for non-weight biology).
4. Acute REE math (Cypess +203 kcal/d) **must not** be annualized into “guaranteed fat loss.” O'Mara shows REE can rise without composition shift over weeks.
5. Animal HFD fat loss (Hao 2019; Zhao metformin combo 2023) = **Speculative** human translation only (rodent BAT mass/β3 density differ).
6. Longer-term (>12 wk) human fat loss under controlled deficit = **Unknown** (no adequate RCT). Do not invent gradual-loss curves from acute REE alone.

**Ceiling summary**

| Claim | Ceiling |
|-------|---------|
| Short-term (≤12 wk) fat/weight loss @ 50–100 mg | **Established null** under studied conditions |
| Longer-term fat loss or chronic 200 mg | **Unknown** |
| Animal fat loss → human protocol | **Speculative** |
| Marketing “steady fat loss 8–12 wk @ 50–150 mg” | **Contradicted** by Finlin/O'Mara — discard as evidence |

### 4.5 What clinical data do **not** show

- Mirabegron as a primary obesity drug with clinically meaningful % body-fat loss in humans.
- Superior fat loss vs cold training, exercise, or GLP-1 RAs.
- Safety of chronic **200 mg** daily for metabolic lifestyle goals.
- Necessity of mirabegron when deficit + resistance training + protein already optimized.
- Cognitive enhancement or sleep benefit (not primary endpoints).
- That “works better in a deficit” is proven — **Speculative** (no controlled cut RCT).

### 4.6 Preclinical / animal (context only)

- Hao 2019 *JPET*: HFD mice — ↓ weight/adiposity, ↑ UCP1, beiging, better GTT/IS.
- Dehvari et al.: β3-dependent glucose uptake BAT/WAT, EE.
- Zhao 2023: metformin + mirabegron additive weight loss in mice — human stack **Unknown**.

## 6. Desired effects — mechanism depth

### 6.1 OAB symptom control

- **Phenotype:** Fewer leaks, fewer voids, larger voided volume, better QoL.
- **Pathways:** **P1** (primary), **P1b**.
- **Chain:** Oral absorption → systemic β3 on detrusor → Gs-cAMP-PKA → SM relaxation during storage → ↑ functional capacity → reduced urgency.
- **Clinical:** Phase 3 co-primary endpoints met (**Established**).
- **Forum:** High concordance.
- **Practical:** Start **25 mg QD**; ↑ **50 mg** after 4–8 weeks if needed (**Established** label). ER whole, with or without food. Combo solifenacin if incomplete response (retention risk ↑).

### 6.2 BAT activation / thermogenesis / acute REE

- **Phenotype:** ↑ FDG uptake in BAT; ↑ RMR/REE; ↑ supraclavicular skin temperature.
- **Pathways:** **P2, P4**.
- **Chain:** β3 → cAMP → PKA → HSL lipolysis → FFA oxidation + UCP1 uncoupling → heat + ↑ EE; glucose uptake rises (PET).
- **Clinical:** Cypess 200 mg acute **+203 kcal/d**; Loh EE at 100 & 200 mg; O'Mara chronic 100 mg BAT volume/activity ↑ + modest baseline REE ↑ (**Established** high-dose acute; **Probable–Established** at 100 mg short chronic). Finlin 50 mg chronic obese IR: **no BAT↑, no REE↑** (**Established null** that dose/phenotype).
- **Forum:** Over-extrapolated to “easy fat loss.”
- **Practical:** Research metabolic interest often **50–100 mg**; **200 mg research-only** with high CV cost. **Honest:** REE ↑ ≠ fat loss without energy deficit and without intake compensation.

### 6.3 WAT beiging / adipose remodeling

- **Phenotype:** ↑ beige markers in SC WAT; improved adipose inflammatory tone.
- **Pathways:** **P3, P15**.
- **Chain:** Chronic β3 tone on white adipocytes → transcriptional beiging + macrophage phenotype shift.
- **Clinical:** Finlin 50 mg × 12 wk obese IR (**Established** markers).
- **Practical:** Achievable at **approved 50 mg** — better risk:benefit than chasing 200 mg for beiging alone.

### 6.4 Insulin sensitivity / glucose / β-cell function

- **Phenotype:** Better OGTT, lower HbA1c, higher GIR, better disposition index.
- **Pathways:** **P5, P6, P3, P15** (indirect muscle/adipose; not direct β3 on β-cells or myocytes as primary).
- **Clinical:** Finlin + O'Mara (**Established** short-term open-label).
- **Practical:** Best evidence-aligned metabolic use case for **50–100 mg** when glucose/IS is the goal rather than scale weight.

### 6.5 HDL / adiponectin / bile acids

- **Phenotype:** ↑ HDL-C, ApoA1, adiponectin; bile acid changes; GIP elevation reported in chronic 100 mg context.
- **Pathways:** **P6, P7**.
- **Clinical:** O'Mara (**Probable–Established** short-term).
- **Practical:** Surrogate CV-metabolic markers; not a statin replacement.

### 6.6 Indirect muscle oxidative phenotype

- **Phenotype:** ↑ vastus lateralis PGC1A, ↑ type I fiber proportion, ↓ muscle TG (Finlin).
- **Pathways:** **P5** — muscle does **not** express meaningful β3 as primary target; adipocyte conditioned media → myotube PGC1A (**Probable** bridge).
- **Clinical:** Finlin 50 mg × 12 wk (**Established** histology/marker package).
- **Performance translation** (endurance, recovery): **Unknown**.

### 6.7 Body fat / weight loss (desired by many gym users)

- **Phenotype sought:** Measurable ↓ fat mass.
- **Pathways claimed:** P2+P3 → EE ↑ → deficit.
- **Clinical human:** **No consistent fat/weight loss** in O'Mara (4 wk, 100 mg) or Finlin (12 wk, 50 mg) (**Established null** at those designs). Acute Cypess not a body-comp trial.
- **Animal:** Positive fat loss (**not** human proof).
- **Forum:** Marketing claims > data.
- **Practical certainty:** Using mirabegron **as a fat-loss primary is not evidence-supported**. If used, pair with measured deficit + training; expect **metabolic remodeling > scale change**. **Speculative** that longer trials or uncompensated higher doses could show small fat loss — unproven, CV-limited. Do not escalate to 200 mg “for more burn.”

## 7. Side effects — mechanism depth + counters

Engineering stance: each major side → mechanism nodes → counters that hit those nodes. Speculative counters labeled. Not a refusal essay.

### 7.1 Blood pressure increase / hypertension

1. **Phenotype:** Mean SBP/DBP ↑ ~0.5–1 mmHg vs placebo at 25–50 mg in OAB patients; larger in healthy-volunteer multiple-dose studies (~3.5/1.5 at 50 mg max mean; escalating toward ~9–10 mmHg SBP territory at 200 mg 24-h averages in thorough studies). Categorical “hypertension” AE more common in some cells. Worsening of pre-existing HTN reported.
2. **Mechanism:** Residual β1 agonism at high exposure + facilitated noradrenaline release → cardiac inotropy/chronotropy + vascular tone (**P8, P9**); possible α1 nuances (**Speculative**). More-than-dose-proportional exposure above 50 mg amplifies.
3. **Pathway → counter table**

| Pathway node | Counter | How it hits the node | Clinical | Forum | Practice notes | Certainty |
|--------------|---------|----------------------|----------|-------|----------------|-----------|
| Excess cardiac β1 tone | Dose ↓ (50→25 mg or stop) | Lowers exposure → less off-target | Label | Common | First step | **Established** |
| Excess cardiac β1 / nonlinear exposure | Avoid 100–200 mg if BP elevated; prefer ≤50 mg metabolic start | Avoids high-exposure band | Loh; label | Biohackers often ignore | Cap research interest near 100 mg with monitoring | **Established** risk gradient |
| NA / β1 drive | Selective β1 blocker (bisoprolol/propranolol) **only if medically indicated** | Blocks cardiac β1 effector | van Gelderen attenuation | Occasional DIY | **Not** a casual “cover” stack; may theoretically blunt metabolic β effects | **Established** PD attenuation; DIY use **not recommended** |
| Baseline HTN | Optimize antihypertensives; home BP logs | Reduces starting risk | Label monitoring | — | Cuff AM/PM baseline → weekly first month | **Established** |
| Severe uncontrolled HTN ≥180/110 | **Do not start** | Absolute label caution | Label | — | Stop criterion | **Established** |
| Sympathetic load stacking | Avoid high-dose yohimbine, high EC, clen co-use | Removes additive pressor | Mechanistic | Common stack risk | Separate experiments | **Probable** |
| Ambient SNS load | Sleep, sodium moderation, alcohol limit, caffeine cap on high-dose days | Lowers ambient tone | General HTN | — | Supportive | **Probable** |
| Timing | Morning dose if nocturnal BP concerns | Aligns peak with daytime monitoring | **Speculative** | — | Optional | **Speculative** |

### 7.2 Heart rate increase / tachycardia / palpitations

1. **Phenotype:** Mean pulse +~1 bpm at 50 mg OAB; thorough QT: +6.7 / +11 / +17 bpm at 50/100/200 mg; AE tachycardia ~1.2–1.6% mono. AF rare signal (FAERS hypothesis-generating).
2. **Mechanism:** Same **P8/P9** chain (β1 + NA).
3. **Counters**

| Pathway node | Counter | How it hits the node | Clinical | Forum | Practice notes | Certainty |
|--------------|---------|----------------------|----------|-------|----------------|-----------|
| β1 chronotropy | Dose reduction / discontinue | Exposure ↓ | Label | Yes | Primary | **Established** |
| β1 chronotropy | β1-selective blocker (medical care) | Receptor block | van Gelderen | — | ECG if symptomatic | **Probable** under care |
| Dual chronotrope | Limit caffeine on peak days (Tmax ~3.5 h) | Reduces additive load | PD | Common | Easy win | **Probable** |
| Arrhythmia risk (rare) | Stop if sustained tachycardia, AF symptoms; ECG | Removes driver | Post-marketing AF | — | Stop criterion | **Established** caution |
| Multi-β stacks | Avoid clen + high-dose mira; extreme EC | Multi-node HR | Mechanistic | Common risk | Do not stack carelessly | **Probable** |

### 7.3 Headache

1. **Phenotype:** ~3% range, similar to or slightly above placebo; common discontinuation reason.
2. **Mechanism:** Vascular/adrenergic tone changes; BP rise; hydration/caffeine confounds (**Probable** multi-factor).
3. **Counters**

| Pathway node | Counter | How it hits the node | Clinical | Forum | Practice notes | Certainty |
|--------------|---------|----------------------|----------|-------|----------------|-----------|
| BP/vascular | Dose ↓; BP control; rule out pressor headache | Removes driver | Label | Yes | Track BP with headache | **Probable** |
| Nociceptive | Simple analgesic short course | Symptom | General | Yes | Not chronic cover-up | **Established** symptomatic |
| Dehydration/caffeine withdrawal | Fluids; caffeine consistency | Confounders | General | Yes | Easy wins | **Speculative** for mira-specific |
| Progressive/neuro signs | Stop + medical eval | Red flags | Standard | — | Stop criterion | **Established** |

### 7.4 Urinary retention (esp. BOO + antimuscarinic)

1. **Phenotype:** Uncommon; post-marketing and combo trials; hesitancy, incomplete emptying, weak stream.
2. **Mechanism:** β3-mediated detrusor relaxation (wanted in OAB) can impair voiding force when outlet obstructed or when muscarinic drive to detrusor is also blocked (**Established** caution logic). Do **not** rely on theoretical urethral α1 antagonism as retention prophylaxis (**Speculative**).
3. **Counters**

| Pathway node | Counter | How it hits the node | Clinical | Forum | Practice notes | Certainty |
|--------------|---------|----------------------|----------|-------|----------------|-----------|
| Dual detrusor suppression | Caution / supervise + solifenacin/oxybutynin if high retention risk; PVR monitoring | Removes or manages second hit | Label 5.2; SYNERGY | Urology forums | Indicated combos exist but monitor | **Established** |
| BOO (e.g. advanced BPH) | Urology assessment; α1-blocker if indicated for outlet | Treats outlet | Standard care | — | Mira alone often OK with caution | **Established** caution |
| Acute retention | Stop drug; catheterize if needed | Removes agonist | Emergency standard | — | Stop criterion | **Established** |

### 7.5 UTI / nasopharyngitis

1. **Phenotype:** Among most common AEs in OAB trials (slightly > placebo).
2. **Mechanism:** UTI: not classic immunosuppression; possible voiding pattern change, residual urine, or surveillance bias in OAB population (**Unknown–Speculative**). Nasopharyngitis: noise + possible mild SNS effects.
3. **Counters:** Hydration; don’t delay voids; screen retention if recurrent UTI; urinalysis if dysuria/fever; treat infection per standard care — do not “immune stack” blindly (**Established** clinical care).

### 7.6 CYP2D6 polypharmacy toxicity

1. **Phenotype:** ↑ substrate AUC — metoprolol Cmax +90%, AUC +229% (IR 160 mg study design); desipramine AUC +241% class magnitude. Bradycardia/hypotension with metoprolol; TCA toxicity; antiarrhythmic level rise; many SSRIs/SNRIs, atomoxetine, tramadol, tamoxifen activation complexity.
2. **Mechanism:** **P10** moderate time-dependent CYP2D6 inhibition (Krauwinkel; label).
3. **Counters**

| Pathway node | Counter | How it hits the node | Clinical | Forum | Practice notes | Certainty |
|--------------|---------|----------------------|----------|-------|----------------|-----------|
| 2D6 inhibition | Full med reconciliation before start | Avoids surprise exposure | Label | Under-discussed in gym | Metoprolol, many psych meds, codeine activation, tamoxifen | **Established** |
| 2D6 substrate levels | Dose-adjust substrate, monitor effects, or avoid combo for NTI drugs | Compensates PK | Label | — | Narrow TI highest priority | **Established** |
| Mixed CV picture (metoprolol + mira) | Clinician titration — exposure↑ of β-blocker **and** mira pressor effects | Dual PD/PK | Mechanistic | — | Do not DIY blindly | **Probable** complexity |
| Switch OAB agent | Vibegron (no CYP2D6 inhibition label) | Removes inhibitor | Comparative pharm | Growing | Strong alternative when polypharmacy heavy | **Established** interaction difference |
| “Natural” 2D6 substrates | Still exposure risk | Same node | Sparse | — | Magnitude **Speculative** | **Speculative** |

### 7.7 Digoxin exposure increase

1. **Phenotype:** Cmax +29%, AUC +27% at 100 mg mira.
2. **Mechanism:** Weak P-gp / labeled digoxin DDI (**P11**).
3. **Counters:** Initiate digoxin at **lowest dose**; serial serum digoxin; toxicity watch (GI, visual, arrhythmia) (**Established** label). Same caution if + solifenacin.

### 7.8 Angioedema (rare serious)

1. **Phenotype:** Face/lips/tongue/larynx swelling; first dose or delayed; airway threat.
2. **Mechanism:** Hypersensitivity (**Established** as reaction class; molecular detail **Unknown**).
3. **Counters:** **Immediate stop**; emergency care if airway involvement; never rechallenge; no “antihistamine stack through it” for laryngeal symptoms (**Established**).

### 7.9 Metabolic non-response for fat loss (efficacy-side for gym users)

1. **Phenotype:** User expects fat loss; scale/DEXA unchanged after 4–12 weeks.
2. **Mechanism:** Small/absent REE at 50 mg obese (Finlin); REE↑ at high dose still ~80–200 kcal/d offsetable; limited adult BAT mass; compensatory intake (**P14**); short trials; no intake-control RCTs.
3. **Counters (protocol engineering, not refusal)**

| Pathway node | Counter | How it hits the node | Clinical | Forum | Practice notes | Certainty |
|--------------|---------|----------------------|----------|-------|----------------|-----------|
| Energy intake compensation | Track intake; 300–500 kcal deficit; high protein | Prevents offset of REE | Energy balance physics | Often ignored | 200 kcal REE easy to eat back | **Probable** |
| Wrong primary tool | Prefer GLP-1 RA / deficit / training for fat loss | Uses tools with established fat-loss RCTs | Obesity literature | — | Mira as adjunct only | **Established** for alternatives |
| Expectation setting | Goal = IS/HDL/beiging/BAT quality, not lbs alone | Aligns with O'Mara/Finlin | Null weight trials | Marketing contradicts | Reduces “failed cycle” behavior | **Established** data alignment |
| Low BAT phenotype / weak REE | Concurrent mild cold; accept beiging/IS goals | Parallel SNS–β3 stimulus | Finlin cold lineage | Biohackers combine | Synergy magnitude **Speculative** | **Speculative** synergy |
| Carnitine | FA shuttle when lipolysis engaged | Hits oxidation node | Thin human stack data | Common list | Low harm if renal OK | **Speculative** |
| Metformin | Only if otherwise indicated (IR/T2D) | Additive weight loss **mice only** (Zhao) | Animal | — | Human weight stack **Unknown** | **Speculative** human fat-loss stack |
| Dose honesty | Do not chase 200 mg for fat loss | Avoids CV cost without proven chronic fat loss | Cypess/Loh | Common escalation fantasy | Cap research interest | **Established** risk–benefit caution |
| Duration realism | ≥12–16 wk body-comp experiment with DEXA + deficit — still may be null | Matches Finlin window | Null composition | — | Reassess; don’t auto-escalate dose | **Probable** |
| Exercise | Resistance + Zone 2 | Raises TDEE; parallel to Finlin oxidative muscle shift | Lifestyle evidence | — | Foundation layer | **Probable** synergy for composition |

### 7.10 Energy perception vs deficit need

1. **Phenotype:** Subjective energy may rise from SNS/REE without net fat loss if calories match new EE.
2. **Mechanism:** **P14** + small absolute REE delta.
3. **Counters:** Log weight weekly + waist; if EE feels higher, **do not auto-increase food**; keep deficit intentional; protect sleep under SNS load (**Probable**).

### 7.11 GI (nausea, diarrhea, constipation, dyspepsia)

1. **Phenotype:** Low single-digit %; sometimes discontinuation.
2. **Mechanism:** Mixed autonomic / nonspecific (**Speculative** detail).
3. **Counters:** Take with food if GI upset (label allows either); dose ↓; fiber/fluids; rule out retention-related abdominal symptoms (**Probable**).

### 7.12 Neuropsychiatric (insomnia, anxiety, confusion, hallucinations — post-marketing)

1. **Phenotype:** Uncommon; often confounded by age/comeds.
2. **Mechanism:** Mild peripheral/central adrenergic tone (**Speculative**).
3. **Counters:** Morning dosing; stop if severe; review interacting meds (**Speculative** causality; **Established** stop-if-severe practice).

### 8.4 Protection-oriented mini-stacks (assembled)

**A. Pressor / HR protection (conditional)**  
- Eligibility filter: BP controlled  
- Monitor: cuff + resting HR  
- Dose discipline: ≤50 mg unless research intent + monitoring  
- Lifestyle: caffeine/alcohol cap  
- Pharmacologic β1-block: **only if clinician-indicated** (not prophylactic DIY)  
- Hard stop: symptomatic tachycardia, chest pain, severe headache with BP spike  

**B. Urinary safety**  
- Screen BOO risk  
- Avoid unneeded antimuscarinic dual therapy  
- Symptom diary for hesitancy / PVR evaluation  

**C. Metabolic non-response (fat) engineering**  
- Deficit mandatory  
- Protein + lifting  
- Cold + steps/Zone 2  
- Optional carnitine  
- Reassess 8–12 wk with composition metrics; if null, do **not** escalate to 200 mg for “more burn” without accepting CV trade  

**D. DDI protection**  
- Full med list vs CYP2D6 + digoxin  
- Pharmacist/clinician pass before polypharmacy  

### 8.5 Protocol sketches

**A. OAB standard**  
25 mg ER QAM × 4–8 wk → if incomplete and BP OK → 50 mg ER QD. Reassess symptoms and BP.

**B. Metabolic “approved-dose remodel” (Finlin-aligned)**  
50 mg ER QD × 12 wk + measured mild deficit + lifting. Track BP, fasting glucose/HbA1c or OGTT if available, waist, DEXA if available. Primary success = IS/glucose/beiging interest, **not** weight.

**C. Metabolic “BAT-push” (O'Mara-aligned)**  
100 mg ER QD × 4 wk only if BP well controlled, no severe HTN history, med list clean of critical 2D6 substrates. Home BP log daily first 2 weeks. Stop if SBP sustained +10–15 mmHg or symptomatic tachycardia. Expect possible REE/BAT signal; **not** guaranteed fat loss.

**D. Avoid**  
200 mg daily “Cypess protocol” outside research setting with CV monitoring.

## 10. Monitoring, contraindications, stop criteria

### Contraindications / strong cautions (**Established** label)

- Hypersensitivity to mirabegron or tablet components.
- **Severe uncontrolled hypertension** (≥180 systolic and/or ≥110 diastolic) — not recommended.
- ESRD / severe hepatic impairment — not recommended.
- Pregnancy: insufficient human data; animal skeletal/fetal weight signals at high multiples — risk-benefit with clinician.
- Pediatric: standard adult OAB labeling context — out of scope (separate NDO programs exist regionally).

### Monitoring checklist

| Item | When | Why |
|------|------|-----|
| BP + pulse | Baseline, 1–2 weeks, then periodic (esp. hypertensives; any dose ≥50–100 mg) | P8/P9 |
| Urinary symptoms (hesitancy, weak stream, retention) | Ongoing; extra if BOO or + antimuscarinic | P1 overshoot |
| Medication list (CYP2D6, digoxin) | Before start + each new drug | P10, P11 |
| Digoxin level | If co-administered | P11 |
| Glucose/HbA1c or waist/DEXA | If metabolic goals | Outcome tracking |
| Angioedema signs | Any time, incl. first doses | Hypersensitivity |

### Stop criteria

- Angioedema / airway swelling — **stop immediately**.
- Symptomatic urinary retention.
- Sustained marked BP rise or hypertensive urgency (e.g. sustained SBP ≥160 or symptomatic).
- New significant arrhythmia / uncontrolled tachycardia / chest pain.
- Severe headache with hypertensive readings.
- Unacceptable polypharmacy interaction not manageable by dose adjust.
- Metabolic run: stop if only goal was fat loss and 8–12 weeks show null composition with sides present — fix intake node or change tool, do not auto-escalate to 200 mg.

## 12. Open questions

1. **Long-duration (≥6–12 mo) placebo-controlled body-composition RCTs** at 50 vs 100 mg with controlled intake — would most change the fat-loss map.
2. Whether **β1-blockade preserves BAT/REE** while removing CV cost (early experiments mixed; not a DIY cover).
3. **Sex and age interactions** on BAT recruitability (O'Mara young women vs Finlin older obese).
4. Optimal **stack with GLP-1/GIP dual agonists** for complementary EI↓ + EE↑ and fat-mass partitioning.
5. Chronic **100 mg** safety outside OAB (BP, AF, myocardial load) — multi-year high-dose metabolic self-experimentation **Unknown**.
6. Human confirmation of **α1** contribution to lower urinary tract effects at therapeutic free concentrations.
7. Whether **appetite compensation** is measurable with continuous intake monitoring under REE increase.
8. Head-to-head **mirabegron vs vibegron metabolic** (BAT/IS) — vibegron metabolic human program thinner.
9. Genetic **ADRB3** variants (e.g., Trp64Arg) as response modifiers.
10. True incidence of **neuropsychiatric** AEs after de-confounding.
11. Human **metformin + mirabegron** fat-loss RCT (animal only so far).
12. Whether chronic β3 agonism **downregulates** BAT response (catecholamine resistance).
13. Quantitative contribution of beige vs classical BAT to REE at 50 vs 100 mg by age/BMI/sex.

## 14. Short boundary

This document is a mechanism-complete research synthesis for informed adults studying mirabegron in OAB and off-label metabolic contexts. It is not medical advice, a prescription, or a guarantee of outcomes. Prescription β3 agonists require clinician oversight for BP, drug interactions (especially CYP2D6 and digoxin), urinary retention risk, and indication. Metabolic use above labeled OAB doses is research territory with clear cardiovascular trade-offs and **no established human fat-loss indication**.
