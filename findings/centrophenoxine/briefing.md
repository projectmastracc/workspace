# Centrophenoxine (Meclofenoxate) — Full Research Briefing

**Slug:** `centrophenoxine` · **Type:** Compound profile · **Research ID:** b3b0da7c · **Date:** 2026-07-31  
**Also known as:** Meclofenoxate · Lucidryl · Helfergin · many regional brand names · ester of **DMAE** + **pCPA**

---

## 1. Executive Card

| Field | Content |
|-------|---------|
| **Compound** | Centrophenoxine / meclofenoxate — synthetic ester of **dimethylaminoethanol (DMAE)** and **p-chlorophenoxyacetic acid (pCPA)**. Cholinergic nootropic developed mid-20th century (often cited ~1950s–60s). Prescription history in some countries; grey-market / research-chem nootropic in others. |
| **Primary use cases** | Memory / alertness support in aging; experimental dementia adjunct (historical); **racetam stack choline source**; anti-aging / lipofuscin lore; mild “stimulant-like” clarity without classic amphetamine pharmacology. |
| **Bottom line** | Centrophenoxine is a **DMAE delivery vehicle** designed so pCPA helps shuttle DMAE; esterases release DMAE, which feeds choline / phospholipid pathways and is marketed as raising **acetylcholine**. Human trials are **mostly old and methodologically limited**. Best signal: **Marcer & Hopkins 1977** — healthy elderly, **600 mg BID × 9 months**, better **delayed free-recall** vs placebo, subjective alertness; other memory tests null. Dementia trials (e.g. Pek 1989 2 g/day) are mixed/inconclusive. **DMAE pyroglutamate** (related active) failed cognitive endpoints in prodromal AD (Dubois 2012, n=242). Preclinical: ↓ lipofuscin, antioxidant effects, learning in aged/impaired rodents; one mouse study ↑ lifespan (Hochschild 1973). Safety: generally mild ACh-excess sides (nausea, headache, insomnia if late); **contraindicated in severe hypertension** in some product literature; **DMAE teratogenicity concern** (neural tube defects in mouse embryo models) → avoid in pregnancy. Not a modern first-line AD drug. |
| **Biggest upsides** | Delayed free-recall signal in healthy elderly (**Speculative–Probable**). Practical oral choline-like support for racetam stacks (**Speculative** ACh mechanism, high forum use). Lipofuscin / oxidative aging biology in animals (**Probable** preclinical). |
| **Biggest downsides** | Weak modern evidence base; dementia claims oversold. Cholinergic sides. Pregnancy risk (DMAE). Possible stimulant sleep disruption. Not superior to standard cholinergic AD meds (donepezil etc.). |
| **Stack role** | Choline arm of racetam stacks; optional elderly memory experiment — **not** a replacement for diagnosis, sleep, or AChEIs when those are indicated. |

**Certainty:** **Established** · **Probable** · **Speculative** · **Unknown**

## 2. Chemistry, classification, PK

### 2.1 Structure and design rationale

| Component | Role |
|-----------|------|
| **DMAE** (dimethylethanolamine) | Putative active cholinergic precursor / membrane phospholipid precursor |
| **pCPA** (4-chlorophenoxyacetic acid) | Synthetic auxin-like carrier; claimed to improve BBB delivery vs free DMAE |
| **Ester bond** | Hydrolyzed by esterases (liver/plasma) → free DMAE + pCPA |

**Classification:** Nootropic / cholinergic precursor (not a racetam, not a cholinesterase inhibitor, not a psychostimulant in the amphetamine class).

### 2.2 Pharmacokinetics

| Parameter | What is known | Certainty |
|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| **Absorption** | Oral well absorbed (clinical use history) | **Probable** |
| **Metabolism** | Ester hydrolysis → DMAE + pCPA | **Established** |
| **CNS entry** | Intact ester and/or DMAE better brain access than free DMAE in older literature; many BBB claims from IV/animal work | **Probable** improved delivery vs plain DMAE; quantitative human CNS PK sparse |
| **Downstream** | DMAE → choline pathways → phosphatidylcholine / ACh synthesis (partial; not as direct as CDP-choline) | **Probable** contribution; quantitative ACh rise in human brain **not well measured** |
| **Half-life** | Poorly reported in modern open literature; practical BID dosing in trials | **Unknown** precise t½ |
| **Food** | Often taken with food (fat-soluble lore; GI tolerance) | **Speculative** optimization |

### 2.3 Dose ranges in literature vs nootropic culture

| Context | Dose |
|---------|------|
| Marcer 1977 (healthy elderly) | **1200 mg/day** (600 mg BID) × 9 months |
| Oliver 1967 (memory deficits) | **1200 mg/day** (300 mg QID) × 3 weeks |
| Ito 1968 (post-traumatic) | **900 mg/day** × 2 weeks |
| Pek 1989 (organic psychosyndrome / dementia) | **2000 mg/day** × 8 weeks |
| Popa 1994 (AD-type, vs Antagonic-Stress) | **1560 mg/day** × 3 months |
| Nootropic culture | Often **250–1000 mg/day**, split; stack with racetams |
| Capsules OTC | Commonly **200–300 mg** unit dose |

## 4. Clinical evidence (full evaluation)

### 4.1 Stratified stack

| Tier | Finding | Limits | Certainty |
|------|---------|--------|-----------|
| **Healthy elderly cognition** | **Marcer & Hopkins 1977** (*Age Ageing*): DB RCT n=74; meclofenoxate **600 mg BID × 9 months**. **Delayed free-recall** better vs placebo (consolidation of new info). **No** effect on immediate free-recall, digit span, recognition, past events, prose. Subjective “alertness / well-being.” | Old; limited test battery; not modern AD composite | **Probable** selective memory signal |
| **Memory deficits (mixed)** | **Oliver & Restell 1967**: n=28 crossover; **1200 mg/day × 3 weeks** — **no** significant drug vs placebo on 8 tests; pre–post changes dominated. Authors: subjects may have been too impaired/heterogeneous. | Short; small | **Null / Speculative** benefit |
| **Post-traumatic syndrome** | **Ito et al. 1968**: n=51 crossover; **900 mg/day × 2 weeks** — ↓ **dizziness** vs placebo; headaches not; memory too rare to analyze. Mild AEs in 8/63-type counts. | Old; short; syndrome-specific | **Speculative–Probable** for dizziness only |
| **Dementia / organic psychosyndrome** | **Pek et al. 1989**: n=50 DB; **2 g/day × 8 weeks**. Intra-individual: 48% CPH vs 28% placebo “improved” memory-ish measures; more worsened on CPH (5 vs 1). Authors optimistic; **statistics weak** (ADDF critique). | High variability; suboptimal analysis | **Inconclusive** |
| **AD-type + combo** | **Popa et al. 1994**: centrophenoxine **1560 mg** alone vs **Antagonic-Stress®** (same CPH + methionine, Mg-aspartate, B vitamins, Zn, etc.). Both improved vs baseline; combo better. **No placebo.** | Practice effects; multi-ingredient | **Cannot attribute** to CPH alone |
| **DMAE analog (related active)** | **Dubois et al. 2012**: V0191 DMAE pyroglutamate **1500 mg/day × 24 weeks**, n=242 prodromal AD — **no** significant cognitive benefit vs placebo; some week-12 trends only. AEs higher on drug; rare serious AEs not fully excludable. | Not identical to CPH ester | Softens enthusiasm for DMAE class in AD |
| **Modern systematic dementia evidence** | No high-quality contemporary meta-analysis establishing CPH as AD standard of care. AChEIs dominate guidelines. | Evidence aged out | Not guideline therapy |
| **Preclinical lifespan** | Hochschild 1973: male mice drinking-water CPH ~60 mg/kg ~10 mo → mean lifespan **+27%**, median **+29%**, max **+26%**; treated mice lighter at end | Diet/metabolic confound possible; single classic study | **Speculative** human longevity |
| **What data do not show** | No modern large Phase 3 for AD prevention; no robust young healthy “IQ boost” RCTs; no proof superior to α-GPC/citicoline for choline delivery; no ongoing US/EU trials (ADDF note) | — | — |

### 4.2 Quality of the evidence base

- Most human trials: **1960s–1990s**.  
- Heterogeneous endpoints, short durations (except Marcer 9 months).  
- Alzheimer’s Drug Discovery Foundation Cognitive Vitality report: **may help a few aspects of cognition; studies old/suboptimal; DMAE safety caveats.**  
- Treat marketing “reverses aging / clears lipofuscin in humans” as **Speculative** extrapolation from animal histology.

### 4.3 Effect-size intuition

| Population | Expected cognitive effect | Certainty |
|------------|---------------------------|-----------|
| Healthy elderly | Small selective memory (delayed recall) | **Probable** (one better trial) |
| Established dementia | Unreliable / mixed | **Inconclusive** |
| Young healthy | Subjective clarity; objective data sparse | **Speculative** |
| Racetam stack support | May reduce “racetam headache” for some | **Speculative** (forum-strong) |

## 6. Desired effects — mechanism depth

### 6.1 Memory consolidation (delayed recall)

- **Phenotype:** Better retention of newly learned material over a delay; not necessarily better short-term span.
- **Pathways:** P2–P3 cholinergic + membrane support; possible arousal (P11).
- **Clinical:** Marcer 1977 primary positive human signal.
- **Certainty: Probable** in healthy elderly at 1200 mg/day long course; **Speculative** generalization.

### 6.2 Alertness / “well-being”

- **Phenotype:** Clearer head, mild energy without classic stimulant crash (user reports).
- **Pathways:** P11; cholinergic cortical activation.
- **Certainty: Probable** as subjective effect in some users/trials.

### 6.3 Racetam stack support

- **Phenotype:** Fewer headaches, better racetam response when choline demand is high.
- **Pathways:** P3 ACh substrate.
- **Clinical:** No formal CPH+racetam RCTs; strong community protocol.
- **Certainty: Speculative** (mechanism-plausible).

### 6.4 Lipofuscin / anti-aging

- **Phenotype:** Theoretical cellular “cleanup.”
- **Pathways:** P4–P5, P7, P12.
- **Clinical:** Human brain lipofuscin reduction not rigorously shown; lifespan only mice.
- **Certainty: Probable** animal histology; **Speculative** human anti-aging outcome.

### 6.5 Dementia treatment

- **Phenotype:** Hoped memory/global cognition improvement.
- **Evidence:** Inconclusive old trials; DMAE analog null in larger modern prodromal AD study.
- **Certainty: Not Established**; do not substitute for standard care.

### 6.6 What it is not

- Not an AChE inhibitor like donepezil.  
- Not a robust acute stimulant like modafinil/amphetamine.  
- Not proven lifespan extension in humans.

## 7. Side effects — mechanism depth + counters

### 7.1 Cholinergic excess (nausea, GI, headache, diarrhea)

1. **Phenotype:** Classic “too much ACh” — nausea, gut cramps, headache, sometimes sweating/salivation.
2. **Mechanism:** P3/P15 — excess peripheral/central cholinergic tone.
3. **Counters**

| Node | Counter | Practice | Certainty |
|------|---------|----------|-----------|
| Excess ACh | **Dose reduce** (e.g. 250–500 mg) | First line | **Probable** |
| Excess ACh | **Pause racetam** or lower racetam | Stack imbalance | **Speculative–Probable** |
| Excess ACh | Rare: anticholinergic is wrong solution for self-experiment | Medical if severe | — |
| Empty stomach GI | Take **with food** | Common advice | **Probable** |

### 7.2 Insomnia / restlessness

1. **Phenotype:** Wired at night if dosed late.
2. **Mechanism:** P11 arousal.
3. **Counters:** Morning / lunch only (matches trial BID breakfast-lunch pattern). **Probable**.

### 7.3 Dizziness, blurred vision

1. **Phenotype:** Occasional in old trauma trial AEs.
2. **Mechanism:** Autonomic/cholinergic or unrelated to head injury in that study.
3. **Counters:** Dose reduce; stop if persistent. **Speculative** frequency.

### 7.4 Hypertension concern

1. **Phenotype:** Product monographs sometimes list severe hypertension as contraindication.
2. **Mechanism:** Poorly detailed in modern literature; treat as labeled caution.
3. **Counters:** Avoid if uncontrolled severe HTN; monitor BP if hypertensive. **Speculative–Probable** prudence.

### 7.5 Pregnancy / teratogenicity (DMAE)

1. **Phenotype:** Neural tube defects in **mouse embryo in vitro** models via blocked choline→PC conversion.
2. **Mechanism:** P13.
3. **Counters:** **Absolute avoid** in pregnancy / trying to conceive; women of childbearing potential should treat as contraindicated unless clinician overrides with data. **Probable** risk signal (preclinical) → conservative human policy.

### 7.6 DMAE-class serious AEs (signal, not proven for CPH)

1. **Phenotype:** Dubois V0191 arm: higher discontinuation; rare cardiorespiratory arrest, cardiac failure death, grand mal — relationship not excluded.
2. **Mechanism:** Unknown; different salt (pyroglutamate) and dose.
3. **Counters:** Do not assume CPH shares those SAEs, but do not assume zero CV/neuro risk either. Prefer medical oversight if cardiac disease. **Speculative** for CPH specifically.

### 7.7 No classic hormonal suppression

Not AAS-like HPTA shutdown. **Established** (class).

## 8. Practical use

| Parameter | Guidance | Certainty |
|-----------|----------|-----------|
| **Solo cognition (elderly experiment)** | **600 mg BID** with meals (Marcer-like), morning + midday; trial **8–12+ weeks** (full 9 months if matching trial) | **Probable** protocol fidelity |
| **Nootropic / racetam stack** | **250–500 mg** 1–2×/day with racetam | **Speculative** efficacy |
| **Upper clinical historical** | Up to **1500–2000 mg/day** in dementia studies | Safety **Probable** short-term in those trials; not needed for most |
| **Timing** | Avoid evening | **Probable** |
| **Cycling** | Optional 5 days on / 2 off or continuous; no strong rebound data | **Unknown** necessity |
| **Form** | Capsules/powder; Lucidryl historical brand | Quality **Unknown** on grey market |

## 10. Monitoring, contraindications, stop criteria

### Monitoring

| Item | Why |
|------|-----|
| Subjective sleep, GI, headache | Primary AEs |
| BP if hypertensive | Labeled caution |
| Cognitive N-of-1 (delayed recall tasks) | Effect tracking |
| Cardiac symptoms | Rare DMAE-class concern |

### Contraindications / high caution

- Pregnancy / planning pregnancy (DMAE teratogen signal)  
- Severe uncontrolled hypertension (product literature)  
- Unstable cardiac disease without medical advice  
- Known severe cholinergic intolerance  

### Stop if

- Persistent insomnia, severe headache, vomiting  
- New cardiac or neurologic events  
- No benefit after adequate trial at reasonable dose
