# Methods Appraisal — Full Neuroprotection from Trenbolone

**Research ID:** 94cfd27c  
**Role:** Methodologist  
**Scope:** Design quality, bias, statistics, external validity, applicability; whether studies support claims of (or against) *full* neuroprotection against trenbolone-related CNS/neuropsychiatric/sleep/structural harms  
**Certainty framework:** Established / Probable / Speculative / Unknown (per evidence-grading.md)  
**Hierarchy:** source-evaluation.md

---

## 1. Question Framing — Dual PICO + Combination Hypothesis

### 1.1 Harm PICO (problem agent)

| Element | Specification |
|--------|----------------|
| **P** | Adult humans (esp. male weightlifters/gym users) exposed to trenbolone or high-dose AAS; complementary animal/in-vitro models of trenbolone or class AAS |
| **I** | Trenbolone (or stacked AAS including trenbolone) at supra-physiological doses/durations typical of non-medical use |
| **C** | Non-using weightlifters / placebo / vehicle / no AAS; for cell work, untreated cultures |
| **O** | Sleep quality/insomnia; anxiety, aggression, mood, psychopathy-related traits; cognition/executive function; structural MRI (volume, thickness, brain-age gap); cellular viability, neurite outgrowth, mitochondrial function; rare severe outcomes (suicidality, violence, stroke) |

### 1.2 Protection PICO (mitigator set)

| Element | Specification |
|--------|----------------|
| **P** | Same populations under trenbolone/AAS exposure (or models thereof) |
| **I** | Candidate mitigators alone or stacked: dose reduction/cessation; sleep defense (behavioral + GABAergic supports: taurine/Mg/GABA lore); NAC/redox; neurotrophic peptides (cerebrolysin class); clinical psychiatric care (SSRIs, mood stabilizers, etc.) |
| **C** | Trenbolone/AAS exposure **without** co-intervention |
| **O** | Complete or near-complete prevention of the harm outcomes above ("full neuroprotection") |

### 1.3 Combination hypothesis under test

> **H_stack:** A multi-pathway protective stack (sleep + NAC + GABAergic supports ± cerebrolysin ± psychiatric care ± dose reduction) can **fully prevent** trenbolone neurotoxicity and neuropsychiatric harm.

**Critical methodological implication:** Overall certainty for H_stack is limited by the **weakest critical link**, not the average of optimistic single-agent findings. Absence of combination RCTs is not proof of null, but it caps causal claims at **Speculative/Unknown** for "full" prevention.

### 1.4 Construct of "full neuroprotection"

Operationally, "full" requires:

1. Prevention across **multiple domains** (sleep + affect/aggression + structure/cognition + cell integrity), not one surrogate.  
2. Effect sizes large enough that residual risk is clinically negligible.  
3. Direct evidence under **trenbolone** (or strong, validated transfer from class AAS).  

No included human study measures protective co-intervention against trenbolone. Preclinical work does not dose-map to gym protocols. Therefore the claim of *full* neuroprotection is **not currently testable at Established/Probable** level; methods must grade partial pathway mitigation separately from the absolute claim.

## 2. Design Appraisal — Per Key Study

### 2.1 Scarth & Bjørnebekk 2021 — *Androgen abuse and the brain* (PMID:34709215; narrative/PRISMA-guided review)

| Domain | Appraisal |
|--------|-----------|
| **Design** | Structured narrative review with PRISMA screening of PubMed (post–Sep 2019 → Mar 2021); 16 studies (11 animal, 5 human incl. 1 case). **Not** a quantitative meta-analysis of neuroprotection. |
| **Strengths** | Transparent selection; dual screening; separates brain-health vs aggression themes; flags AR-independent toxicity (cites Zelleroth); notes therapeutic vs supra-physiological dose distinction. |
| **Limitations** | Short look-back; heterogeneous outcomes; no pooled effect sizes for imaging or aggression; human n small and largely Oslo-program cross-sectional; psychiatric-only outcomes excluded by design (may under-weight mood literature). |
| **What it supports** | **Probable:** multi-modal signal that prolonged high-dose AAS is associated with adverse neurobiological and cognitive findings. **Does not support:** any protective stack, trenbolone-specific human dosing, or "full" prevention. |
| **GRADE-like** | Review synthesis of mostly Low–Very Low primary evidence → **Low** for causal human brain harm; **Very Low** for mitigator claims (not addressed). |

### 2.2 Zelleroth et al. 2021 — Primary rat cortical cultures; trenbolone worst for viability/mitochondria (PMID:33677017)

| Domain | Appraisal |
|--------|-----------|
| **Design** | In-vitro; immature (1–4 DIV) mixed neuronal/glial primary cortical cultures from E17 Wistar; repeated 24 h × 3 days exposure to testosterone, nandrolone, stanozolol, trenbolone at **10 / 30 / 100 µM** ± flutamide 10 µM; outcomes: neurite length/neuron, neuron count, MTT mitochondrial function, LDH, Tubb3 mRNA. n cultures typically 3–6; ANOVA + Dunnett. |
| **Key observed effects (not human effect sizes)** | Highest dose (100 µM): **trenbolone** markedly reduced neurite length (ANOVA F(6,35)=16.03, p<0.0001), neuron number (F(6,35)=2.806, p=0.0246), MTT (F(6,21)=4.901, p=0.0028), and Tubb3 mRNA (F(4,10)=76.13, p<0.0001). Testosterone and nandrolone reduced neurite length at 100 µM only; stanozolol null on these endpoints. Flutamide did **not** restore neurite length or Tubb3; partial rescue of neuron count/MTT only. |
| **Strengths** | Head-to-head structural AAS comparison; AR-antagonist arm; multi-endpoint toxicity; concentration–response. |
| **Critical limits** | **Preclinical ≠ human dosing.** 10–100 µM are supra-pharmacological bath concentrations chosen to mimic "high-dose illicit" literature; authors explicitly state they **cannot** be read as brain concentrations under real use. Immature embryonic cultures ≠ adult human cortex. No blood–brain barrier, no stacking, no sleep/psych context. Short exposure (3 days). Statistical assumption of normality; small biological n. |
| **What authors claim vs data** | Claim of "possible" contribution to behavioral alterations in users is mechanistic inference — appropriate as **Speculative** for human outcomes. "Trenbolone worst" is **Established within this assay**, not Established for human neurotoxicity ranking. |
| **GRADE-like** | **Very Low** for human clinical inference; useful **mechanistic context only** (redox/mitochondria/neurite vulnerability; AR-independent component). **No mitigator dosing advice** can be derived. |

### 2.3 Bjørnebekk et al. 2017 — Structural brain imaging of long-term AAS (PMID:27616036) + Oslo program extensions (brain-age gap)

| Domain | Appraisal |
|--------|-----------|
| **Design** | Observational MRI; male weightlifters with prolonged AAS vs non-using weightlifters. 2017 structural paper: negative correlations of AAS use with brain volume and cortical thickness. Related work: brain-age gap — T1 MPRAGE; ML brain-age models trained on independent males; AAS n≈130 vs WLC n≈99 cross-sectional; longitudinal subset n≈76, mean interval ~3.5 y. |
| **Strengths** | Largest systematic AAS neuroimaging program; weightlifting-matched controls reduce pure fitness confounding; duration/dependence analyses; longitudinal component strengthens temporal argument beyond pure cross-section. |
| **Limitations** | **Non-randomized; self-reported exposure**; polypharmacy (other AAS, stimulants, opioids common — see polypharmacy metasynthesis); selection into AAS use (personality, prior ADHD/psych); IQ/education differences often favor controls; multiple brain regions → multiplicity risk if not tightly controlled; brain-age is a **composite surrogate**, not a clinical dementia diagnosis. Trenbolone-specific subsample analyses generally underpowered or absent. |
| **Causation ceiling** | Bradford Hill partially met (consistency within program, dose/duration gradients, biological plausibility, some longitudinal change) → causal inference for **class AAS structural burden** reaches **Probable** at best, not Established. Reverse causation and residual confounding remain live. |
| **GRADE-like** | **Low–Moderate** observational for association of long-term AAS with thinner cortex / higher brain-age gap; **Low** for trenbolone-specific structural harm; **does not address protection**. |

### 2.4 Klonteig, Scarth, Bjørnebekk 2024 — Sleep pathology & AAS (PMID:38254047)

| Domain | Appraisal |
|--------|-----------|
| **Design** | Cross-sectional (+ phase-aware cycling subsample) comparison of male AAS-using weightlifters vs WLC; PSQI, Jenkins Sleep Scale, self-reported side effects, sleep med use; linear mixed models for AAS use/withdrawal vs sleep quality. |
| **Key magnitudes (reported)** | ~**66%** of AAS group reported sleep problems as side effect; **38%** used sleep medication; groups differed on depression/anxiety (HSCL higher in AAS). Strong PSQI–JSS correlation (rho≈0.72). About half of users historically report insomnia as subjective side effect (cited prior). |
| **Strengths** | Direct sleep focus; validated questionnaires; same research group/population as imaging work (coherent program); withdrawal phase attention. |
| **Limitations** | Self-report sleep (limited polysomnography in this sample); confounding by anxiety/depression, stimulants, training timing, night sweats; no randomized sleep intervention; **no trenbolone-only arm** (though tren is common in real stacks and forum sleep lore). Cannot isolate whether sleep pathology is primary AAS effect vs secondary to mood/CV/night sweats. |
| **GRADE-like** | **Low–Moderate** for association AAS ↔ poorer sleep in weightlifters; **Very Low** for any specific sleep-stack efficacy against trenbolone. |

### 2.5 Nelson et al. 2022 — AAS, psychopathy, risk-taking, anger (Sci Rep)

| Domain | Appraisal |
|--------|-----------|
| **Design** | Cross-sectional survey/comparison of bodybuilders with vs without prior AAS history; odds of psychopathic traits, sexual/substance risk-taking, anger problems. |
| **Strengths** | Multi-domain behavioral outcomes beyond "aggression only"; peer-reviewed. |
| **Limitations** | **Self-selection** into bodybuilding and into AAS; psychopathy/anger may **precede** use (confounding by indication); no randomized exposure; no biomarker confirmation of AAS; social desirability; cross-sectional → directionality opaque. Effect estimates are **associations (ORs)**, not experimental causal effects of trenbolone. |
| **GRADE-like** | **Low** observational association; **does not prove** AAS *induces* psychopathy; **does not test** neuroprotection. |

### 2.6 Piacentino et al. 2015 — AAS & psychopathology in athletes (PMC4462035)

| Domain | Appraisal |
|--------|-----------|
| **Design** | Systematic review of AAS use and psychopathology in athletes (mood, anxiety, somatoform/eating, psychosis-spectrum, aggression). |
| **Strengths** | Broad clinical map; distinguishes on-cycle hypomania vs withdrawal depression pattern; acknowledges predisposition. |
| **Limitations** | Heterogeneous primary designs (many case series, convenience samples); limited controlled evidence; publication bias toward dramatic cases; dated relative to Oslo imaging wave; no quantitative synthesis of effect sizes for most domains. |
| **GRADE-like** | **Low** synthesis for association of AAS with mood/anxiety/aggression in predisposed users; conclusions appropriately cautious about reverse causation. |

### 2.7 Chegeni et al. 2021 — AAS administration & self-reported aggression meta-analysis (RCTs)

| Domain | Appraisal |
|--------|-----------|
| **Design** | Systematic review + meta-analysis of **RCTs** of AAS administration in healthy males; self-reported and observer-reported aggression. |
| **Effect sizes (demand these)** | After outlier exclusion: self-reported aggression **Hedges' g ≈ 0.171** (95% CI 0.029–0.312; k=11; p=0.018) random-effects — **small**. Acute AAS self-report (fixed-effect): **g ≈ 0.291** (0.014–0.524). **Not** replicated for observer-rated aggression; **not** significant when restricted to higher doses (>500 mg) and longer regimens (3 days–14 weeks) in the authors' restricted analyses. |
| **Strengths** | Highest design tier available for aggression (experimental AAS in humans); pre-specified meta approach; reports CI and sensitivity. |
| **Limitations** | Laboratory doses/regimens often **far below** gym trenbolone stacks; ethical RCTs cannot deliver multi-week 19-nor stacks at bodybuilding doses; self-report vs observer discordance; small k; outlier handling affects estimate; ecological validity low for "roid rage" narratives. |
| **What this supports** | **Probable** small average increase in **self-reported** aggression under controlled AAS in healthy men. **Does not** establish large, inevitable violent aggression; **does not** isolate trenbolone; **does not** test NAC/cerebrolysin/sleep stacks. |
| **GRADE-like** | **Moderate** for small self-report effect in RCTs; **Low** for real-world violence; external validity downgrade for gym trenbolone. |

### 2.8 NAC reviews (Tenório 2021; Fan 2020; Cherneva 2025 class) — standalone redox/CNS

| Domain | Appraisal |
|--------|-----------|
| **Design** | Reviews of NAC antioxidant/GSH precursor effects; some hippocampal oxidative stress models; clinical CNS uses (addiction, psych, etc.) separate from AAS. |
| **Strengths** | Plausible pathway match to AAS-linked oxidative stress/inflammation (animal ND/AAS literature). |
| **Limitations** | **Zero identified RCTs of NAC + trenbolone** (or NAC + any AAS) for neuroprotection. Human CNS NAC trials use different indications, doses, and endpoints. Preclinical rescue in non-AAS models does not transfer to "full neuroprotection from tren." BBB/CNS exposure and dosing for gym users unvalidated for this indication. |
| **GRADE-like** | **Probable/Established** for NAC as GSH precursor and for some psychiatric/addiction adjunct uses (indication-specific); **Speculative** for AAS/trenbolone neuroprotection; **Unknown** for "full" prevention. |

### 2.9 Cerebrolysin findings — neurotrophic peptide class

| Domain | Appraisal |
|--------|-----------|
| **Design** | Clinical literature concentrated in **stroke/TBI** (and related neurodegeneration contexts); not AAS. |
| **Limitations** | **No AAS or trenbolone protective trials.** Extrapolating neurotrophic support from acute brain injury to chronic androgen neurotoxicity is pathway analogy only. Parenteral peptide logistics, regulatory status, and quality issues further limit gym applicability. |
| **GRADE-like** | **Speculative** mechanistic overlay; **Unknown** as trenbolone mitigator; **cannot** support "full neuroprotection" claims. |

### 2.10 Case series / case reports (Thiblin suicide series; testosterone+trenbolone violent suicide attempt; Viegas fluoxetine after AAS-related attempt)

| Domain | Appraisal |
|--------|-----------|
| **Design** | Case series / single cases. |
| **Role** | Signal generation for severe psychiatric outcomes and possible psychiatric treatment responsiveness. |
| **Limits** | No denominator; selection of extreme outcomes; polypharmacy; cannot estimate risk or prove drug-specific causality; fluoxetine response in one context ≠ prevention protocol. |
| **GRADE-like** | **Very Low**; supports clinical vigilance, not population effect sizes or full prevention. |

## 4. Statistical Adequacy

| Evidence stream | Adequacy | Notes |
|-----------------|----------|-------|
| **Chegeni aggression MA** | Acceptable for small effects | Reports Hedges' g + 95% CI; k=11 after outlier; random-effects appropriate. Power limited for moderator analyses (dose, duration). Observer-null result undercuts large "roid rage" effect claims. |
| **Zelleroth** | Adequate for in-vitro ANOVA | F-tests + Dunnett; SEM; ROUT outliers. **No standardized effect sizes (e.g., Cohen's d) reported in abstract/results narrative** — percent-of-control bars dominate. Biological replicate n small (3–6). Assumptions of normality stated, not proven. |
| **Oslo MRI / brain-age** | Mixed | Large-n for rare population; LME for longitudinal BAG appropriate. Multiple regions require strong multiplicity control — verify in primary papers before treating every regional finding as confirmatory. Brain-age prediction error from training set transfers uncertainty into BAG. |
| **Klonteig sleep** | Descriptive + LMM | Prevalence % useful; causal effect sizes of "AAS → ΔPSQI" less cleanly isolated. Spearman rho for scale concordance good. |
| **Nelson / Piacentino** | Association-level | ORs/prevalence without experimental identification; underpowered for dose–response of psychiatric symptoms (Amaral 2022 notes unclear dose association). |
| **Protective stack** | **Absent** | No sample size, no power, no CI for NAC+tren, cerebrolysin+tren, or multi-agent prevention of any primary neuro endpoint. |

**Rule applied:** Demand effect sizes. Where only p-values or "significant association" appear without magnitude, claims of clinical importance are **not supported**. A g≈0.17 aggression effect is **not** evidence of catastrophic inevitable violence; MTT reductions at 100 µM are **not** human dose–response curves.

## 5. External Validity

### 5.1 Gym vs clinical / laboratory

| Setting | Match to gym trenbolone user |
|---------|------------------------------|
| **Oslo weightlifter AAS cohorts** | **Best available** — same culture of lifting and non-medical AAS; still Norway-specific, heavy long-term users, high polypharmacy. |
| **AAS aggression RCTs** | Poor–fair: healthy volunteers, often short/low-dose testosterone-class protocols; rarely 19-nors at blast doses. |
| **Zelleroth cultures** | Poor for external dose; fair for relative compound ranking **within assay**. |
| **Stroke/TBI cerebrolysin trials** | Poor — different injury biology, care setting, endpoints. |
| **NAC psych/addiction trials** | Fair pathway analogy; poor indication match. |
| **Forums** | High ecological face validity for **symptoms** (insomnia, night sweats, irritability); zero validity for causal efficacy of stacks. |

### 5.2 Trenbolone-specific vs class AAS

| Claim type | Validity |
|------------|----------|
| Class AAS ↔ sleep problems, mood risk, structural associations | **Probable** transfer to users who include tren in stacks |
| Trenbolone uniquely harsher on viability/mitochondria/neurites | **Established in Zelleroth assay**; **Speculative** magnitude in human brain |
| Human MRI/psych findings as "trenbolone effects" | Mostly **class-level** — users stack; few pure-tren longitudinal MRI cells |
| Protective interventions validated on trenbolone | **None** |

**Indirectness downgrade:** Most human brain evidence is **AAS-class**, not trenbolone-isolating. Trenbolone's 19-nor profile, non-aromatization, high AR affinity, and progestogenic activity justify **hypothesis of worse neuropsychiatric burden** but do not license treating class effect sizes as trenbolone-specific effect sizes.

## 6. Clinical / Applied Applicability

| Decision | Methods support? |
|----------|------------------|
| Assume some neuropsychiatric/sleep risk with prolonged high-dose AAS including trenbolone | **Yes — Probable** (multi-design concordance) |
| Rank trenbolone as higher cellular neurotoxicity risk than testosterone/stanozolol in vitro | **Yes — Established in that model**; use only as harm-reduction **hypothesis** in humans |
| Promise "full neuroprotection" via multi-agent stack while continuing trenbolone | **No — Unknown/failed construct**; no combo trials; "full" unmeasured |
| Dose reduction / cessation / shorter exposure as risk mitigation | **Probable** (duration gradients in imaging; exposure dependence in harm literature) — strongest practical lever |
| Sleep defense as partial harm reduction | **Speculative–Probable** for improving sleep itself; **Unknown** whether it prevents structural or affective outcomes |
| NAC for AAS redox | **Speculative** pathway; standard NAC safety literature may support low-stakes trial **as adjunct**, not as proven shield |
| Cerebrolysin as AAS neuroprotectant | **Not applicable** on current evidence; do not translate stroke doses |
| Psychiatric care for acute mood/suicidality | **Probable** clinical standard (case-level response signals); not proven primary prevention of AAS-induced structural change |
| Forum GABA/taurine/antihistamine sleep stacks | **Speculative** practical notes only; never upgrade to Established |

**Harm-reduction framing (methods-consistent):** Any guidance must separate (1) reducing exposure, (2) treating symptoms (sleep, mood), and (3) unproven cellular "protection." Conflating these is a methods error that overstates certainty.

## 7. Conclusion Alignment — Where Authors Outrun Data

| Source | Supported by data | Outruns data |
|--------|-------------------|--------------|
| **Zelleroth 2021** | Trenbolone most toxic in this culture system at 100 µM; AR antagonist incomplete rescue | Bridging "could contribute to behavioural alterations in AAS users" without human PK/PD or adult in-vivo confirmation |
| **Scarth/Bjørnebekk 2021** | Recent multi-method signal of brain impact of high-dose androgens | Occasional tone that brain alterations "**likely** cause" cognitive/emotional deficits — directionality still partly open |
| **Bjørnebekk imaging program** | Association of long-term AAS with volume/thickness/BAG differences | Interpreting BAG as clinical "accelerated aging" diagnosis; attributing all signal to AAS rather than polypharmacy/lifestyle/pre-morbid factors |
| **Klonteig 2024** | High prevalence of self-reported sleep problems in AAS users | Causal isolation of AAS molecule vs confounders |
| **Chegeni 2021** | Small self-report aggression increase in RCTs | Media/"roid rage" generalizations; authors themselves note non-replication on observer and high-dose long-term subsets |
| **Nelson 2022** | Higher odds of anger/risk/psychopathy traits among prior AAS bodybuilders | AAS *caused* psychopathy (vs selection) |
| **Piacentino 2015** | Association AAS–psychopathology in literature | Uniform risk; underplays predisposition and reverse causation (text is relatively careful) |
| **NAC / cerebrolysin discourse (if claimed for tren)** | Standalone mechanisms / other indications | Any claim of validated full or substantial neuroprotection against trenbolone |
| **Forum narratives** | Consistent reports of tren insomnia/night sweats/irritability | Certainty that taurine/GABA/NAC "cancel" tren neurotoxicity |

**Methodologist adjudication:** Literature **supports harm signals** for class AAS (Probable) and trenbolone-leaning cellular toxicity (assay-Established, human-Speculative severity). Literature **does not support** H_stack as Established or Probable. Authors who frame multi-agent stacks as achieving "full neuroprotection" outrun all available designs.

## 8. Anecdotal Evidence Limits (Tren Forums)

| Feature | Methodological status |
|---------|----------------------|
| Consistent multi-source reports: tren → insomnia, night sweats, irritability, "mental sides" | First-class **experiential** evidence; ceiling **Speculative** for causal attribution and for stack efficacy |
| GABA / taurine / glutamine / antihistamine sleep lore | Non-peer-reviewed; uncontrolled; placebo, concurrent dose changes, and cycle phase confounded; **do not upgrade design quality** |
| Claims of "tren is fine if you take X" | Selection bias (survivors post); no objective sleep/MRI/psychometrics; toxic positivity / sponsorship possible |
| Concordance rating | **literature-silent to partial** for symptom *occurrence* (sleep literature partially aligns); **literature-silent** for full neuroprotection stacks |
| Recommendation ceiling | Speculative practical notes (e.g., "users commonly prioritize sleep hygiene and dose reduction when mental sides appear") — **never** Established protocols |

**Rule enforced:** Forums are not Low-quality RCTs; they are **Very Low** design with possible high face validity for *what users feel*, not for *what works to fully protect the brain*.

## 9. Interaction Gaps — Combo Trials vs Single-Agent Pathway Adequacy

### 9.1 What does **not** exist (search/intake)

| Combination | Controlled human evidence | Controlled animal evidence specific to combo |
|-------------|---------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| NAC + trenbolone (neuro endpoints) | **None identified** | **None identified** as primary protection RCTs |
| Cerebrolysin + trenbolone | **None** | **None** |
| Multi-agent "neuro stack" + trenbolone | **None** | **None** |
| Sleep intervention RCT in trenbolone users | **None** | N/A |
| Psychiatric prevention RCT (SSRI prophylactically on-cycle) | **None** for structural/sleep primary outcomes | Limited related animal aggression-modulation (e.g., valproate/GABA LAH work — not clinical prevention) |

### 9.2 Single-agent pathway adequacy (does not fill the gap alone)

| Pathway | Single-agent evidence grade | Fills "full neuroprotection from tren"? |
|---------|----------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Redox / GSH (NAC) | Adequate for general antioxidant biology; some CNS clinical use | **No** — missing AAS interaction trials |
| Neurotrophic support (cerebrolysin) | Stroke/TBI literature only | **No** — wrong indication, no combo |
| GABAergic modulation | Animal AAS-aggression LAH data (valproate, bicuculline reversal); forum sleep lore | **No** — no human sleep RCT on tren; aggression animal ≠ full CNS protection |
| Dose/duration reduction | Best observational gradients (imaging, dependence severity) | **Partial** risk reduction only — not "full" if any exposure remains |
| Psychiatric treatment | Case-level + general psych standards | Treats **syndromes**, does not prove prevention of cellular/structural injury |

### 9.3 Weakest-link rule for H_stack

```
H_stack certainty ≤ min(
  certainty(tren causes multi-domain harm),
  certainty(each mitigator hits a real causal pathway),
  certainty(mitigators combine without antagonism),
  certainty(coverage is complete across domains),
  certainty(effect size ≈ full prevention)
)
```

Even if harm is **Probable** and each pathway is **Speculative–Probable** in isolation, the **joint** claim of complete multi-domain prevention under continued trenbolone exposure remains **Unknown** (no tests) or at best **Speculative** (mechanism-only stacking).

### 9.4 Preclinical ≠ human dosing (re-stated)

- Zelleroth 10–100 µM bath ≠ mg/week injectable trenbolone brain free concentrations.  
- Animal ND/testosterone aggression or BDNF studies ≠ validated human NAC/cerebrolysin regimens for AAS.  
- Any quantitative "protective dose" extrapolated from cell or rodent work into gym practice **violates** evidence-grading rules and must be rejected or labeled pure speculation without recommendation force.

## 10. Summary Verdict (Methods)

| Question | Verdict | Certainty |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| Does prolonged high-dose AAS associate with adverse sleep, mood/aggression signals, and structural brain differences in humans? | Yes, with confounding | **Probable** (class AAS) |
| Is trenbolone harsher than several other AAS on neurites/viability/mitochondria in vitro? | Yes, in Zelleroth 2021 | **Established (assay)**; human severity **Speculative** |
| Is RCT-confirmed aggression increase large? | No — small self-report g≈0.17; observer null | **Probable small effect** |
| Do any studies show **full neuroprotection** from trenbolone via multi-agent stacks? | **No** | Claim **unsupported**; status **Unknown** |
| Can pathway-plausible mitigators (NAC, sleep, GABA supports, cerebrolysin, psych care) be graded as proven shields? | Only as partial, untested-on-tren strategies | **Speculative** (notes) / **Unknown** (full prevention) |
| Strongest evidence-based harm-reduction lever | Reduce dose, duration, and polypharmacy; treat sleep and psychiatric symptoms clinically | **Probable** for risk *reduction*, not full prevention |

**Bottom line for the research program:** Methods do **not** license the claim that full neuroprotection from trenbolone is achievable with current candidate stacks. They **do** license careful, certainty-labeled mapping of harms (class AAS + trenbolone cellular signal) and **Speculative** discussion of partial mitigations, with dose reduction and clinical psychiatric/sleep care outranking untested peptide/antioxidant combinations for practical priority.
