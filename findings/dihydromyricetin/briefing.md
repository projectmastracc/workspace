# Dihydromyricetin (DHM) — Full Research Briefing

**Slug:** `dihydromyricetin` · **Type:** Compound profile · **Research ID:** db625c0a · **Date:** 2026-07-31  
**Also known as:** Ampelopsin, ampeloptin, DMY · **Class:** Dihydroflavonol polyphenol (not a drug)

---

## 1. Executive Card

| Field | Content |
|-------|---------|
| **Compound** | Dihydromyricetin (DHM) — major flavonoid from *Ampelopsis grossedentata* (vine tea / rattan tea) and *Hovenia dulcis* (Japanese raisin tree). CAS 27200-12-0; C₁₅H₁₂O₈. |
| **Primary use cases** | Hangover / alcohol “come-down” mitigation (dominant consumer use); alcohol-associated and metabolic liver support; experimental metabolic/anti-inflammatory stacks; emerging interest in alcohol use disorder (AUD) biology and neuroinflammation (preclinical). |
| **Bottom line** | DHM is a multi-target plant flavonoid with **strong preclinical** data for counteracting ethanol intoxication at GABA_A receptors (Shen 2012), hepatic AMPK/SIRT1/PGC-1α mitochondrial rescue in alcohol models, and broad antioxidant/anti-inflammatory actions (Nrf2, NF-κB). **Human evidence is thin but real:** a small NAFLD RCT (Chen 2015: ~**600 mg/day** × 3 months) improved enzymes, glucose, LDL, HOMA-IR, and inflammatory markers without changing ultrasound steatosis or body weight; a T2DM vine-tea study (Ran 2019) improved fasting glucose. **No rigorous peer-reviewed RCTs prove hangover efficacy** despite market saturation (Verster 2021: hangover products including DHM lack published safety/efficacy data). **Oral bioavailability is very poor (~4% in rats)** — short half-life, rapid conjugation — so many animal CNS effects used IP dosing that consumers do not replicate. Short-term human use looks well tolerated (LiverTox likelihood **E** — unlikely DILI). Practical role: optional liver/metabolic adjunct and **plausible but unproven** hangover aid; not a license to drink more. |
| **Biggest upsides** | Mechanistic coherence for alcohol + liver (GABA_A + hepatic metabolism/mito/inflammation) — **Probable** preclinical. Metabolic enzyme/glucose signals in small human trials — **Probable**. Favorable short-term safety signal — **Probable**. Cheap OTC. |
| **Biggest downsides** | Hangover claims **Speculative** in humans. Bioavailability bottleneck may explain “works for some, nothing for others.” Product purity/dose often unlabeled (Verster). Does not replace medical care for AUD or liver disease. Possible CYP interaction potential with better formulations — **Unknown–Speculative**. |
| **Stack role** | Situational alcohol-night tool (with NAC, electrolytes, sleep — pathway-matched) and/or daily metabolic polyphenol at 300–600+ mg if chasing liver/glucose markers; not a PED or nootropic cornerstone. |

**Certainty:** **Established** · **Probable** · **Speculative** · **Unknown**

## 2. Chemistry, classification, PK

### 2.1 Identity

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Structure** | Dihydroflavonol (polyphenolic hydroxy dihydroflavonol); many phenolic –OH groups |
| **Sources** | *A. grossedentata* (high DHM content in vine tea), *H. dulcis*, some *Pinus*/*Cedrus*; isolated “DHM” capsules |
| **Related** | Not the same as milk thistle (silymarin) or quercetin, though mechanism families overlap |
| **Regulatory** | Dietary supplement / TCM-derived ingredient in US; not FDA-approved for any disease |

### 2.2 Pharmacokinetics — the central practical constraint

| Parameter | Data | Certainty |
|-----------|------|-----------|
| **Oral absolute bioavailability (rat)** | ~**4.02%** (Liu et al. LC-MS/MS) | **Established** (rodent) |
| **t½** | IV ~2 h; oral ~3–4 h (rat) | **Probable** (rodent) |
| **Tmax oral** | ~2–3 h (rat suspension); formulation-dependent | **Probable** |
| **Absorption limits** | High polar surface area (~147), poor permeability, phase II conjugation of OH groups | **Established** chemistry |
| **Human PK** | Controlled published human PK sparse; **NCT05623501** Phase 1 dose-escalation designed for safety/PK/MTD (alcohol-associated liver disease context) — interpret as evidence gap being actively closed | **Unknown** (open literature) |
| **CNS exposure** | Landmark GABA_A alcohol work used **IP** DHM; oral CNS levels uncertain | **Speculative** for oral hangover capsules |
| **Enhanced delivery** | Liposomes, SEDDS, micelles, nano: multi-fold AUC gains (e.g. SEDDS ~4× AUC in mice); verapamil co-admin modestly raised F in rats | **Probable** (preclinical tech) |

**Implication:** Standard powder capsules may under-deliver systemic (especially brain) levels relative to Shen-style experiments. Liver exposure via portal first-pass may still matter for metabolic claims even when systemic F is low — **Speculative** but mechanistically plausible for NAFLD/alcohol liver endpoints.

### 2.3 Typical consumer / trial doses

| Context | Dose | Notes |
|---------|------|-------|
| LiverTox “typical” | **300–1000 mg/day** | Product range |
| Chen 2015 NAFLD | **150 mg × 2 capsules BID** = **600 mg/day** × 12 weeks | Best human metabolic RCT |
| Ran 2019 T2DM (vine tea extract) | **~760 mg** DHM-related once daily × 1 month | Glycemic signal |
| Hangover products | Often **300–600+ mg** pre/during/post drinking; many labels **omit** actual DHM mg (Verster) | Quality risk |
| Forums | 300–1000 mg night-of; some 150–300 mg | Anecdote |

## 4. Clinical evidence (full evaluation)

### 4.1 Stratified evidence

| Tier | Finding | Limits | Certainty |
|------|---------|--------|-----------|
| **Hangover / intoxication human RCTs** | **None of high quality** showing DHM alone prevents or treats hangover. Verster 2021 surveyed 82 hangover products (DHM in ~48%): **no peer-reviewed human efficacy/safety data** for any product evaluated; many omit DHM dose. | Market >> evidence | Hangover benefit **Speculative** |
| **NAFLD RCT** | **Chen et al., 2015** (*Pharmacol Res*): n=60 adults with NAFLD; DHM **150 mg capsules, two BID** (**600 mg/day**) vs placebo × **3 months**. Vs placebo: ↓ ALT, AST, GGT; ↓ glucose, LDL-C, apoB; ↓ HOMA-IR; ↓ TNF-α, CK-18, FGF21. **No** change in body weight; ultrasound steatosis improved **similarly** both arms. **No AEs** reported either arm. | Small; single center; imaging endpoint null between groups; short | Metabolic labs **Probable**; steatosis cure **not shown** |
| **T2DM / vine tea** | **Ran et al., 2019** (*Eur J Clin Nutr*): n=80 T2DM; ~**760 mg** DHM (or extract standardized) daily × 1 month → fasting glucose ↓ vs placebo; lipids/insulin largely unchanged; “no adverse reactions.” | Short; concurrent meds; extract vs pure | Glycemic **Speculative–Probable** |
| **Longer DHM supplement combos** | Later dietary-supplement trials combining DHM with vitamins (e.g. 150 mg DHM per tablet, 600 mg/day total in multi-ingredient designs) report enzyme/glucose signals — confounded | Multi-ingredient | **Speculative** for pure DHM attribution |
| **Phase 1 modern** | **NCT05623501**: open-label dose-escalation safety/PK/MTD healthy volunteers, purified cGMP DHM — addresses “no controlled human PK/optimal dosing published” gap stated by investigators | Results depend on publication status at read time | **Unknown** until published |
| **Landmark preclinical alcohol** | **Shen et al., 2012** (*J Neurosci*): DHM counters EtOH intoxication, prevents GABA_A plasticity of dependence, reduces withdrawal anxiety-like behavior, reduces voluntary EtOH in rats — **GABA_A BZD-site related** | **IP** route; not hangover RCT | Mechanism **Established** rodent |
| **Alcoholic liver preclinical** | Silva 2020/2021: less steatosis, lipid rewiring, enhanced EtOH metabolism claims, mito AMPK/SIRT1/PGC-1α | Mouse | **Probable** animal hepatoprotection |
| **Negative / null metabolism** | **Skotnicová et al., 2020**: rat in vivo/in vitro — **no** clear effect of DHM on alcohol metabolism / limited hepatoprotection in their assays | Contradicts some positive metabolism claims | Metabolism boost **not Established** |
| **Safety / hepatotoxicity** | **LiverTox (2023)**: Likelihood score **E** (unlikely cause of clinically apparent liver injury); no case series linking pure DHM to jaundice/enzyme injury; short human trials quiet on AEs | Low systematic pharmacovigilance for supplements | Short-term safety **Probable** |
| **What is not shown** | No Phase 3 disease claims; no AUD abstinence RCTs; no BAC-controlled hangover RCTs; no long-term CV outcomes | — | — |

### 4.2 Effect-size snapshot (Chen 2015 — directional)

At **600 mg/day × 12 weeks** in NAFLD (vs placebo): clinically meaningful **laboratory** improvements in liver enzymes and metabolic markers; **not** a proven imaging-proven fat regression superior to placebo. Treat as signal-generating, not guideline therapy.

### 4.3 Funding / product COI

Hangover industry heavily markets DHM with TCM story + Shen paper. Separate academic NAFLD/T2DM trials exist. Always check whether a “DHM study” is pure compound vs multi-herb blend.

## 6. Desired effects — mechanism depth

### 6.1 Hangover mitigation / anti-intoxication

- **Phenotype:** Less next-day headache, nausea, anxiety, cognitive fog; sometimes less acute drunkenness.
- **Pathways:** P1 (primary CNS claim), P2 (acetaldehyde/EtOH), P6–P7 (inflammation), sleep not fixed by DHM.
- **Chain:** EtOH positively modulates GABA_A → intoxication + plasticity of dependence. DHM in rodents **competes/modulates** at BZD-sensitive GABA_A sites → blunts intoxication and withdrawal-like states (Shen). Separately, hepatic antioxidant/inflam programs may reduce cytokine contribution to “hangover sickness.”
- **Clinical:** Human hangover RCTs lacking. **Certainty: Speculative** for oral OTC use.
- **Forum:** Split but popular.
- **Practical:** If used: **with** food/fat optional for absorption experiments; dose **before and after** drinking per product lore; **do not** increase alcohol intake; combine electrolytes + sleep (hit non-DHM hangover nodes).

### 6.2 Alcoholic / chemical liver protection

- **Phenotype:** Better enzymes, less steatosis in models; human data mainly NAFLD labs.
- **Pathways:** P3–P7, P12.
- **Clinical:** Chen NAFLD labs **Probable**; pure ALD human outcome trials insufficient. Phase 1 ALD-oriented PK ongoing (NCT05623501).
- **Certainty: Probable** preclinical; **Speculative–Probable** human enzyme support.

### 6.3 Metabolic syndrome / NAFLD markers

- **Phenotype:** Lower ALT/AST/GGT, glucose, LDL, HOMA-IR, inflammatory biomarkers.
- **Pathways:** P3, P4, P9, P10, P7.
- **Clinical:** Chen 2015 primary support. Imaging fat not superior to placebo.
- **Certainty: Probable** for labs at ~600 mg/day × 12 weeks in that population.

### 6.4 Glycemic control (T2DM adjunct)

- **Ran 2019** fasting glucose signal short-term. **Certainty: Speculative–Probable**. Not a metformin replacement.

### 6.5 Neuroinflammation / cognition / anxiety

- Preclinical AD, LPS, social isolation models (P8, P13). Human cognition: **Unknown**. Alzheimer’s Drug Discovery Foundation-style reviews: interesting mechanisms, limited clinical translation.

### 6.6 AUD / reduced drinking

- Shen voluntary drinking reduction in rats. Human: **Unknown**. Do not market as AUD cure.

## 7. Side effects — mechanism depth + counters

### 7.1 Apparent lack of acute toxicity (short term)

1. **Phenotype:** Trials report few/no AEs; LiverTox E.
2. **Mechanism:** Partly **low oral bioavailability** may mask on-target sides that appear if F is engineered upward (AlzDiscovery caution).
3. **Counters / notes:** Prefer reputable purity; treat “no sides” as incomplete, not proven lifelong safety. **Certainty: Probable** short-term benign at 300–600 mg.

### 7.2 Gastrointestinal discomfort

1. **Phenotype:** Occasional nausea, loose stool (polyphenol pattern) — forums low–moderate.
2. **Mechanism:** Local GI irritation / microbiome shift (P12).
3. **Counters**

| Node | Counter | Certainty |
|------|---------|-----------|
| Local irritation | Take with food | **Probable** |
| High single bolus | Split dose | **Speculative** |
| Sensitivity | Lower to 150–300 mg | **Probable** |

### 7.3 Behavioral risk — more alcohol

1. **Phenotype:** User feels “protected,” drinks more → worse net harm.
2. **Mechanism:** Not pharmacologic toxicity; risk compensation (especially if GABA_A blunting is real).
3. **Counters:** Explicit rule: **DHM does not increase safe drinking limit**; set drink cap before dosing. **Probable** behavioral risk.

### 7.4 Drug interactions (theoretical)

1. **Phenotype:** Unknown clinically.
2. **Mechanism:** Flavonoid CYP/UGT modulation hypothesized (P15); higher-F formulations amplify risk.
3. **Counters:** Caution with narrow-therapeutic-index CYP substrates; space from meds; medical review if polypharmacy. **Speculative**.

### 7.5 Product contamination / mislabeling

1. **Phenotype:** No effect or unexpected sides from blend (NAC, kava, high niacin in “hangover stacks”).
2. **Counters:** Prefer single-ingredient DHM with elemental dose listed; third-party test when possible. **Probable** market problem (Verster).

### 7.6 Long-term unknown / enhanced-bioavailability products

1. **Phenotype:** None established.
2. **Mechanism:** Chronic high systemic exposure unstudied; mito/SIRT activation is generally adaptive but dose-dependent unknowns.
3. **Counters:** Cycle or use situationally until long-term data exist; treat nano/SEDDS products as higher exposure. **Speculative**.

### 7.7 No classic hormone suppression

Unlike AAS/SARMs — no HPTA shutdown expected. **Established** (class).

## 8. Practical use

| Parameter | Guidance | Certainty |
|-----------|----------|-----------|
| **Hangover situational** | 300–600 mg before drinking + 300–600 mg after (common practice); stay at total ~600–1200 mg night | **Speculative** efficacy |
| **Metabolic / NAFLD-labs oriented** | **~600 mg/day** split (Chen protocol) × 8–12 weeks then reassess enzymes/glucose | **Probable** lab signal in similar patients |
| **Form** | High-purity DHM (≥98% claims); liposomal/SEDDS if seeking higher F (experimental) | Absorption **Probable** better with tech; clinical T still thin |
| **Timing** | With meals for GI; for alcohol: pre-first drink and bedtime | **Speculative** optimization |
| **Duration** | Situational for alcohol; 1–3 months for metabolic experiment | Safety long-term **Unknown** |
| **Beneficial co-stack (hangover)** | Water/electrolytes, sleep, stop drinking earlier, optional **NAC** (acetaldehyde/GSH node — separate evidence), B vitamins if diet poor — **not** megadose niacin | Pathway-matched **Speculative** stack |
| **Risky patterns** | Using DHM to enable binge drinking; stacking multiple untested hepatics without labs | **Probable** harm pattern |
| **Women / pregnancy** | No good reproductive toxicity package for high-dose purified DHM → avoid in pregnancy | **Unknown** safety |

## 10. Monitoring, contraindications, stop criteria

### Monitoring (if using regularly)

| Marker | Why |
|--------|-----|
| ALT/AST/GGT | Metabolic/liver goals; baseline and 8–12 weeks |
| Fasting glucose / HbA1c | If metabolic use |
| Lipids | Chen signal |
| Symptom log | Hangover severity if that is the goal |

### Prefer avoid / caution

- Active severe liver failure without clinician oversight  
- Pregnancy / breastfeeding (data gap)  
- Using as excuse for escalating alcohol  
- Unverified multi-ingredient hangover pills with excessive B3/B9 (Verster: some exceed ULs)

### Stop if

- New jaundice, dark urine, severe RUQ pain  
- Paradoxical worsened anxiety/sedation with CNS depressants  
- No benefit after controlled N-of-1 trials (e.g. 3 nights with vs without)

## 12. Open questions

1. Published **human oral PK** and CNS levels at 300–1000 mg (await/extend NCT05623501).  
2. Placebo-controlled **hangover RCT** with fixed EtOH dose, sleep control, and pure DHM.  
3. Does oral DHM engage GABA_A in humans at supplement doses?  
4. Head-to-head powder vs liposomal/SEDDS on enzymes and subjective hangover.  
5. Long-term safety >6–12 months and CYP interaction panel.  
6. Replication of Chen 2015 with MRI-PDFF steatosis primary endpoint.  
7. Whether DHM reduces real-world alcohol consumption (AUD endpoint).

## 14. Short boundary

This briefing maps mechanisms and evidence for dihydromyricetin as used in supplements and research. It is not medical advice and does not authorize unsafe alcohol use. DHM is not an approved treatment for hangover, AUD, or liver disease in the US; human hangover proof is lacking, and oral bioavailability limits translation from some animal CNS studies.
