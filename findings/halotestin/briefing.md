## 2. Chemistry, Classification, Pharmacokinetics

### Structure and classification
- **IUPAC / common:** 9α-fluoro-11β-hydroxy-17α-methyltestosterone; androst-4-en-3-one, 9-fluoro-11,17-dihydroxy-17-methyl-, (11β,17β)-.
- **Key substituents:**
  - **17α-methyl** → oral activity by retarding first-pass hepatic metabolism; primary driver of **hepatotoxicity class risk** (**Established**; label T1).
  - **9α-fluoro** + **11β-hydroxyl** → corticosteroid-like motif; basis for **11β-HSD2 inhibition** and weak micromolar GR affinity (**Established** enzyme / binding data).
  - Parent is testosterone scaffold (Δ4-3-one, 17β-OH).
- **Classification:** Synthetic androgen and anabolic steroid; AR agonist; 17α-alkylated oral AAS; US CSA Schedule III; ATC G03BA01.
- **Brands / history:** Halotestin, Ultandren, Ora-Testryl; described 1956, medical use ~1957. Brand Halotestin largely discontinued in US; generics historically available. Label explicitly states **not shown safe/effective for athletic enhancement**.

### Pharmacokinetics

| Parameter | Value | Certainty | Source trust |
|-----------|-------|-----------|--------------|
| Route | Oral tablets (historical 2 / 5 / 10 mg) | Established | Label T1 |
| Oral bioavailability | ~**80%** | Established | Label / pharma synthesis |
| t½ | ~**9.2 hours** | Established | Label T1 |
| Protein binding / SHBG | Very low SHBG affinity: **<5% of testosterone, <1% of DHT** → high free fraction | Established | Binding literature (Saartok-class) |
| Metabolism | Hepatic: major **6β-hydroxylation**; 5α- and 5β-reduction; 3α/3β-HSD; 11β-hydroxy oxidation → **11-oxofluoxymesterone**; **5α-dihydrofluoxymesterone** (5α-reductase product in androgenic tissues) | Established | Schänzer T1–T2 |
| Unchanged urine | **<5%** | Established | Label / metabolism |
| Dosing implication of t½ | Split doses (2–4×/day medical label) for more stable levels; community often splits AM/PM or pre-workout load | Established PK / Probable practice | Label + forum T4 |

### Receptor / endocrine character
- **AR agonist** — primary anabolic/androgenic mechanism (**Established**). Absolute AR affinity is relatively modest among 17α-alkylated AAS in some assays; sustained free levels (low SHBG, oral BA, t½) support clinical potency (**Probable** synthesis).
- **5α-reductase substrate** → 5α-dihydrofluoxymesterone in skin, hair follicle, prostate → **amplifies local androgenic phenotype** (**Established** chemistry; **Probable** clinical androgenicity contribution).
- **Non-aromatizable** — C11β-OH steric hindrance blocks aromatase conversion; little/no classic estrogenic fluid/gyno drive from the compound itself (**Established** structure literature; **Probable** for performance dryness). Paradoxical gyno case reports exist (mechanism not estrogen from halo itself — see §7.9).
- **Progestogenic activity:** little/no meaningful PR activity (**Probable**/literature consensus).
- **11β-HSD2:** potent inhibitor — IC₅₀ **60–100 nM** (cell lysates), **~160 nM** intact SW-620, **~530 nM** MCF-7; potency in range of **glycyrrhetinic acid** (licorice) (**Established**, Fürstenberger 2012 T1). No direct MR agonist activity observed; effect is **cortisol-sparing → illicit MR activation**. **Critical methods note:** much weaker inhibition in rat/mouse systems → rodent BP models **cannot** stand in for this off-target.
- **GR:** weak **micromolar** affinity (structural resemblance to corticosteroids) — clinical significance **Speculative**/unclear at human doses.
- **Animal A:A ratios:** often-cited paper values (e.g. ~1900:850-style) and Wikipedia rodent ranges (~1:1–1:15 androgenic:anabolic) are **methodologically invalid for human PE counseling** — assay-dependent, species enzyme mismatch (11β-HSD2), no human hypertrophy RCT. Human phenotype is **strongly androgenic with modest true hypertrophy** relative to strength effect (**Probable** triangulation). **Do not translate rodent ratios.**

## 4. Clinical Evidence — Full Evaluation

**Methods ceiling reminder:** Medical human dose data are relatively informative for exposure and AE architecture (mg overlap with gym use). Performance efficacy data are almost entirely ethnographic. Unique mechanistic gold is 2012 human 11β-HSD2 enzymology — high internal validity, incomplete clinical effect-size bridge.

### 4.1 Label indications and pharmacology (FDA / DailyMed HALOTESTIN SPL — T1)

**Established.**

**Male:**
- Replacement for primary hypogonadism (testicular failure) and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism.
- Delayed puberty (careful titration; bone-age monitoring; limited duration 4–6 months medical framing).
- Typical complete replacement range: **5–20 mg/day** oral, single or divided (3–4) doses.

**Female:**
- Palliation of androgen-responsive recurrent mammary cancer in women **>1 and <5 years postmenopausal**, or hormone-dependent tumor proven by prior castration response.
- Dose: **10–40 mg/day** divided (short action → split dosing for stable levels). Subjective response often ≥1 month; objective 2–3 months.

**Explicit label warnings (core):**
- 17α-alkyl → cholestatic hepatitis/jaundice; prolonged high-dose use associated with **hepatic adenomas, HCC, peliosis hepatis**.
- Edema ± CHF risk with cardiac/renal/hepatic disease; Na/Cl/H₂O/K/Ca/PO₄ retention.
- Polycythemia; lipid adverse shift (“serum cholesterol may increase”); virilization; prostatic hypertrophy/carcinoma concern in elderly males (evidence incomplete).
- Anticoagulant potentiation (clotting factors II, V, VII, X suppressed); diabetes med adjustment; TBG effects.
- CNS label list: libido change, headache, anxiety, depression, paresthesia — **not** athletic “aggression” phenotype language.
- **“This drug has not been shown to be safe and effective for the enhancement of athletic performance.”**
- Contraindications: hypersensitivity; male breast Ca; known/suspected prostate Ca; pregnancy (Cat X); serious cardiac/hepatic/renal disease.
- t½ **~9.2 h** after oral administration.
- Schedule III controlled substance.

**Label limits:** No modern controlled PE data; no quantitative HDL/LDL deltas; no 11β-HSD2 mention; no head-to-head vs injectable T for hypogonadism QoL. Label LH/FSH feedback language is **class template** — conflicts with Jones 1977 and must not be over-read as Halo-specific gonadotropin data (prefer Jones for Halo endocrine dynamics).

### 4.2 Jones TM et al. 1977 — *J Clin Endocrinol Metab* (PMID 137913) — T2

**Established for direction of endocrine dynamics; weak for magnitude precision.**

- n = 9 men; fluoxymesterone **10–30 mg/day × 12 weeks** (+ short-term single-dose arm).
- **Profound plasma testosterone suppression within 24 h**; further reductions over treatment.
- **Modest** suppression of sperm production.
- **No significant change in plasma LH or FSH**.
- Authors propose a **local Leydig-cell effect not mediated by gonadotropins**.

**Methods appraisal:** Open physiologic study; 1970s assays less sensitive than modern LC-MS/MS; small n; no placebo; no PE endpoints. Internal validity good for “suppresses T”; “LH/FSH unchanged → pure local Leydig effect” is **Speculative–Probable** hypothesis only — absence of significant gonadotropin change in n=9 with old assays does not prove zero HPG feedback.

**PE relevance:** Explains why “Halo alone” can feel strong yet leave the user **hypogonadal endogenous-T–wise** almost immediately; supports **test base** logic even on short runs. Spermatogenic impact is real but milder than T crash in this series. Does **not** support dose–response across 5–40 mg athletes, recovery kinetics after 1–4 week pulses, or any performance claims.

### 4.3 Neff MS et al. 1981 — *NEJM* (PMID 7010161) — T1 for domain

**Established for comparative erythropoiesis in hemodialysis anemia only.**

- RCT, maintenance hemodialysis anemia; multi-period structure with attrition (77 → 56 → 35 across periods).
- Compared **nandrolone decanoate**, **testosterone enanthate** (injectables) vs **oxymetholone**, **fluoxymesterone** (orals).
- **Injectables clearly superior** for % responders and mean hematocrit rise (~half of patients ≥5-point HCT rise on injectable androgen).
- Transfusion-dependent and bilaterally nephrectomized patients did not respond well.

**PE relevance:** Fluoxymesterone is a **real but second-tier erythropoietic androgen** clinically; polycythemia remains a monitoring item on longer/stacked PE exposure. **Cannot** support “Halo is safe for HCT in gym users” or PE polycythemia ranking vs Deca. External validity to healthy lifters is weak (uremia, iron, residual EPO dominate).

### 4.4 Breast cancer clinical program — T1–T2 oncology

**Established medical efficacy in selected advanced disease; not PE.**

| Study | Design / dose | Key result | Certainty |
|-------|---------------|------------|-----------|
| **Manni A et al. 1981** *Cancer* (PMID 7296499) | Fluoxymesterone **10 mg BID** after tamoxifen (± hypophysectomy); Stage IV; n=33 | **39% objective remissions**; mean duration ~11+ months; responses even after TAM + hypophysectomy → argues **direct tumor-level** androgen action | Established |
| **Ingle JN et al.** (1988/1991 metastatic RCT TAM±FLU; **2006** adjuvant TAM±FLU early-stage PMID 16538529) | Randomized TAM alone vs TAM+FLU | Metastatic combination questions historical; adjuvant early-stage addition studied with toxicity tradeoffs — not modern standard care | Established as historical RCT data; PE irrelevant |
| **Kono M et al. 2016** (PMID 27663436) | ER+ MBC after contemporary hormonal therapy | Clinical benefit rate **43%** (CR 3%, PR 10%, SD≥6 mo 30%); median PFS **3.9 months**; AR IHC not clearly predictive in this series | Established contemporary salvage signal |

**PE translation:** Doses **10–40 mg/day** overlap gym lore — **favorable external validity for exposure/AE architecture**; host physiology (postmenopausal women, cancer, concurrent endocrine Rx) ≠ male athlete stack for efficacy claims. Supports virilization liability in women (**Established**).

### 4.5 Fürstenberger C et al. 2012 — *Toxicol Sci* (PMID 22273746) — T1 enzyme

**Established molecular uniqueness.**

- Fluoxymesterone = **potent human 11β-HSD2 inhibitor** among AAS screened.
- IC₅₀ 60–100 nM lysates; 160 nM SW-620; 530 nM MCF-7.
- Medium inhibitors: oxymetholone, oxymesterone, danazol, testosterone — **fluoxymesterone clearly stronger**.
- **No direct MR modulation** in their assays.
- **Species note:** much weaker in rat/mouse — do not dismiss risk from rodent models.
- Implication: cortisol-driven MR activation → electrolyte disturbance, **HTN, CVD contribution**.

This is the key paper linking Halo’s **9α-F / 11β-OH** chemistry to a side-effect pathway that other popular orals (Anavar, Winstrol, Anadrol) do **not** share at the same potency. Human ambulatory BP/cortisol:cortisone ratio studies at 10–40 mg are **lacking** → clinical contribution ceiling = **Probable**, not Established effect size.

### 4.6 Metabolism / doping science
- **Schänzer** and related work (T1–T2): 6β-hydroxylation as major metabolic route for 17α-methyl AAS including fluoxymesterone; multi-step reduction/oxidation pathways; urinary detection via GC-MS of metabolites/TMS derivatives (**Established** analytical chemistry).
- Long detection windows typical of 17α-alkyl metabolites in sport testing (**Probable** operational doping risk — compound-specific window depends on lab/method).

### 4.7 Hepatotoxicity — class clinical data (LiverTox T2–T3 + label T1)
**Established for 17α-alkylated AAS as a class; Halo is a member.**

- Cholestatic DILI / jaundice — dose- and duration-related; often reversible on cessation if caught early.
- Peliosis hepatis, hepatic adenoma, HCC — rare but labeled and documented across 17α-alkyl agents with prolonged/high exposure.
- Mechanism of 17α-alkyl hepatotoxicity still incompletely solved (oxidative stress / transporter / AR hepatic hypotheses — **Speculative** detail; **Established** association).
- Halo-specific incidence at 20 mg × 14 days in healthy males = **Unknown** (nonzero, not zero). Relative harshness rank vs Anadrol at PE doses = **Speculative** (forum ordinal folklore).

### 4.8 What clinical evidence does **NOT** show
- **No modern RCTs of fluoxymesterone for athletic performance, strength PRs, body composition, or powerlifting outcomes.**
- No high-quality controlled data quantifying “hardness,” aggression scales, or dose–response for PE endpoints in healthy athletes.
- Inferiority as first-line male hypogonadism therapy vs modern testosterone formulations is clinical practice consensus (**Probable**/guideline-level).
- Animal A:A ratios do **not** transfer cleanly to human PE phenotype (**Invalid** for protocol design).
- Exact mmHg BP rise at community doses; TUDCA/NAC outcome efficacy for AAS DILI prevention; modern eugonadal male free-drug vs 11β-HSD2 IC₅₀ bridging.

**Evidence hierarchy for PE claims:** Forum/anecdotal **Probable** for short-term strength/hardness/aggression; clinical **Established** for suppression, hepatotox class risk, 11β-HSD2 enzyme, medical doses; athletic RCT = effectively **absent (Unknown / not demonstrated)**.

## 6. Desired Effects — Mechanism Depth

### 6.1 Explosive strength / force production
- **AR in muscle (P1a)** → myosin/contractile protein support, possible fiber-level force (**Established** AR biology; magnitude for Halo vs other AAS **not** RCT-quantified).
- **CNS / neuromuscular (P1b)** contribution likely dominant for the “same-week PR” lore: AR in CNS, altered effort tolerance, aggression-linked motor unit recruitment (**Probable** phenotype; **Speculative** pathway detail).
- **Low SHBG + oral BA + t½ (P9)** → rapid rise in free androgenic signal (**Established** PK).
- **Not** primarily a glycogen-supercompensation mass agent (contrast high-dose Anadrol lore).
- **Certainty ceiling:** **Probable** community-consensus phenomenology; **Speculative** as isolated drug effect size; cannot reach Established without PE RCT.

### 6.2 Muscle hardness / density / dry look
- **Non-aromatizing (P3)** → less subcutaneous water from E2 (**Established** structure; **Probable** appearance contribution).
- Possible **intramuscular water/glycogen redistribution (P13)** without scale mass (**Speculative**).
- Low body fat is a prerequisite for “granite” look — Halo does not melt fat by itself (**Probable**).
- Stack context: often with Masteron/Tren/Winny in contest lore for additive dryness (**forum Probable**).

### 6.3 Aggression / training drive
- AR-rich limbic/hypothalamic circuits (P1b-class); androgen-linked irritability/dominance behaviors in humans are documented in broader AAS psychiatry literature (**Probable** class; Halo frequently rated high among orals — **Probable** relative ranking as lore; unique severity **Speculative**).
- Dose-dependent; sleep disruption amplifies next-day volatility (**Probable**).
- Functional in the gym; **liability** socially and for decision quality (**Probable**).

### 6.4 Vascularity / contest appearance
- Low fluid retention (non-aromatizing) + hardness + possible BP/vascular tone changes → enhanced vascularity when lean (**Probable**).
- Peak-week role is appearance + gym aggression, not recomp (**Probable** community use).

### 6.5 Medical desired effects (context only)
- **Androgen replacement:** functional but inferior convenience/safety profile to modern T (**Probable** practice).
- **Breast cancer palliation (P17):** AR/hormonal pathway clinical benefit in selected ER+ advanced disease (**Established**). Historical “anti-estrogen” lore does **not** make Halo a modern AI/SERM for male gyno.

## 7. Side Effects — Mechanism + Pathway → Counter Tables

Every major side: mechanism chain first, then pathway-node → counter table.

### 7.1 Hepatotoxicity (17α-methyl + first-pass) — P4 / P4a–d

**Mechanism chain:** 17α-alkylation impairs first-pass clearance → sustained hepatic exposure (P4c) → canalicular bile-salt export interference → intrahepatic cholestasis (P4a) ± oxidative/redox amplification (P4b) → ALT/AST/GGT/bilirubin rise → clinical jaundice/pruritus/RUQ pain. Prolonged/high exposure associated with peliosis hepatis, hepatic adenoma, HCC (P4d) (**Established** class warning; exact molecular initiator for fluoxymesterone specifically **Unknown–Speculative**). Time course: enzymes can move within 1–3 weeks; structural disease is longer-horizon. Host factors: alcohol, other orals, viral hepatitis, NAFLD (**Speculative** individual risk modifiers).

| Dose load | Prefer **10–20 mg**; treat 30–40 as advanced/short | **Probable** | Nonlinear side risk perception in forums |

### 8.1 Assembled short peak-strength block (engineering map)

#### PRE (T−14 to T−7 before first Halo dose)
1. **Labs:** CBC (HCT), CMP/LFTs (ALT, AST, ALP, GGT, bilirubin), fasting lipids, BP log ×7 days; optional sensitive E2/total T if managing test base finely.
2. **Clearance check:** No active hepatitis symptoms; HCT not already high; BP controlled; no concurrent oral 17α.
3. **Test base already stable:** Prefer established TRT or cycle test (enanthate/cypionate weekly, or propionate if short-ester design) — **do not start Halo as monotherapy**.
4. **Support procurement:** TUDCA, NAC, EPA/DHA, ketoconazole shampoo if hair-prone, home BP cuff.
5. **Lifestyle gates:** alcohol zero planned; sleep plan; no high-stim preworkout during block; sodium awareness (P5).
6. **Licorice audit:** remove glycyrrhizin products.
7. **Social/behavioral brief:** aggression risk acknowledged; high-stakes interpersonal conflict postponed.

#### ON (example Days 1–14)

| Element | Example frame | Certainty / notes |
|---------|---------------|-------------------|
| Fluoxymesterone | **10–20 mg/day** oral, split 2× if desired (t½ ~9.2 h) | **Probable** effective PE window; 40 mg only for experienced, accepts more S1–S6 load |
| Test base | Continue TRT/cycle dose | **Probable** required |
| TUDCA | 250–500 mg BID | **Probable** adjunct — **false security if duration extended** |
| NAC | 600–1200 mg/day | **Speculative–Probable** ROS node |
| EPA/DHA | 2–4 g/day | **Probable** lipid adjunct |
| Cardio | 2–4× LISS/week even in peak | **Established** direction |
| Training | Heavy neural strength work; skill/technique; avoid reckless PRs if BP/sleep wrecked | Practical |

| Optional hair | Minoxidil cont.; keto shampoo | **Established**/ **Probable** |

**Not recommended on this block:** second oral, high-dose stim PWO, licorice, binge drinking, spironolactone “for BP” (antiandrogen conflict).

#### EXIT / PCT (Day 15 → recovery)
1. **Halo stop:** drug largely cleared in ~2–3 days (t½ 9.2 h); hepatic and lipid stress lag can persist days–weeks.
2. **Continue test base** if on TRT indefinitely, **or** complete planned injectable cycle length.
3. **PCT timing:** dictated by **longest ester** (e.g. test e/c → wait clearance window then SERM), **not** by Halo’s short half-life alone. If only short-ester test + 2-wk Halo: earlier SERM start possible — still individualize.
4. **Community SERM example frame (not medical prescription):** tamoxifen or clomiphene × several weeks post-ester clear; hCG if used is typically front-loaded before/during early PCT. Certainty: **Probable** community practice / limited formal AAS RCT.
5. **Post labs:** LFTs + lipids + CBC at 2–4 weeks off oral; hormones as indicated.
6. **Lipid recovery:** expect weeks; maintain fish oil + cardio.
7. **Liver:** continue TUDCA/NAC briefly into washout **optional**; stop once enzymes normalize.
8. **Do not** immediately chain another oral.

#### Protection stack hierarchy
- **Primary (non-negotiable):** short duration, moderate dose, test base, no alcohol, no second oral, BP monitoring, stop criteria.
- **Secondary (adjunct):** TUDCA + NAC + EPA/DHA + sleep/environment + sodium awareness + no licorice + no high stims.
- **Tertiary (conditional):** hair topicals, clinical antihypertensives if needed, phlebotomy if polycythemic, SERM/hCG per exit design.

**Do not sell** secondary agents as making Halo “safe.” They are **node-targeted damage control** around a high-androgen, high-hepatic, 11β-HSD2-active oral used only when strength peak justifies the risk package.

#### Conflicts / anti-goals
| Conflict | Why it matters |
|----------|----------------|
| **Spironolactone vs anabolic goals** | MR blockade is pathway-correct for P5 but AR antagonism undermines strength/androgenicity and can cause gyno. Prefer sodium control, stim removal, and non-antiandrogen antihypertensives under medical care. |
| **Finasteride vs “full androgenicity”** | May partially reduce 5α-dihydrofluoxymesterone formation; parental AR remains. Sexual sides possible. Not a free lunch for hair. |
| **Beta-blockers vs peak performance** | May calm rage/tremor but blunt readiness for max attempts. |
| **AI overuse on dry compound** | Halo doesn’t raise E2; crashing E2 from aggressive AI on low-aromatizing stacks harms joints/mood. Titrate to labs/symptoms if test base aromatizes. |
| **TUDCA false security** | Users extend orals because “liver is protected.” TUDCA is **secondary**; duration/dose/alcohol gates are **primary**. |
| **Halo as mass agent** | Misaligned expectations → dose escalation → sides without hypertrophy payoff. |
| **Solo Halo “mini-cycle”** | Deep T suppression (Jones) + liver + no replacement androgen = poor design. |
